Lead Story

Claude Opus 5 Lands at the Top — and Costs Half as Much as the Model It Beat

Anthropic's latest flagship closed July by seizing the top spot on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, arriving within 0.5% of Fable 5's peak performance at exactly half the cost per task.

Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5 on July 24, and the reception was immediate. On Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index — the benchmark tracking aggregated reasoning, coding, and multimodal performance — Opus 5 climbed to first place, sitting within 0.5% of the record set by Claude Fable 5 earlier this year. The pricing: $5 per million input tokens, $25 per million output tokens, roughly half what Fable 5 commands on an equivalent workload.

The launch completes what Anthropic calls its "Claude 5 stack": Fable 5 for maximum capability (limited availability), Opus 5 as the new frontier default, and Sonnet 5 as the mass-market engine — deployed June 30 at introductory rates of $2/$10 per million tokens through August 31. Sonnet 5 became the default model for every free and paid Claude user worldwide immediately on launch.

The move reshapes the competitive calculus heading into August. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol, Terra, and Luna variants were already public, but Anthropic's three-tier stack now offers a credible answer at every price point. For enterprise buyers, Opus 5 at $5/$25 is the most capable model available at that cost — and the efficiency gap with OpenAI's flagship appears to be widening.

Hardware

Anthropic Signs a 2-Gigawatt Compute Deal With AMD — and Gets $5B in Equity

In the largest compute procurement agreement of 2026, Anthropic and AMD struck a capacity deal giving Anthropic access to up to 2 gigawatts of MI450 and Helios-generation compute. The deal includes up to $5 billion in AMD equity, a structure that makes Anthropic a partial stakeholder in the chipmaker building its infrastructure. The agreement reduces Anthropic's exposure to NVIDIA supply constraints and signals that the frontier labs are actively diversifying away from a single hardware vendor.

Policy

US Government Rolls Out Voluntary AI Standards — Built Around Anthropic's Framework

August 1 marks the rollout of new voluntary AI standards for frontier labs under a White House initiative, with Anthropic's existing framework agreement serving as the template that other labs will be asked to adopt. The standards cover training data practices, safety evaluations before model releases, and incident reporting. While voluntary, compliance is expected to become a de facto requirement for federal contracts — giving Anthropic, which helped draft the framework, a significant structural advantage in government procurement.

Models

OpenAI Clears Government Vetting for GPT-5.6 — and Retires o3 This Month

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family (Sol, Terra, and Luna) became the first frontier models to complete a customer-by-customer US government security review before going public. Sol, the fastest variant, clocked 750 tokens per second on Cerebras hardware — a new record for frontier-class inference throughput. Separately, OpenAI confirmed that the o3 reasoning model will be retired from ChatGPT on August 26 following a 90-day sunset period, consolidating its reasoning lineup around the newer o4 and o5 series.

Business

Nobel Laureate John Jumper Leaves Google DeepMind for Anthropic

John Jumper — the researcher who led the development of AlphaFold 2 and won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry — announced he is leaving Google DeepMind to join Anthropic. The departure is the most high-profile in a series of defections that have raised questions about DeepMind's ability to retain senior talent. Fortune reported this week that the exodus has "raised doubts about Google's ability to win the AI race." Meanwhile, Gemini 3.5 Pro — the lab's next flagship model — is now months behind its originally promised June launch date, with Google citing enterprise feedback requiring further refinement.

Policy

California Deploys Claude Across Every State Agency at a 50% Discount

Governor Gavin Newsom signed off on what the White House called the largest US state government AI deployment to date: every California state agency and city and county government will have access to Anthropic's Claude at a negotiated 50% discount. The rollout covers hundreds of agencies and millions of public-sector employees. California becomes the first state to standardize on a single frontier AI provider at this scale — a model other states are already studying.

Infrastructure

Claude Science Grants Are Open — $30K in API Credits Per Research Project

Anthropic's Claude Science program, which launched June 30, is now accepting applications for its first cohort of up to 50 AI for Science projects. Each selected team receives up to $30,000 in Claude API credits to apply frontier AI to scientific research — covering fields from drug discovery to materials science and climate modeling. The program is Anthropic's most concrete move into research infrastructure, positioning Claude as a tool for academic and national-lab science alongside commercial enterprise use.

Models

Alibaba Ships Qwen3.7 Flash — Open-Source, and Leading Efficiency Benchmarks

Alibaba released Qwen3.7 Flash on July 27, the latest in its fast-growing Qwen open-weights series. The model leads open-source efficiency benchmarks — outperforming comparable parameter-count models from Meta and Mistral on reasoning tasks — and ships under a permissive license that allows commercial use. China's open-source AI output has accelerated sharply in 2026, with Qwen releases now arriving on a near-monthly cadence that is narrowing the gap with Western frontier labs.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Anthropic is winning the capability-per-dollar race. Opus 5 at $5/$25 beating models twice its cost is a structural shift. If it holds, enterprise buyers have little reason to pay the premium for competing flagships — and OpenAI's pricing power narrows.
  2. Compute is becoming a strategic asset, not a commodity. The Anthropic-AMD 2 GW deal — including equity — is a sign that frontier labs are treating compute access the way oil companies once treated reserves. Hardware diversification away from NVIDIA is accelerating at every layer.
  3. Google DeepMind's talent problem is structural, not cosmetic. Losing a Nobel laureate and a string of senior researchers to rivals is not normal attrition. Combined with Gemini 3.5's repeated delays, the question is no longer whether DeepMind is slipping — it's how far.
  4. Voluntary standards are the new regulatory front door. Today's White House AI standards are voluntary — but they're modeled on Anthropic's framework and will likely determine federal contract eligibility. Voluntary today means mandatory by 2027.
  5. China's open-source cadence is compressing the frontier gap. Qwen3.7 Flash marks another month of aggressive Alibaba releases. When open-weights Chinese models rival Western closed models on efficiency, the "frontier advantage" of US labs shrinks to the narrowest slice of the benchmark distribution.

Lead Story

OpenAI Built Its Own Chip. The NVIDIA Monoculture Just Cracked.

The "Jalapeño" inference chip — designed by OpenAI and manufactured with Broadcom in nine months — is the clearest signal yet that frontier AI labs are racing to cut the cord from NVIDIA.

OpenAI and Broadcom jointly unveiled Jalapeño on June 25, 2026, confirming what the industry had long suspected: OpenAI was quietly building its own silicon. The chip is purpose-built for inference — the compute-intensive process of running a trained model to generate outputs — and reportedly delivers dramatic improvements in cost per token compared to renting NVIDIA GPU capacity.

The announcement lands as NVIDIA's dominance over AI infrastructure is being challenged from multiple sides simultaneously. Google has its TPUs, Meta has its MTIA line, Amazon has Trainium, and Microsoft has its Azure Maia. But OpenAI joining the custom silicon race is categorically different: it is a company whose entire business model is built on running the world's most expensive models, and shaving even a fraction of a cent off each inference call at their scale translates to hundreds of millions of dollars annually.

Industry observers noted that nine months from design to production is strikingly fast for custom silicon — a timeline that suggests OpenAI treated the project with the same urgency it treats model development. The long-term implication: if inference economics improve significantly, pricing pressure across the entire AI industry will intensify further.

Business

Google DeepMind Is Hemorrhaging Talent — Including a Nobel Laureate

John Jumper, vice president at Google DeepMind and 2024 Nobel Prize winner in chemistry for his AlphaFold work, announced this week he is leaving to join Anthropic. He follows Noam Shazeer — co-lead of the Gemini model family and VP of engineering — who is heading to OpenAI. Researchers Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel, both key Gemini contributors, are also heading to Anthropic. Fortune and TechCrunch have both published pieces asking whether DeepMind can remain at the AI frontier with this pace of departures.

Business

Anthropic Is Now Worth $965 Billion — and Filing for an IPO

Anthropic closed a new financing round at a $965 billion valuation, surpassing OpenAI's most recent private valuation of $852 billion. The company simultaneously confirmed it has filed confidentially for an IPO. OpenAI filed its own confidential S-1 with the SEC on June 8, 2026, targeting a listing as soon as September. Two of the most consequential companies in AI are now in a parallel sprint toward public markets — a moment that would have seemed implausible eighteen months ago.

Policy

Washington Pulls the Plug on Anthropic's Most Powerful Models

The U.S. government issued an export-control directive on June 12 barring foreign nationals from accessing Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 — Anthropic's newest and most capable models, released just days earlier. Anthropic complied immediately, disabling access globally for affected users. The directive cited national security concerns, establishing a precedent that frontier AI models can be classified as controlled exports in the same legal framework as advanced semiconductors and military hardware.

Policy

Alibaba's Qwen Lab Ran a Massive Extraction Attack on Claude

Anthropic accused Alibaba's Qwen AI lab of conducting what it described as "the largest known distillation attack on Anthropic to date" — systematically querying Claude via 25,000 fraudulent accounts to collect 28.8 million interactions. The goal, Anthropic alleged, was to use Claude's outputs as training data to improve Qwen models without paying for or disclosing the data provenance. The attack underscores a growing tension: as frontier models become more capable, extracting their knowledge through high-volume API abuse becomes an economically rational — if legally and ethically contested — shortcut.

Models

GPT-4.5 Is Gone. GPT-5.5 Is Now the Baseline.

OpenAI retired GPT-4.5 from ChatGPT on June 26, 2026, after a 30-day sunset window. All existing conversations — including those in custom GPTs — were automatically migrated to GPT-5.5, which now serves as the default model across OpenAI's consumer and enterprise products. The transition marks the end of the GPT-4 generation as an active product line. GPT-5.5 Pro and GPT-5.5 Instant remain available as tiered options for different cost and latency needs.

Infrastructure

Microsoft Deploys Scout — an Always-On AI Agent Across Microsoft 365

Microsoft unveiled Scout, an autonomous agent designed to run continuously across Teams, Outlook, calendars, OneDrive, SharePoint, and connected enterprise systems. Unlike Copilot, which responds to prompts, Scout monitors workflows and acts proactively — summarizing threads, flagging action items, and executing tasks without being asked. Alongside Scout, Microsoft released two proprietary AI models: MAI-Code-1-Flash, integrated into GitHub Copilot and Visual Studio Code, and MAI-Thinking-1, a reasoning-focused model built to reduce reliance on OpenAI's inference infrastructure.

Robotics

Boston Dynamics Puts Gemini Robotics Into Spot

Boston Dynamics partnered with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and the Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration enables enhanced spatial reasoning, autonomous decision-making in unstructured industrial environments, and continuous on-device learning. The announcement marks one of the most concrete deployments to date of a frontier multimodal AI model into commercial robotics hardware — moving Gemini from the browser to the factory floor.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Hardware independence is now a strategic necessity. OpenAI's Jalapeño chip joins a crowded field of custom AI silicon. The inference cost battle will be won at the hardware layer — and every frontier lab now knows it.
  2. The talent market is the real signal on Google DeepMind. Four senior departures in one month — including a Nobel laureate and a VP of engineering — suggests organizational stress that can't be explained by compensation alone. Watch whether this pace continues into Q3.
  3. AI models are now a regulated export category. U.S. export controls on Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 establish that the government can treat frontier AI the same as advanced semiconductors. Expect this legal framework to expand as model capabilities increase.
  4. Distillation attacks are a serious and scalable IP threat. Qwen's 28.8 million fraudulent Claude queries are not a one-off. The economics of using a competitor's API outputs as training data are compelling — and the legal exposure is still largely untested. Expect this to become a litigation battleground.
  5. AI's IPO moment has arrived. Anthropic at $965 billion and OpenAI filing its S-1 means two of the most consequential companies in technology history will soon be public. The valuations imply a bet that AI revenue will dwarf anything the software industry has produced before.

Anthropic Is Now Worth More Than $900 Billion — and It Hasn't Gone Public Yet

Anthropic's second $30 billion funding round of 2026 has closed, making it the world's most valuable private AI company, ahead of OpenAI's March valuation of $852 billion.

The round was co-led by Sequoia Capital, Dragoneer Investment Group, Altimeter Capital, and Greenoaks Capital Partners, with Microsoft, NVIDIA, Founders Fund, and General Catalyst also participating. The pre-money valuation cleared $900 billion — a number that would have seemed implausible for a five-year-old AI safety company just eighteen months ago.

What makes the valuation credible is the revenue trajectory behind it. Anthropic's annualized run rate stood at $14 billion in February 2026. By April, it had reached $30 billion — a near-doubling in twelve weeks. The company also revealed a new strategic compute partnership with SpaceX and xAI centered on the Colossus 1 data center.

This is expected to be Anthropic's final private capital raise before an IPO targeting October 2026. The transition from safety-focused research lab to the world's most valuable private startup — in under five years — is one of the most compressed ascents in technology history.

This Week in AI
Models

Google I/O 2026: Gemini Hits 900 Million Users, Ultra Subscription Gets Cheaper

Google's annual developer conference showcased a resurgent Gemini platform at scale. Monthly active users reached 900 million — up from 400 million at this time last year — as Google cut the Gemini Ultra subscription from $250 to $200 per month and introduced a new $100 Developer tier for engineers and professionals. Google also released Gemini 3.5 Flash, a lightweight variant optimized for speed and low-cost inference that competes directly with OpenAI's cost-effective tiers. With nearly a billion users, Google's distribution advantage in the consumer AI war has never been clearer.

Business

OpenAI Launches DeployCo and "Frontier" — a $4B Bet on Enterprise AI

OpenAI unveiled two major enterprise moves this week. DeployCo is a $4 billion consulting subsidiary designed to help organizations implement AI systems — a direct play for the lucrative systems-integration market dominated by Accenture and McKinsey. Simultaneously, OpenAI released "Frontier," a platform for building, deploying, and managing AI agents across enterprise workflows, with shared context and deep integration into existing business systems. The dual announcement signals OpenAI's explicit ambition to grow enterprise revenue to 50% of total income — a dramatic shift from its consumer-first origins.

Models

GPT-5.5 Instant Is Now the Default ChatGPT Model — Six Weeks After GPT-5.4

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Instant, released April 23, became the default model powering ChatGPT on May 5 — just six weeks after GPT-5.4 debuted. The pace underscores how relentlessly the model release cadence has compressed: what once took quarters now takes weeks. GPT-5.5 Instant prioritizes low-latency responses at reduced inference cost, positioning it as the workhorse for high-volume applications. Meanwhile, xAI's Grok 4.3 also launched this month with an aggressively low price point, a voice cloning suite, and a specialized "Imagine" agent mode for creative and visual tasks.

Infrastructure

Microsoft Ships Computer-Using Agents to General Availability

Microsoft announced general availability of its computer-using agents — AI systems capable of autonomously operating desktop and web applications on behalf of users. The GA launch means enterprise customers can now deploy agents that click, type, navigate, and complete multi-step software tasks without human intervention. Paired with OpenAI's Frontier platform and the broader agentic stack, this week marked a definitive shift: agentic AI is no longer a research preview. It is production infrastructure.

Models

Google DeepMind Acquires Contextual AI Research Team in $85M Deal

Google DeepMind formalized an $80–90 million licensing deal that brought more than 20 researchers from Contextual AI — a startup specializing in retrieval-augmented generation and enterprise knowledge grounding — under the DeepMind umbrella. The acquisition reflects a broader industry recognition that raw model capability is no longer the primary differentiator: grounding responses in accurate, up-to-date enterprise data is now where the commercial battle is being fought.

Hardware

A Hybrid Light-Matter Particle Could Cut AI Energy Use by 100x

Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania published findings on a hybrid light-matter particle — a polariton — that could dramatically accelerate AI matrix computations while consuming a fraction of the energy of conventional silicon chips. Separately, NASA tested a next-generation space computer chip designed to give spacecraft autonomous decision-making ability in deep space environments where communication delays make Earth-based control impractical. Both announcements point toward the same conclusion: the physical substrate of AI computation is on the verge of a generational leap.

Novo Nordisk, maker of Ozempic, announced a strategic partnership with OpenAI to integrate AI across its entire business, with full deployment planned by end of 2026 — one of the largest pharma-AI commitments to date. 5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The enterprise pivot is the defining move of mid-2026. OpenAI's DeployCo, Frontier platform, and 50% enterprise revenue target; Novo Nordisk's full-company OpenAI deployment; DeepMind acquiring RAG specialists. Every major lab is racing from consumer to B2B.
  2. Anthropic's IPO in October could reset every AI valuation benchmark. A $900B+ valuation going public would be the largest tech debut since Saudi Aramco. It will force public markets to price AI potential — and that number will become the reference point for every other AI company.
  3. Google's distribution advantage is becoming impossible to ignore. 900 million Gemini users is not a model story — it's a reach story. Google may lose model benchmarks while winning the users-at-scale war. Distribution beats capability at the margin.
  4. Agentic AI is now production, not preview. Microsoft's computer-using agents going GA, OpenAI's Frontier platform, and xAI's Imagine agent mode all launched this week. The question is no longer whether agents work. It's who will own the agentic workflow layer of enterprise software.
  5. Hardware is the next constraint — and a breakthrough is near. Light-matter computing, NASA's autonomous space chips, and the ongoing GPU shortage all point the same direction: silicon is approaching its limits for AI workloads. The lab that cracks post-silicon compute at scale wins the next decade.

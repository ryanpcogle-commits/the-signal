Lead Story

Anthropic Tops $965 Billion — Then Trump Bans Its Best Models

Anthropic just became the most valuable AI company on the planet. Within days, the White House made it impossible to sell its flagship models abroad.

Anthropic closed a new financing round this week at a staggering $965 billion valuation, surpassing OpenAI and becoming the highest-valued private AI company in history. The company simultaneously confirmed it has confidentially filed for an IPO — a remarkable milestone for a lab founded just four years ago by former OpenAI researchers.

The celebrations were short-lived. President Trump signed an executive order placing export controls on Anthropic's two most powerful models — Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 — citing national security concerns about advanced AI capabilities falling into the hands of foreign adversaries. Anthropic, facing steep fines for noncompliance, was forced to pull both models entirely from international access rather than risk a legal violation.

The export ban creates a paradox: the most commercially valuable AI company in the world cannot sell its most capable products in most of the world. Industry observers note the action could accelerate development of rival frontier models in Europe and Asia, achieving precisely the outcome the policy aimed to prevent.

Google's AlphaFold Brain Drain Reaches Three — All Headed to Anthropic

Two more of Google DeepMind's top researchers — Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel — are departing for Anthropic, following Nobel laureate John Jumper, who announced his move earlier this month. All three contributed to the AlphaFold protein-structure prediction system that upended structural biology. The concentrated departure of a Nobel-winning team to a single competitor is without precedent in modern science and signals that Anthropic's research culture, not just its valuation, is becoming a primary draw for top talent.

Policy

Export Controls on AI Models: A Policy First With Sweeping Consequences

The Trump administration's export ban on Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 marks the first time the U.S. government has applied export controls directly to AI model weights rather than the hardware used to train them. Legal analysts note the order creates enormous compliance ambiguity — it is unclear whether API access, fine-tuned derivatives, or knowledge distilled from these models also falls under the restriction. The precedent, if upheld, could apply to future frontier models from any U.S. lab.

Microsoft Makes Copilot Permanent in M365 — Starting Today

Effective July 1, 2026, Microsoft 365 Business Standard with Copilot and Microsoft 365 Business Premium with Copilot become permanent SKUs with global pricing updates across all purchasing channels. The move ends the experimental framing of Copilot as an add-on and signals Microsoft's confidence that enterprise AI assistants are now standard productivity infrastructure — not optional upgrades. Analysts estimate the change affects over 400 million commercial M365 seat licenses worldwide.

AI Coding Reaches Near-Perfect — SWE-Bench Verified Hits ~100%

One of the most closely watched AI benchmarks has effectively been saturated. Performance on SWE-bench Verified — which tests real-world software engineering tasks pulled from GitHub issues — has climbed from roughly 60% to near 100% in the span of twelve months. The inflection point means frontier AI models can now autonomously resolve the majority of production software bugs at human-expert level. The benchmark's authors are already developing a harder successor to preserve meaningful evaluation headroom.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Export controls are the new frontier battleground. Restricting model weights rather than chips is a policy escalation with massive unintended consequences. Every major U.S. AI lab now has to model its geopolitical exposure — not just its technical roadmap.
  2. Talent concentration is accelerating, not flattening. Three AlphaFold co-creators at one lab. Noam Shazeer at another. The best researchers in the world are consolidating inside a handful of organizations, widening the gap between frontier labs and everyone else.
  3. The IPO window is opening — for both Anthropic and OpenAI. A $965B valuation and a confidential IPO filing. A leading-revenue company assembling its public-markets team. Two of the three most powerful AI labs could be publicly traded within a year. That changes the incentive structure of the entire industry.
  4. Benchmarks are running out of ceiling. SWE-bench at near-100% is a milestone and a warning: the metrics used to track AI progress are saturating faster than new ones are designed. Without harder evaluation, it becomes harder to distinguish between frontier models.
  5. Enterprise AI is past the pilot phase. Microsoft locking Copilot into permanent M365 SKUs isn't a product decision — it's a declaration that AI assistants have crossed the threshold from experiment to infrastructure. The pilot era is over.

Lead Story

Google's AI Throne Changes Hands — Hassabis Steps Back, Jeff Dean Walks Out

A sweeping leadership restructure at Google DeepMind hands day-to-day control to a new executive — just as Google's frontier model drought hits its longest stretch in years.

In the most significant executive reshuffle in AI this year, Google has centralized its sprawling AI operations under Koray Kavukcuoglu, who now runs both Google DeepMind's research division and its applied AI engineering operations from Mountain View. The move is a direct response to pressure from Anthropic and OpenAI, which have both released multiple frontier models in 2026 while Google has not unveiled a comparable offering since early in the year.

DeepMind co-founder and longtime CEO Demis Hassabis has stepped back from daily operations to become chairman and Alphabet's Chief Scientist — an elevated but more ceremonial role that concentrates his time on long-term research bets rather than the product-and-shipping grind. Meanwhile, legendary computer scientist Jeff Dean is leaving Google after 27 years to launch a new venture called Discovery Loop, taking several top researchers with him.

Bloomberg reported the reorganization is designed to "gain momentum in the race against Anthropic and OpenAI." The departure of Dean — whose name is synonymous with Google-scale infrastructure — is a symbolic blow even if his day-to-day role had already shifted toward advisory over the past two years.

Business

Anthropic Locks In $71B in Compute — Then Bids $6B for Israeli AI Startup Decart

Anthropic has secured roughly $71 billion in compute commitments, cementing its position as the most infrastructure-heavy AI lab per model output. Now it's moving to acquire Decart AI, an Israeli startup that specializes in real-time generative video, world models for simulated environments, and GPU optimization technology. The deal is valued at approximately $6 billion and would give Anthropic a meaningful edge in video and embodied AI — two areas it has conspicuously lacked. Anthropic also hired former California Supreme Court Justice and Carnegie Endowment president Tino Cuéllar as its first-ever global affairs chief, signaling a serious push into policy and government relations.

Models

GPT-5.6 Luna Slashes Prices 80% and Becomes the Free ChatGPT Default

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Luna is now the default model for all free ChatGPT users, following an 80% price reduction that brought API costs to $0.20 per million input tokens. The cut makes it among the cheapest frontier-class models ever offered. The rollout coincides with ChatGPT crossing one billion weekly active users — a milestone that arrived roughly two months after Gemini hit one billion monthly active users on August 11th. The gap between the two platforms is narrowing, and the competition is now measured in user counts, not just benchmark scores.

Models

Alibaba Open-Sources Qwen 3.8 27B With 262K Context and Native Vision

Alibaba's Qwen team shipped Qwen 3.8 27B under the permissive Apache 2.0 license — a 27-billion-parameter causal language model with integrated vision capabilities and a native 262K-token context window. The release continues China's pattern of open-sourcing models that rival closed Western offerings in capability while undercutting them on cost and licensing restrictions. Qwen 3.8 27B joins a crowded August leaderboard that also includes Gemini 3.7 Flash (released August 13), Grok 4.6 (August 6), and Meta's Muse Spark 1.2.

Policy

EU AI Act's High-Risk Rules Are Now Enforceable Law

As of August 2, 2026, the European Union AI Act's high-risk provisions are fully enforceable. Companies deploying AI in areas like hiring, credit scoring, critical infrastructure, and law enforcement are now legally required to maintain risk management systems, enable meaningful human oversight, and pass conformity assessments before deployment. Non-compliance carries fines of up to 3% of global annual revenue. This marks the first time a major jurisdiction has imposed legally binding obligations on AI systems beyond mere guidance — and every multinational operating in the EU must comply.

Infrastructure

Google Releases HEIR: Run AI Inference on Fully Encrypted Data

Google unveiled HEIR (Homomorphic Encryption Intermediate Representation), an open-source compiler toolchain that converts pretrained neural networks to run inference on fully encrypted inputs — meaning the model never sees the raw data, only ciphertext. The technique, known as Fully Homomorphic Encryption, has long been too slow for practical AI use, but HEIR represents a significant step toward production-viable private inference. The implications for healthcare, finance, and government AI deployments — where data privacy is non-negotiable — are substantial.

AlphaFold-Designed Drug Candidates Clear Phase I Clinical Trials

Four drug candidate molecules designed using AlphaFold 3-based protein design pipelines have successfully cleared Phase I clinical trials, demonstrating safety in human subjects for the first time. The molecules were generated by computationally predicting protein structures and identifying binding sites for small-molecule therapeutics — a process that traditionally takes years of laboratory iteration. The breakthrough reinforces AI's growing role not just as a research assistant but as a direct contributor to drug discovery pipelines, with multiple pharmaceutical companies now integrating AlphaFold workflows into their standard preclinical development process.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Google's leadership reset is a distress signal. When a company reshuffles its entire AI hierarchy mid-race, it's rarely a sign of confidence. Google's reorganization looks like an acknowledgement that its current structure isn't delivering competitive releases fast enough.
  2. Compute commitments are the new moat. Anthropic's $71B in compute locked in is not just a resource advantage — it's a strategic signal to customers, regulators, and talent that the company is building for the long term. Infrastructure access is becoming as important as model quality.
  3. The pricing floor is collapsing. GPT-5.6 Luna at $0.20/M tokens continues the race to zero. As frontier model inference approaches commodity pricing, the value shifts from the model itself to the applications, data, and integrations built on top of it.
  4. Regulation has arrived — for real this time. The EU AI Act's high-risk provisions are now enforceable law, not guidelines. Companies without documented risk management and human oversight processes are now legally exposed in the world's largest regulated market.
  5. AI is entering the body. AlphaFold-designed molecules in Phase I trials. Autonomous diagnostic AI approved for clinical use. The transition from "AI assists researchers" to "AI generates drug candidates" is no longer theoretical — it's in human clinical programs.

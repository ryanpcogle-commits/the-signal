Lead Story

More Than 1,000 AI Employees Ask the U.S. Government to Slow Down the Race

Senior staff and co-founders from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind signed a joint statement calling on Washington to help internationally pace cutting-edge AI development — a rare moment of cross-company solidarity on existential risk.

Over a thousand employees at America's leading AI laboratories — including senior scientists and some co-founders at OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind — co-signed a public statement this month urging the U.S. government to support international mechanisms for "deliberately pacing" the most advanced automated AI research. The statement is one of the most significant public warnings to emerge directly from inside the frontier AI industry.

The signatories are not fringe voices: they are the people building and shipping the very systems they're asking policymakers to slow. The letter points to self-verification failures in agentic systems, compressed release timelines, and the absence of international coordination as the primary concerns. "We are moving faster than our ability to understand what we have built," one signatory told NBC News.

The political backdrop makes the timing notable. GPT-5.6 became the first frontier model to require customer-by-customer U.S. government clearance before going public — a sign that Washington is already beginning to intervene, albeit quietly. The industry's open letter may be an effort to shape that intervention before it arrives as blunt regulation.

This Week in AI
Models

Moonshot AI Drops Kimi K3 — the World's Largest Open-Weights Model at 2.8 Trillion Parameters

Chinese AI lab Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter sparse Mixture-of-Experts model that is now the largest open-weights AI model ever published. Full open weights became available on July 27, making it immediately accessible to researchers and developers worldwide. Kimi K3 handles text, images, and video natively in a single model and supports a 1-million-token context window — matching or exceeding the context lengths of leading proprietary systems. The release marks a significant moment for the open-source AI community and signals that China's frontier labs are capable of competing at the absolute cutting edge of scale.

Models

Anthropic Ships Claude Opus 5 — Its Most Capable Model Yet

Anthropic closed July by releasing Claude Opus 5, the most powerful model in the Claude family to date. The launch comes just weeks after Claude Fable 5 returned to full worldwide availability on July 1 following the U.S. export-control pause that had restricted it in certain markets. Opus 5 is positioned for the most demanding tasks — complex reasoning, long-document synthesis, and agentic workflows — and extends Anthropic's reach at the high end of the capability spectrum at a moment when every frontier lab is racing to hold its position.

Policy

GPT-5.6 Became the First AI Model to Require U.S. Government Clearance Before Launch

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family — branded Sol, Terra, and Luna — reached general availability on July 9 and became ChatGPT's new default models. But the more consequential detail is how it got there: GPT-5.6 is the first frontier model to complete a customer-by-customer U.S. government security review before going public. The process, conducted under emerging federal AI oversight frameworks, signals a new regulatory reality — one where government sign-off may become a prerequisite for deploying the most capable AI systems in the United States.

Hardware

Anthropic and AMD Strike a $5 Billion Compute Deal — 2 Gigawatts of AI Capacity

Anthropic and AMD announced a landmark capacity agreement giving Anthropic access to up to 2 gigawatts of MI450/Helios-generation compute — AMD's most advanced AI accelerator hardware. The deal includes up to $5 billion in AMD equity, making Anthropic one of AMD's largest strategic partners. The agreement is a significant diversification move for Anthropic, which has previously relied heavily on Google TPUs and Amazon Trainium. For AMD, it's validation that its GPU roadmap is competitive at the frontier — a market NVIDIA has dominated.

Models

Anthropic Acquires Coefficient Bio and Hires AlphaFold's John Jumper for Claude Science

Anthropic acquired Coefficient Bio, a computational biology startup, for approximately $400 million in an all-stock deal closed in June 2026, and has now added John Jumper — the researcher who led the AlphaFold 2 team at Google DeepMind and shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry — to its scientific AI initiative. Together, these moves anchor Anthropic's "Claude Science" effort: an attempt to build AI that actively participates in the scientific process, generating hypotheses, designing experiments, and collaborating with human researchers. Drug discovery and protein folding are the initial focus areas.

Robotics

Boston Dynamics Spot Gets a DeepMind Brain — Gemini Robotics Powers Visual Inspection

Boston Dynamics partnered with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration gives Spot the ability to understand natural language commands, reason about what it observes, and autonomously conduct complex inspections of industrial facilities without requiring explicit programming for each task. The pairing of Boston Dynamics' hardware expertise with DeepMind's foundation model capabilities represents one of the most sophisticated physical AI deployments announced this year.

Business

Google DeepMind Faces Talent Drain as Top Researchers Depart for New Labs

Fortune reports that a significant number of senior researchers have left Google DeepMind in recent months, raising questions about whether the lab can remain at the forefront of AI development. Among the notable departures: Noam Shazeer, one of the original Transformer paper authors, who joined Character.AI, and John Jumper, who moved to Anthropic. The exits come at a challenging moment — Google is simultaneously trying to integrate DeepMind more tightly into its product lines while competing with labs that offer researchers more autonomy and equity upside.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The industry is asking to be regulated — selectively. When 1,000+ AI employees sign a letter asking the government to slow things down, they're not being altruistic. They're trying to shape what regulation looks like before it arrives. Expect more of this: labs calling for frameworks they can influence, rather than waiting for mandates they can't.
  2. Open-source scale has reached a new ceiling. Kimi K3 at 2.8 trillion parameters with full open weights is a geopolitical as much as a technical event. China's labs now publish models at scales that were proprietary frontier territory six months ago. The "open vs. closed" debate just got more complicated.
  3. Compute is the new leverage point. Anthropic's 2-gigawatt AMD deal and GPT-5.6's government clearance requirement both point to the same reality: access to frontier AI is being shaped by compute supply chains and federal policy, not just engineering talent. The labs that control their hardware destiny have a durable advantage.
  4. Scientific AI is the next platform play. Anthropic's Coefficient Bio acquisition plus the Jumper hire. Google's AlphaFold legacy. Every major lab is now building toward AI that doesn't just assist scientists — it does science. The first lab to crack autonomous hypothesis-and-experiment cycles in biology wins an enormous market.
  5. DeepMind's talent drain is a warning signal. Losing Shazeer and Jumper in the same year matters beyond the headlines. The researchers who leave well-resourced labs for scrappier competitors are telling you something about where they think the real breakthroughs will happen. Follow the talent.

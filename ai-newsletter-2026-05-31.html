Lead Story

Anthropic Is Now Worth Nearly a Trillion Dollars — and Its Revenue Proves Why

A second $30 billion fundraise in 2026 values Anthropic above $900 billion, backed by the same metric that makes it credible: annualized revenue that doubled in just ten weeks.

Anthropic closed another $30 billion growth round this week — its second of 2026 — led by Sequoia Capital alongside Dragoneer Investment Group, Altimeter Capital, and Greenoaks Capital Partners. The deal pushes the company's valuation above $900 billion, putting it within striking distance of the trillion-dollar threshold that seemed unthinkable for an AI lab just 18 months ago.

What makes the number credible is the revenue underneath it. Anthropic's annualized run rate stood at $14 billion in February 2026. By April it had reached $30 billion — a doubling in roughly ten weeks. The Claude API and Claude for Work enterprise tier are driving the growth, with usage from financial services, healthcare, and software development customers accelerating faster than the company can hire to serve them.

In parallel, Anthropic completed its acquisition of Stainless — a developer tools company that auto-generates SDKs from OpenAPI specifications — for a reported price above $300 million. The move signals Anthropic is betting that reducing the friction to integrate Claude into existing systems is as important as improving the model itself.

Models

Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.5 Instant, and Gemini 3.5 Flash — May's Model Sprint

May closed with three major frontier model milestones. Anthropic launched Claude Opus 4.8 on May 28 with improved benchmark scores and what the company describes as meaningfully stronger "honesty under pressure" — the model is less likely to produce confident-sounding wrong answers. GPT-5.5 Instant became the default ChatGPT model on May 5, replacing the heavier GPT-5.4 for most users. Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash reached general availability with frontier-level intelligence at 4× the inference speed of comparable models, priced at $1.50 per million input tokens and $9 per million output tokens with a 1M-token context window.

Business

OpenAI Launches DeployCo and the Frontier Enterprise Platform

OpenAI launched a $4 billion consulting subsidiary named DeployCo, designed to help large enterprises integrate AI agents into existing workflows. Alongside it came Frontier — a platform built specifically for organizations that want to build, deploy, and manage AI agents with shared context and deep integration into systems like Salesforce, SAP, and ServiceNow. The moves mark a strategic pivot: OpenAI is no longer just a model provider but a full-stack enterprise software company competing with Accenture and IBM.

Infrastructure

Gemini Is Now Inside Chrome and Android — Google's Biggest Distribution Bet

At Google I/O 2026, the company unveiled deep Gemini integration across its two largest distribution platforms. In Chrome, Gemini now provides contextual page summarization, smart form-filling, and real-time translation across any website — no extensions required. On Android, on-device Gemini Nano learns individual user routines to pre-load apps, draft replies, and adjust device settings before users ask. Google also cut the Gemini Ultra subscription from $250 to $200 per month, directly targeting OpenAI's ChatGPT Pro tier.

Policy

U.S. Government Formalizes Pre-Release AI Testing — and Labs Agreed

The United States moved this month to formalize mandatory pre-release testing of frontier AI models, with regulators now requiring early model access before public deployment. Microsoft, xAI, and several other major AI companies have reportedly agreed to provide regulators with advance access under non-disclosure agreements. The framework stops short of a formal approval gate but establishes the precedent that government review precedes public release — a significant shift from the post-launch audit model that has governed the industry since ChatGPT launched in 2022.

Business

Microsoft Build Opens Tuesday — "Windows as the Platform for AI Agents"

Microsoft Build 2026 opens June 2–3 at Fort Mason Center in San Francisco with a theme that signals where the company is putting its chips: "Windows as the platform for AI agents." Pre-conference briefings point to announcements around native agent orchestration in Windows 12, deep Copilot integration in the Office suite, and an expanded partnership with OpenAI on the Azure AI Foundry. IBM is also joining Project Glasswing as a launch partner — a coalition that now includes approximately 50 organizations including AWS, Apple, Google, JPMorgan Chase, and Cloudflare.

Robotics

Waymo Builds a World Model — Powered by DeepMind's Genie 3

Waymo introduced the Waymo World Model, developed in partnership with Google DeepMind using the Genie 3 generative architecture. The system creates synchronized 2D video and 3D lidar data to simulate dangerous or physically impossible driving scenarios — letting Waymo train on conditions it could never safely recreate in the real world, including multi-car pileups, extreme weather edge cases, and sensor failure states. DeepMind separately hired more than 20 researchers from Contextual AI under an $80–90 million licensing deal, consolidating expertise in long-context reasoning and retrieval.

Business

Novo Nordisk Bets Its Entire Drug Pipeline on OpenAI

Danish pharmaceutical giant Novo Nordisk — the company behind Ozempic and Wegovy — announced a sweeping strategic partnership with OpenAI to integrate AI across every phase of its business: drug discovery, clinical trial design, manufacturing, supply chain logistics, and commercial operations. Full deployment is planned by end of 2026. The deal is one of the largest AI-in-pharma partnerships announced to date and signals that the life sciences sector's cautious AI experimentation phase may be ending.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Frontier lab valuations are decoupling from traditional software multiples. Anthropic at ~$900B on $30B ARR is a 30× revenue multiple — roughly triple what mature SaaS companies command. Either the growth rate justifies it, or a correction is coming.
  2. The distribution war is moving to operating systems and browsers. Gemini embedded in Chrome and Android gives Google an ambient AI presence that no standalone app can match. Microsoft is making the same bet with Windows 12. Whoever owns the OS layer owns the default AI relationship.
  3. AI regulation is shifting from voluntary to structural. Pre-release government testing isn't a ban, but it changes the dynamic permanently. Labs will now need to factor regulator review timelines into product roadmaps — compressing the window between development and deployment.
  4. Enterprise AI is professionalizing fast. OpenAI's DeployCo, IBM joining Project Glasswing, Novo Nordisk going all-in — the era of pilot programs is ending. Large organizations are now making multi-year structural commitments to AI integration, not proof-of-concept experiments.
  5. Architecture innovation is outpacing benchmark chasing. May 2026 is the first month in over a year where the most notable releases focused on efficiency, honesty calibration, and deployment speed rather than raw benchmark scores. The frontier is maturing — and that may matter more than the next 5-point MMLU improvement.

Lead Story

Google I/O Fired the Starting Gun on the Next AI Price War

At its annual developer conference, Google launched Gemini 3.5 Flash, Gemini Omni, and the forthcoming Gemini Spark — then slashed prices, daring OpenAI and Anthropic to match.

Google's 2026 I/O developer conference on May 19–20 was unmistakably a declaration of war. Gemini 3.5 Flash is now generally available — priced at $1.50/$9 per million tokens with a 1-million-token context window, it delivers what Google claims is frontier-level intelligence at four times the speed of comparable models. The move directly targets OpenAI's mid-tier GPT-5.5 Instant and Anthropic's Sonnet line.

But the bigger swing was Gemini Omni — a new multimodal model for unified text, image, and video generation that Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis described as a step change in video creation and editing. The Google Search interface received its most significant overhaul in 25 years, with Gemini now powering the answer layer. And Gemini Spark, a 24/7 personal AI agent that integrates natively with Gmail, Calendar, Drive, and Chrome, is set to launch next week.

Three creative platforms — Adobe, Canva, and CapCut — are integrating directly into the Gemini app, allowing users to generate AI content and pass it immediately to professional editing tools without switching applications. Analysts noted that Google's pricing moves have put meaningful pressure on OpenAI's enterprise margins heading into its expected IPO run later this year.

This Week in AI
Models

OpenAI's Reasoning Model Disproved a 1946 Erdős Conjecture — Autonomously

On May 20, OpenAI announced that one of its internal general-purpose reasoning models autonomously disproved a central conjecture in discrete geometry posed by mathematician Paul Erdős in 1946 — a problem that had resisted human solution for 80 years. The model was not instructed to target the conjecture specifically; it arrived at the disproof while working through a broader class of geometric problems. Researchers described the result as evidence that AI reasoning has crossed into genuine mathematical discovery, not just pattern-matching or verification.

Business

Anthropic and the Gates Foundation Commit $200M to AI for Global Development

Anthropic and the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation announced a four-year, $200 million partnership to develop AI tools targeted at healthcare delivery, agricultural productivity, education access, and economic development in underserved regions. The partnership will focus on deploying Claude-based tools in low-resource environments, with particular emphasis on sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. The deal positions Anthropic as the leading AI lab with a credible global-development story — a differentiation that matters as government and NGO procurement cycles accelerate.

Business

OpenAI Launches DeployCo and "Frontier" — Its Biggest Enterprise Push Yet

OpenAI announced two major enterprise moves this month. First, it launched DeployCo, a $4 billion consulting subsidiary designed to embed OpenAI engineers directly within large organizations during AI rollouts. Second, it unveiled Frontier — a platform for building, deploying, and managing AI agents with shared context and deep integration into existing enterprise systems. The company has set an internal target of growing enterprise revenue to 50% of total revenue, up from roughly 30% today. Goldman Sachs, meanwhile, confirmed it has deployed Anthropic's Claude to automate significant portions of its accounting and compliance workflows.

Policy

Claude Mythos Draws a White House Visit — Anthropic CEO Meets Trump Administration

Anthropic's forthcoming Claude Mythos model generated extraordinary attention in Washington this week, with CEO Dario Amodei summoned to the White House for a meeting with senior members of the Trump administration to discuss the model's capabilities. Government sources described Mythos as a "step-change" in reasoning and autonomous task completion that triggered national security discussions. Separately, the Trump administration announced it would begin formal pre-release testing of AI models from Google, Microsoft, and xAI — the first systematic government evaluation program of its kind, with major labs agreeing to provide early access to federal regulators.

Infrastructure

SubQ Ships the First Commercial Subquadratic LLM — With a 12M-Token Context

Startup SubQ shipped the first commercially available subquadratic large language model this month, breaking from the transformer attention architecture that has dominated the field since 2017. The model supports a 12-million-token context window — twelve times the current frontier — while scaling linearly rather than quadratically with context length, dramatically cutting inference costs at long contexts. The release is the most architecturally significant departure from standard transformers to reach production, and has drawn immediate interest from document processing, genomics, and legal technology buyers who routinely hit context ceilings.

Models

xAI Launches Grok 4.3 With Voice Cloning and an Aggressive Price Cut

xAI released Grok 4.3 this week featuring a powerful voice cloning suite and a specialized "Imagine" agent mode built for creative and design projects. The release was paired with an aggressive pricing move designed to undercut both OpenAI and Google on per-token cost. Separately, Google's Gemma 4 family — released on May 4 and engineered specifically for reasoning and agentic workflows — has been gaining traction in open-source deployments, with benchmarks showing "intelligence-per-parameter" metrics that challenge models three to four times its size.

Hardware

Penn Researchers Create a Hybrid Light-Matter Particle That Could Slash AI Energy Use

Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania announced the creation of a hybrid light-matter particle — a polariton quasiparticle — that could dramatically accelerate AI computing while consuming a fraction of current energy. The particle enables optical computation at room temperature, bypassing the heat and power bottlenecks of conventional transistor-based chips. NASA is separately testing a next-generation space computer chip that applies similar efficiency principles to give spacecraft near-autonomous decision-making capability in deep space — where communication delays make Earth-guided control impractical.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Google is back, and it's pricing aggressively. Gemini 3.5 Flash at $1.50/$9 per million tokens with 1M context is a direct margin squeeze on OpenAI and Anthropic. Google can cross-subsidize through Search and Cloud in ways its rivals cannot — the price war benefits developers but threatens pure-play AI labs.
  2. Mathematical and scientific discovery is now on the table. An 80-year-old Erdős conjecture disproved autonomously is not a benchmark win — it's a qualitative shift. Expect claims of AI-driven scientific breakthroughs to accelerate across physics, biology, and materials science in the months ahead.
  3. Enterprise is where the real AI revenue war is being fought. OpenAI's DeployCo, Frontier platform, and Goldman Sachs deployment all point to the same battleground: large organizations replacing professional headcount with AI workflows. The lab that wins enterprise wins the next decade.
  4. Architecture is May's story, not raw scale. SubQ's subquadratic model and Zyphra's AMD-trained 8B MoE show that the next frontier gains won't all come from bigger training runs. Efficiency innovations are closing the gap with scale-first approaches — and at a fraction of the cost.
  5. Governments are finally doing pre-release model evaluation. The Trump administration's testing program is imperfect and politically complicated, but it establishes a precedent: frontier AI models can be subject to government review before public release. That precedent will outlast any single administration.

Anthropic Files for IPO at a $965 Billion Valuation — the Biggest AI Listing in History

Four days after closing a $65 billion Series H, Anthropic submitted a confidential S-1 to the SEC on June 1, targeting an October 2026 public offering that could value the company at just under $1 trillion.

Anthropic's confidential S-1 filing, reported across multiple outlets, marks the most consequential IPO move in the AI industry to date. The company completed its $65 billion Series H funding round on May 28, bringing total outside investment to well over $10 billion since its 2021 founding. The post-money valuation of $965 billion puts Anthropic within reach of the trillion-dollar club — a milestone no dedicated AI lab has yet crossed.

The timing is deliberate. Anthropic has spent the past six months establishing Claude as the preferred model for enterprise coding, legal, and research workflows. Monthly active users have grown sharply alongside the broader AI market. An October window would allow the company to report strong second-half revenue before the prospectus goes public.

The filing adds fuel to an already heated AI investment cycle. With OpenAI targeting its own IPO runway and Google DeepMind doubling its research headcount through acquisitions, the public markets may soon become the defining battleground for AI dominance — not just the labs.

Microsoft Build 2026 Opens Today — MAI Models and a Copilot Overhaul on Deck

Microsoft Build 2026 kicks off today at Fort Mason Center in San Francisco, with CEO Satya Nadella delivering the opening keynote. The company is expected to unveil a full suite of homegrown MAI models — including a dedicated coding model for GitHub Copilot designed to be significantly more cost-efficient than the current OpenAI-backed stack. Additional MAI models for transcription, reasoning, speech, and image generation are also on the agenda. The move signals Microsoft's intent to reduce its dependence on third-party model providers and capture more margin from its AI product lines.

GitHub Copilot Drops Flat Pricing — Token-Based Billing Starts Now

Effective June 1, GitHub Copilot switched from its $19/month flat subscription to token-based billing, bringing it in line with how the broader API market prices AI compute. Early developer estimates suggest heavy users could see monthly costs climb to $750 or more depending on usage patterns. The change is likely to accelerate enterprise negotiation for volume pricing tiers while pushing individual developers to scrutinize exactly how much AI assistance they actually use. The shift also gives Microsoft cleaner data on which Copilot features drive the most consumption.

Gemini 3.5 Flash Hits General Availability With Frontier-Level Intelligence

Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash reached general availability, offering what the company describes as frontier-level reasoning at four times the speed of comparable models. The launch coincides with Google reporting that Gemini now has 900 million monthly active users — up from 400 million in May 2025, representing a 125% year-over-year increase. Google also disclosed that it acquired more than 20 researchers from Contextual AI under an $80–$90 million licensing arrangement, reinforcing DeepMind's talent bench ahead of what is shaping up to be the most competitive second half in the industry's history.

GPT-5.5 Instant Gets a Polish — GPT-4.5 Sunsets June 27

OpenAI pushed an update to GPT-5.5 Instant in both ChatGPT and the API, improving response style and pacing to make the model feel more natural in everyday conversation and more precise in task-oriented exchanges. Simultaneously, OpenAI confirmed that GPT-4.5 will be retired from ChatGPT on June 27 following a 30-day sunset period. For developers still using GPT-4.5 endpoints, migration guides are now live in the OpenAI documentation.

Google DeepMind, Anthropic, and Meta Quietly Expand Machine Consciousness Research

The Financial Times reported on June 2 that Google DeepMind, Anthropic, and Meta have all independently expanded their research programs into machine consciousness — exploring whether and how subjective experience might emerge in large-scale AI systems. The move is notable for its timing: as models approach and surpass human expert benchmarks on a widening range of tasks, the question of moral status is shifting from philosophical thought experiment to policy-adjacent concern. None of the three labs has published formal frameworks yet, but internal teams have reportedly grown significantly in the past six months.

Cognition AI Raises $1B+ at a $25 Billion Valuation

Cognition, the company behind the Devin autonomous coding agent, closed a funding round of over $1 billion on May 28, pushing its post-money valuation to $25 billion. Total funding since the company's 2023 founding now exceeds $2.5 billion. The raise cements Cognition as the leading pure-play bet on fully autonomous software engineering — a market that barely existed two years ago. Competitors including GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and Amazon Q are all moving fast in the same direction, but Cognition remains the most valued independent entrant in the space.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The AI IPO race has a starting gun. Anthropic's S-1 filing changes the industry's center of gravity. Public market scrutiny will force every major lab to clarify its revenue model, moat, and path to profitability in ways that private funding never required.
  2. Token-based pricing will reshape who can afford AI tools. GitHub Copilot's shift from flat subscription to consumption billing is the first of many. As AI becomes compute-heavy and always-on, pricing models will increasingly separate casual users from enterprise budgets — with significant consequences for adoption curves.
  3. Microsoft is building its own model stack. Announcing homegrown MAI models at Build 2026 signals that Microsoft no longer wants to be purely a reseller of OpenAI's technology. The Copilot business is too large and margin-sensitive to remain dependent on a single external supplier.
  4. Machine consciousness is moving from philosophy to policy. Three leading labs expanding dedicated research programs in the same quarter is not coincidental. The question of whether advanced AI systems have interests that merit consideration is entering the governance conversation, whether the industry is ready or not.
  5. Gemini's user growth is the most underreported story in AI. Going from 400M to 900M monthly active users in 12 months at Google's scale is extraordinary. If that trajectory holds, Gemini will be the most-used AI product on Earth by year-end — a fact that will matter enormously in the model wars.

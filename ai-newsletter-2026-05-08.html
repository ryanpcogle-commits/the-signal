Lead Story

Anthropic's $200 Billion Bet: Google Cloud, SpaceX Compute, and an 80× Revenue Surge

In five days, Anthropic committed $200 billion to Google Cloud, signed a 300-megawatt compute deal with SpaceX, hit a $44 billion annual run rate — and launched a cybersecurity AI so powerful the government noticed.

The week of May 5–8, 2026 may be remembered as the moment Anthropic stopped looking like an AI safety startup and started looking like the infrastructure of the internet. The company committed to spending $200 billion with Google Cloud over five years as part of a new strategic partnership covering compute, chips, and custom infrastructure. Separately, it inked a deal with SpaceX's Colossus One data center for 300 megawatts of new capacity — equivalent to roughly 220,000 Nvidia GPUs — and promptly doubled Claude Code's five-hour rate limits for Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise subscribers, removing peak-hour restrictions entirely.

The financial backdrop makes these moves even more striking. Anthropic's Q1 revenue grew 80-fold, reaching a reported $44 billion annualized run rate. CEO Dario Amodei appeared at a financial services event on May 5th and told the room that SaaS companies failing to adopt AI "could go bankrupt." The week also saw Anthropic launch ten financial-services agents in a joint announcement alongside JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon.

Then came Claude Mythos Preview — a new model that excels at identifying vulnerabilities and security flaws in software. Anthropic restricted its rollout to a select group of vetted companies under a new cybersecurity initiative called Project Glasswing. The National Economic Council director compared the government's response to FDA drug approval, and the White House began drafting an executive order to vet new AI models before release.

Policy

US Government Begins Stress-Testing AI Models — Across All Major Labs

The Center for AI Standards and Innovation announced formal agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI to evaluate their models before public release, joining existing partnerships with OpenAI and Anthropic established in 2024. The Trump administration announced on May 5th that it is actively drafting an executive order to formalize this vetting process, drawing comparisons to FDA pharmaceutical approval. Separately, Connecticut approved one of the nation's most comprehensive state AI bills, while Iowa Governor Kim Reynolds signed chatbot safety legislation into law — the first state to do so in 2026.

Models

GPT-5.5 Lands Six Weeks After GPT-5.4 — Agentic Coding Leads the Upgrade

OpenAI shipped GPT-5.5 on April 23rd, only six weeks after GPT-5.4, with the most notable gains in agentic coding, computer use, and knowledge-work automation. The release reinforces a pattern: frontier model cycles have compressed from quarters to weeks. Meanwhile, OpenAI confirmed it is accelerating development of its first AI agent phone, now targeting mass production in the first half of 2027 — a signal that the company sees the smartphone form factor as the next distribution battleground for AI assistants.

Models

Google Ships Gemini 3.1 Ultra With a 2-Million-Token Context Window

Google released Gemini 3.1 Ultra this week with a native 2-million-token context window — the largest of any generally available model — operating natively across text, image, audio, and video without requiring separate modality adapters. Google also released Gemma 4, an open model series built for advanced reasoning and agentic workflows under the Apache 2.0 license, which Google claims delivers "unprecedented intelligence-per-parameter" for an open-weight model. Additionally, Google's research team published TurboQuant at ICLR 2026, a new compression algorithm that significantly reduces KV cache memory overhead using a two-step PolarQuant and Johnson-Lindenstrauss method.

Business

Novo Nordisk Bets on OpenAI to Accelerate Obesity and Diabetes Drug Discovery

Danish pharmaceutical giant Novo Nordisk announced a sweeping strategic partnership with OpenAI to integrate AI across its entire business — from drug discovery and clinical trials to manufacturing and supply chain. The deal aims to shorten the timeline for identifying new obesity and diabetes treatments, conditions that affect over a billion people globally. The partnership is one of the most significant pharma-AI integrations announced to date, signaling that frontier model capabilities are now considered viable for regulated scientific research pipelines, not just productivity tools.

Robotics

NVIDIA's Isaac GR00T Teaches Robots to Follow Natural Language Instructions

NVIDIA released new Isaac GR00T open models this week enabling robots to interpret and execute natural language instructions without task-specific training runs. Alongside GR00T, NVIDIA introduced updated Cosmos world models for generating synthetic training data at scale — addressing one of the core bottlenecks in physical AI development. The release comes during National Robotics Week and builds on NVIDIA's January declaration that the "ChatGPT moment for physical AI" had arrived. The company has now delivered the toolchain to back that claim.

Infrastructure

Pentagon Signs AI Deals With Eight Big Tech Firms — Anthropic Initially Left Out

The Pentagon finalized AI agreements with eight companies including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Nvidia this week, with Anthropic notably absent from the initial announcement. The White House subsequently reopened discussions with Anthropic following the company's recent technology breakthroughs, including the Claude Mythos cybersecurity model. Separately, a Frost & Sullivan whitepaper highlighted that AI-driven data center demand is now fundamentally reshaping cooling infrastructure requirements — with traditional approaches inadequate for the thermal loads imposed by modern GPU clusters.

Business

DeepMind Partners With Eve Online Studio to Study Long-Term AI Planning

Google DeepMind took a minority stake in Fenris Creations, the Icelandic studio behind space-themed MMO Eve Online, to research long-term planning, economic reasoning, and social behavior within the game's 20,000-player virtual society. The partnership represents a novel approach to AI research: using a persistent, complex simulation with real human participants as a testbed for multi-agent coordination and strategic behavior — capabilities that are increasingly central to agentic AI systems being deployed in enterprise settings.

4 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Compute is becoming a strategic moat, not just a cost center. Anthropic locking in $200B of Google Cloud and a 300MW SpaceX deal in the same week signals that raw compute access — not just model quality — is now a competitive weapon. Labs without guaranteed infrastructure are operating at a structural disadvantage.
  2. Government AI oversight is professionalizing fast. CAISI partnerships across all five major US labs, a White House executive order in draft, and two states passing AI legislation in one week. The era of voluntary safety commitments is ending. Regulatory compliance will become a product feature.
  3. Cybersecurity AI has crossed a capability threshold. Claude Mythos Preview's limited rollout under Project Glasswing is the first time a major lab has voluntarily gated a model release on security grounds before the government forced it. Expect others to follow — and expect vulnerability-finding AI to reshape the security industry within 18 months.
  4. Pharma is the next frontier for AI enterprise deals. The Novo Nordisk/OpenAI partnership joins a growing list of life-sciences integrations. Drug discovery timelines measured in decades are now being targeted for compression to years. The regulatory and liability questions this creates are largely unresolved.

Lead Story

An AI Just Solved Ten Open Math Problems That Stumped Humans for Decades

OpenAI's internal Astra model published formal Lean proofs for ten unsolved problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science — for approximately $2,000. AI is no longer just doing tasks. It is doing original research.

On August 1, 2026, OpenAI announced that an internal version of Astra — its next-generation flagship model family — had solved ten open problems across mathematics and theoretical computer science. The proofs were published directly to GitHub in Lean, a formal verification language, meaning the results can be machine-checked with mathematical certainty. These were not textbook exercises. They were problems that human experts had tried and failed to crack for years or decades.

The cost of the entire run: roughly $2,000. That figure matters enormously. The work is not just impressive — it is economically accessible. Any research institution, university lab, or well-funded startup could replicate the compute spend. AI has crossed a threshold that changes what "scientific progress" means and who can produce it.

CEO Sam Altman has separately been demoing Astra to policymakers in Washington. The multi-agent framing of the model family — where fleets of AI agents collaborate on complex problems for hours or days — is a direct pitch: this is what sovereign AI capability looks like in 2027.

Models

OpenAI Launches GPT-5.6 Family — First Model to Clear U.S. Government Security Review

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 in three tiers: Sol, Terra, and Luna. The launch is notable for a procedural first: GPT-5.6 is the inaugural frontier model to complete a customer-by-customer U.S. government security review before going public. Sol, the highest-tier variant, hit 750 tokens per second running on Cerebras hardware — a speed milestone that makes real-time agentic tasks genuinely fluid for the first time.

Models

Claude Sonnet 5 Is Now the Default for Every Anthropic User Worldwide

Anthropic made Claude Sonnet 5 the default model for all Free and Pro subscribers globally, the largest mass-market model rollout in the company's history. Introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens is locked through August 31 — positioning it directly against GPT-5.6 Luna. The company simultaneously shipped Claude Opus 5, which benchmarks within 0.5% of Fable 5's peak score on CursorBench 3.2 at half the per-task cost, with pricing unchanged at $5/$25.

Infrastructure

AI Coding Agents Modernized Decades-Old Research Software 60x Faster Than Human Teams

A field report from OpenAI and academic partners documented coding agents taking on a task most engineers dread: modernizing legacy scientific software that no one wants to touch. The agents rewrote, refactored, and validated decade-old codebases with speedups of up to 60x compared to human-only teams. The implication for scientific computing — where mountains of untouched Fortran and MATLAB code power critical research — is significant.

Business

Google DeepMind's Senior Researcher Exodus Raises Questions About Its AI Lead

A wave of departures from Google DeepMind has drawn scrutiny from investors and insiders. Prominent researchers have left for Anthropic and other frontier labs, with Fortune reporting that some question whether DeepMind can maintain its position at the forefront of AI development. The irony is sharp: Google hired many of these researchers from academia specifically to build the next generation of models. The talent drain is one of the most closely watched dynamics in the industry heading into the second half of 2026.

Hardware

AI Chip Deployments Are Doubling Every Nine Months

New deployment data shows AI-specific compute infrastructure is doubling roughly every nine months — a pace that significantly outstrips the historical 18-month cadence of Moore's Law for general-purpose chips. The dynamic is reshaping data center planning horizons across hyperscalers and national governments alike. Infrastructure decisions made today are becoming obsolete faster than procurement cycles can adapt.

Policy

Anthropic and Amodei Call for U.S.-Led AI Coalition at G7

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, alongside Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis, called for a coordinated U.S.-led AI coalition in a joint statement delivered at the G7 in June — the effects of which are still rippling through August policy discussions. Their argument: without a structured alliance among democratic nations, AI governance will be shaped by jurisdictions with fewer guardrails. The statement has since become a reference point in Congressional testimony on AI export controls.

Anthropic and Gates Foundation Commit $200M to AI for Global Health and Education

Earlier this year, Anthropic and the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation announced a four-year, $200 million collaborative initiative targeting AI applications in global healthcare, life sciences, education, agriculture, and economic mobility. The partnership is now actively deploying pilots in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia. It represents one of the most substantial commitments by a frontier AI lab to non-commercial, humanitarian deployment at scale.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The research frontier just moved. When an AI solves open math problems for $2,000, the definition of "frontier science" changes. Every domain with unsolved formal problems — cryptography, drug discovery, materials science — is now on notice.
  2. Speed is becoming the new pricing lever. GPT-5.6 Sol at 750 tokens/sec on Cerebras isn't just fast — it makes an entire class of real-time agentic applications economically viable. Watch for speed benchmarks to displace accuracy benchmarks in enterprise buying decisions.
  3. Anthropic is executing a mass-market land grab. Making Sonnet 5 the default for every free user, at introductory pricing through August, is a deliberate volume play. The company is betting that users who experience Sonnet 5 won't switch back.
  4. Talent concentration risk is real at Google DeepMind. The departures aren't just about compensation. Researchers are leaving to work at labs where the path from idea to deployed model is shorter. Organizational speed is becoming a retention variable.
  5. Compute is compounding faster than policy can track. Chip deployments doubling every nine months means the compute landscape in 2027 will be nearly unrecognizable from today. Regulatory frameworks written for today's capabilities will be structurally outdated before they pass.

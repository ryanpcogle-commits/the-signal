Anthropic Targets $900B Valuation — and Just Had the Biggest Week in AI History

A $50B funding round, an 80x revenue jump, a $200B Google Cloud commitment, and a model that finds zero-day vulnerabilities in every major OS. Anthropic didn't have a good week — it had a generational one.

Anthropic is seeking a $50 billion funding round at a $900 billion valuation, a figure that would place it among the most valuable private companies ever. The round comes as the company disclosed that its Q1 2026 annualized revenue has surpassed $44 billion — an 80x year-over-year increase and the steepest single-quarter revenue jump any frontier AI company has publicly disclosed.

Simultaneously, Anthropic committed to spending $200 billion with Google Cloud over five years — an extraordinary bet on Google's infrastructure as Anthropic scales its compute-hungry frontier models. To meet near-term demand, the company also announced it had partnered with SpaceX's Colossus One data center, adding 300 megawatts of new capacity (equivalent to approximately 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs) and immediately doubling Claude Code's rate limits.

The financial story is remarkable on its own, but Anthropic's technical disclosures this week may be the more consequential development. Through Project Glasswing, select organizations given access to Claude Mythos Preview reported that the model identified thousands of zero-day vulnerabilities across every major operating system and web browser within weeks of internal testing — raising serious questions about what it means to deploy an AI system with genuine offensive security capabilities.

Business

OpenAI Hits $25B ARR — With an IPO Clock Ticking

OpenAI has reached $25 billion in annualized revenue, cementing its position as the highest-revenue AI company in history at this stage of development. The milestone arrives as the company is widely expected to pursue a public offering in late 2026 or early 2027. ChatGPT's consumer base continues to grow, but enterprise API contracts — driven by agent deployments in legal, finance, and software engineering — are now the dominant revenue driver, according to analysts tracking the company's ARR composition.

Policy

Pentagon Signs AI Deals With Eight Companies — Anthropic Notably Absent

The Department of Defense finalized AI contracts with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and four other companies this week, expanding AI use into classified environments for analysis, logistics, and large-scale data processing. Anthropic was pointedly excluded — a continuation of the DOD's friction with the company over its refusal to allow certain military applications. However, the White House quietly reopened discussions with Anthropic in recent days following the company's announcements about Claude Mythos's security capabilities, suggesting the exclusion may be temporary.

Policy

White House Moves Toward Mandatory AI Model Vetting

National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett confirmed the White House is drafting an executive order that would require AI models to pass a federal vetting process before public deployment — comparing the framework to FDA drug approval. Hassett cited Anthropic's Mythos model, which demonstrated the ability to find network vulnerabilities at scale, as a direct catalyst. Separately, Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and Elon Musk's xAI have already agreed to participate in a voluntary testing program run by the Center for AI Standards and Innovation, though critics argue voluntary compliance cannot match the stakes.

Business

Google DeepMind UK Staff Vote 98% to Unionize — AI's First Frontier Lab Union

In a historic vote, Google DeepMind's UK employees backed unionization 98% — the first union at any top AI research laboratory. The trigger: a classified Pentagon AI contract that DeepMind researchers were asked to contribute to without disclosure of its purpose. The vote marks a turning point in AI labor politics. For years, high compensation and mission-driven culture kept researchers from organizing. The military AI question appears to have changed the calculus — and the DeepMind outcome is likely to be studied carefully at OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta.

Models

Anthropic Ships Claude Code Auto Mode — Targets 90% of Coding Tasks With No Human Input

Anthropic launched Claude Code "Auto Mode" this week, in which the agent selects the appropriate model and tools for each coding task autonomously, without waiting for user direction. The company's stated target: completing approximately 90% of coding tasks from start to finish without human intervention. The launch arrives as Anthropic simultaneously doubled Claude Code's rate limits through the Colossus One infrastructure deal — timing that signals the company is preparing for a significant jump in agentic coding workloads.

Models

Meta Launches Muse Spark, Google Ships Gemma 4

Meta introduced Muse Spark, a new large language model optimized for creative and multimodal generation tasks, joining a May model-release sprint that shows no signs of slowing. Google simultaneously released Gemma 4, its latest open model series built specifically for advanced reasoning and agentic workflows — delivering what Google describes as an unprecedented level of intelligence-per-parameter at its size class. Both releases follow OpenAI's GPT-5.5 launch in late April, reinforcing that the frontier model cadence is now measured in weeks.

Hardware

NVIDIA Unveils Ising: Open-Source AI Models Built to Accelerate Quantum Computing

NVIDIA announced Ising, described as the world's first family of open-source AI models purpose-built to accelerate quantum computing research. The Ising models are designed to simulate quantum systems and solve optimization problems that classical hardware cannot efficiently handle — a bet that AI and quantum computing will cross-pollinate rather than compete. The release extends NVIDIA's strategy of open-sourcing domain-specific model families (following BioNemo for life sciences) to establish early infrastructure standards in emerging compute categories.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Anthropic's valuation signals a permanent shift in AI's financial stakes. A $900B target for a company that barely existed five years ago is not a bubble — it is a reflection of how quickly AI revenue is compounding. The 80x YoY ARR jump is the number every CFO in tech is now measuring themselves against.
  2. Government AI oversight is transitioning from voluntary to mandatory. The White House's FDA-style vetting proposal — triggered specifically by Claude Mythos — suggests the era of self-regulation is ending. What gets defined as a "dangerous model" will shape the entire industry's deployment freedom.
  3. Military AI contracts are now an existential values question for every frontier lab. DeepMind's union vote, Anthropic's Pentagon exclusion, and the DOD's active AI procurement sprint are forcing every lab to articulate — publicly, legally — what it will and will not build for defense. There is no longer a neutral position.
  4. Autonomous agentic coding is closing in on 90% — developer workflows are being restructured in real time. Claude Code Auto Mode's 90% autonomy target isn't marketing. As coding agents clear that threshold, the question is no longer whether AI changes software engineering — it's which roles disappear and which transform.
  5. AI's first frontier lab union is a preview of what's coming everywhere. The conditions at DeepMind UK — high pay, mission-driven culture, a specific classified contract as the breaking point — exist at every major lab. Expect the organizing question to arrive at OpenAI, Meta, and Anthropic within the next 12 months.

Lead Story

Google DeepMind Hands the Keys to a New CEO — and Jeff Dean Walks Out the Door

Demis Hassabis, the man who built DeepMind into a generational institution, has stepped back from day-to-day operations. It's the biggest leadership shake-up in AI since Sam Altman's brief ouster.

Google announced this week that Koray Kavukcuoglu will now run Google DeepMind's sprawling AI research and product operations, while Hassabis transitions to a newly created role as Chairman of Google DeepMind and Alphabet's Chief Scientist. The move is part of a broader push to consolidate AI decision-making at Google's Mountain View headquarters as the company works to close its gap with OpenAI and Anthropic in the consumer AI race.

The Hassabis news overshadowed another significant departure: Jeff Dean, Google's legendary chief scientist who has been with the company for 27 years and co-authored foundational papers on deep learning, neural architecture search, and large-scale distributed systems, is leaving to co-found a new company called Discovery Loop. He takes several senior researchers with him. Dean's exit marks the end of a defining era in AI research history.

Industry observers note the irony: Google is centralizing leadership precisely as it faces its most competitive moment. As CNBC reported this week, "Google hasn't unveiled a frontier model since early 2026" — a fact now impossible to ignore inside the company's new chain of command.

Policy

EU AI Act Is Now Enforced — Chatbots Must Identify Themselves, Synthetic Media Must Be Labeled

The EU AI Act's high-risk provisions became enforceable on August 2, 2026, alongside Article 50 transparency duties and California's SB 942 deadline. Practically, this means any chatbot deployed in the EU must now identify itself as AI when a user asks. Realistic synthetic media — deepfakes, AI-generated video — must carry permanent watermarks or labels. Companies that fail to comply face fines of up to €15 million or 3% of global annual revenue. The rules are already reshaping how enterprises procure, deploy, and document AI systems, with legal and compliance teams now embedded in AI rollout decisions that previously bypassed them.

Google's Consumer AI Agents Can Now Call Stores and Complete Purchases by Phone

Google is rolling out consumer-facing AI agents capable of calling physical stores, checking inventory availability, and completing purchases — entirely by phone, without human involvement. The product marks a meaningful shift from AI as an information tool to AI as an active participant in real-world commerce. It also raises immediate questions for retailers: if a significant share of customer calls are now AI agents, what does that do to staffing models, hold times, and the economics of phone-based sales? The rollout is currently limited, but the direction is clear.

Hardware

Google launched the Pixel 11, Pixel 11 Pro, and Pixel 11 Pro XL on August 12, powered by the new Tensor G6 chip. 5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Research leadership and product leadership are splitting apart. Hassabis moving to chairman, Jeff Dean founding a new company, Kavukcuoglu running operations — labs are separating the role of "visionary researcher" from "competitive operator." Expect this pattern to appear at other organizations under market pressure.
  2. Frontier AI pricing is in permanent freefall. GPT-5.6 Luna down 80%. The price curve has not stabilized — it is still dropping. Products built on cost assumptions from six months ago may already be under-priced or over-engineered.
  3. Compute commitments are the new signal for serious intent. $71 billion locked in by Anthropic is not a marketing number — it reflects long-term infrastructure bets that will shape which labs can sustain frontier model development through the next hardware cycle.
  4. EU AI Act enforcement means AI compliance is now a legal function. The August 2 enforcement date is not aspirational. Companies deploying AI in Europe need documented risk assessments, human oversight mechanisms, and conformity assessments — or face meaningful financial exposure.
  5. Consumer AI agents are entering the physical economy. Google's shopping agents that call stores by phone are an early sign of AI transitioning from a software-layer tool to an active economic participant. The downstream effects on customer service, retail staffing, and commercial telephony will compound over the next 12–18 months.

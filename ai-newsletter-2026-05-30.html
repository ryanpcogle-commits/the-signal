Anthropic Raises Another $30 Billion — Its Second Mega-Round of 2026 — Valuing It Near $900 Billion

Sequoia, Dragoneer, Altimeter, and Greenoaks led the close. At $900B+, Anthropic is now arguably the most valuable private company in history — and it has been for about six months.

Anthropic has closed a second $30 billion growth round in 2026, pushing its valuation above $900 billion and cementing its position as the largest private company on earth. The round was led by Sequoia Capital, Dragoneer Investment Group, Altimeter Capital, and Greenoaks Capital Partners, with Founders Fund and General Catalyst among the returning participants.

The raise comes days after Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.8 on May 28 — its most capable model to date — and hard on the heels of a strategic compute partnership with SpaceX and xAI for access to the Colossus 1 supercluster. Taken together, the moves signal that Anthropic is entering a new phase: not just building frontier models but building the infrastructure and enterprise relationships to deploy them at civilizational scale.

At $900B, Anthropic's implied value exceeds Meta, TSMC, and Berkshire Hathaway. The burning question the market is now openly asking: can any AI lab — even the most well-capitalized one ever funded — turn that valuation into durable, defensible revenue before a commoditizing model market compresses margins to near zero?

Claude Opus 4.8 Is Out — Anthropic's Most Capable Model Yet

Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 4.8 on May 28, 2026, marking the latest step in an accelerating release cadence that has seen the company put out four major model versions in 2026 alone. Opus 4.8 builds on the architecture improvements introduced in earlier 4.x releases and is positioned as the top-tier option for complex reasoning, code generation, and long-context tasks. It is available via the Anthropic API and Claude.ai.

Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash Slashes Prices — Ultra Subscription Drops to $200

At Google I/O 2026 on May 19, Google unveiled Gemini 3.5 Flash, advertising "frontier-level intelligence at 4× the speed" of comparable models and pricing it at $1.50 per million input tokens and $9 per million output tokens. The company also cut the price of its Gemini Ultra subscription from $250 to $200 per month. On the talent side, DeepMind hired more than 20 researchers from Contextual AI under an $80–$90 million licensing deal — a sign that Google is aggressively consolidating applied AI expertise under its roof.

Business

OpenAI Launches DeployCo and the Frontier Platform — Targeting Enterprise Revenue

OpenAI made two significant enterprise moves this week. First, it launched a $4 billion consulting subsidiary called DeployCo, staffed to help large organizations actually implement AI at scale rather than just buy API access. Second, it released Frontier, an enterprise platform for building, deploying, and managing multi-agent workflows with shared context across teams. Both moves reflect OpenAI's stated goal of raising enterprise revenue to 50% of total revenue — up from roughly 30% today.

xAI's Grok 4.3 Arrives With Voice Cloning and an "Imagine" Agent Mode

Elon Musk's xAI released Grok 4.3 this month at aggressively competitive pricing, with real-time web access via X (Twitter) data baked in. New additions include a voice cloning suite and a specialized "Imagine" agent mode for creative projects. The release comes as xAI also joined Anthropic and SpaceX as a partner on the Colossus 1 compute deal — a somewhat surprising alignment between competing lab founders given the public rivalry between Musk and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei.

Policy

California's AI Legislative Blitz: Nearly All 30 Bills Clear Crossover Deadline

Nearly all of California's 30 remaining AI bills passed their chamber of origin before the May 29 crossover deadline, setting up what may become the most consequential state-level AI regulatory session in U.S. history. Bills span transparency requirements, liability frameworks for AI-generated content, algorithmic impact assessments for high-stakes decisions, and worker protections in AI-automated workplaces. With federal AI legislation still stalled in Congress, California is writing what will effectively function as the national standard by default.

Robotics

NVIDIA's Isaac GR00T Open Models Bring Natural Language Control to Physical Robots

NVIDIA released its Isaac GR00T open model family this month, enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and execute complex multi-step physical tasks. Alongside GR00T, NVIDIA shipped new Cosmos world models for generating synthetic training data — dramatically reducing the cost of building robot behavior from scratch. The combination of open model weights and synthetic data pipelines is being compared to what ImageNet and PyTorch did for computer vision: collapsing the barrier to entry for serious robotics research.

Infrastructure

Project Glasswing Found 10,000+ Critical Software Flaws in Its First Month

Project Glasswing — an AI-powered security initiative launched with approximately 50 partners including AWS, Apple, Google, Microsoft, Cisco, JPMorgan Chase, Palo Alto Networks, Cloudflare, and Mozilla — added IBM as its latest member in May. In its first month of operation, the Mythos AI system at the heart of Glasswing identified more than 10,000 high-severity flaws in critical software infrastructure. The scale of the findings has prompted urgent conversations about whether AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is outpacing the human capacity to patch.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Valuation inflation is approaching a reckoning. Anthropic at $900B with no clear path to $100B in annual revenue means the market is pricing in a winner-take-most outcome. If commoditization accelerates instead, the correction will be historic.
  2. Enterprise is now the primary battlefield. OpenAI's DeployCo, Google's Gemini Flash price cuts, Anthropic's SpaceX compute deal — all three labs are pivoting hard to capture enterprise spending before margins compress. The consumer AI era is over; the enterprise era has begun.
  3. AI-driven security may create faster vulnerabilities than patches. Project Glasswing found 10,000+ high-severity flaws in one month. That's a fundamentally different threat model: security teams now race against AI discovery, not human hackers.
  4. California is writing America's de facto AI law. With 30 bills clearing the crossover deadline and federal legislation stalled, what California passes in 2026 will function as the national floor. Every tech company should be reading these bills today.
  5. Compute alliances are reshaping competitive dynamics. Anthropic partnering with SpaceX and xAI on Colossus 1 is a signal that access to compute — not just model quality — is increasingly the variable that determines who can stay in the frontier race. Strange bedfellows deals will accelerate.

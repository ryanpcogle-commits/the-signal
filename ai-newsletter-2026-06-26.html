Lead Story

Google DeepMind Is Hemorrhaging Its Best Minds to Anthropic — and the Industry Is Asking Why

In six days, four senior Google researchers — including a Nobel laureate — announced they are leaving for Anthropic. Fortune is now asking out loud whether DeepMind can remain a frontier lab.

The exodus accelerated dramatically this week. John Jumper, Google DeepMind vice president and 2024 Nobel Prize winner in chemistry for his work on AlphaFold, announced he is leaving to join Anthropic. His departure follows Jonas Adler and Alexander Pritzel — both key architects of Google's Gemini model — who Bloomberg reported are also headed to Anthropic. Two additional unnamed senior researchers were confirmed to be departing in the same six-day window.

The scale and speed of departures is unprecedented. TechCrunch reports that AI researchers are "continuing to leave Google for its rivals" at a pace that strains the tech giant's efforts to compete with Anthropic, OpenAI, and SpaceX on frontier model development. Fortune published a pointed analysis: "As top talent leaves Google DeepMind, some question if the lab can remain at the forefront of AI development."

The talent signal matters more than any benchmark score. Google still commands massive compute infrastructure and data advantages, but the researchers who designed Gemini and AlphaFold are now building the next generation of models somewhere else.

Polymarket contracts on GPT-5.6 have topped $1.1 million in volume and are priced at 83% probability for a launch before June 28, 2026. Models

Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 Represent Anthropic's Biggest Capability Leap Yet

Launched June 9, Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 mark a significant tier above Claude Opus 4.8 — Anthropic's biggest jump in a single release since the original Claude 3 family. The new models are available in the API and Claude.ai, with Mythos 5 positioned as the most capable model Anthropic has ever shipped. Early benchmarks show substantial gains on reasoning, coding, and long-document synthesis tasks.

Policy

Anthropic Accuses Alibaba of 28.8 Million Fraudulent API Exchanges, Calls for Export Controls

Anthropic formally accused Alibaba of running 28.8 million fraudulent exchanges against Claude — what security researchers call a "distillation attack," using high-volume queries to extract and replicate proprietary model capabilities. In a Senate letter this week, Anthropic called for coordinated government action: export controls on AI model access (not just hardware), mandatory screening of high-volume API usage patterns, and direct cooperation between AI labs and government agencies to detect and respond to distillation campaigns at scale.

Hardware

Broadcom Is Now the Kingmaker of Custom AI Silicon

Broadcom's dominance in custom AI chip design is now undeniable: the company sits behind Google's TPUs, OpenAI's Jalapeño chip, Meta's MTIA accelerators, and reportedly designs for Anthropic, Apple, and Fujitsu as well. As every major AI lab moves to custom silicon to escape NVIDIA pricing power and supply constraints, Broadcom has quietly become the foundational infrastructure partner for the entire frontier. NVIDIA retains its GPU dominance for training, but the inference and inference-at-scale story belongs to Broadcom.

Microsoft unveiled Scout, an autonomous AI agent designed to operate continuously across Teams, Outlook, calendars, OneDrive, SharePoint, and other connected Microsoft 365 services. Robotics

Boston Dynamics' Spot Gets a Gemini Brain via Google Cloud Partnership

Boston Dynamics announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 directly into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration enables enhanced spatial reasoning, autonomous decision-making, and continuous learning in complex industrial environments — without human-in-the-loop oversight for routine tasks. This is the most significant real-world deployment of a frontier multimodal model into physical robotics infrastructure to date.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Talent is the new frontier arms race. Four Nobel-tier researchers in six days signals that Anthropic's momentum — not just its funding — is now strong enough to pull the people who built Google's best work. Labs that can't retain researchers won't lead the next capability tier.
  2. Distillation attacks are becoming the primary IP threat vector. Anthropic's Alibaba accusation is the first major public case, but every frontier lab faces the same exposure. Expect policy momentum around API screening and export controls on model access to accelerate significantly in Q3.
  3. OpenAI's IPO changes the incentive structure for everyone. A public offering at $852B means quarterly earnings pressure, disclosure requirements, and institutional shareholders who care more about revenue than safety milestones. The IPO is a civilizational moment for the industry.
  4. Custom silicon is no longer a differentiator — it's table stakes. Google, OpenAI, Meta, Apple, Anthropic, and Fujitsu all have Broadcom-backed custom chip programs. NVIDIA wins training; custom silicon wins inference economics.
  5. Agentic AI is shipping into production, not just demos. Microsoft Scout, OpenAI Codex agents, Gemini Robotics in Spot. The conversation has shifted from "can agents do this?" to "how do we manage agents doing this at scale?"

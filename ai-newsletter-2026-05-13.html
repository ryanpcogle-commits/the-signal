Lead Story

Pentagon Blacklists Anthropic. Then xAI Surrenders Its Compute to Them.

In the span of two weeks, Anthropic went from pariah to power broker — excluded from a $1B defense deal over safety principles, then handed a massive compute windfall when Elon Musk folded xAI into its orbit.

The Pentagon finalized AI agreements with eight companies — including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Nvidia — while conspicuously excluding Anthropic. The reason: Anthropic refused to strip out safety guardrails governing AI's role in warfare. Defense officials characterized the company as a "supply-chain risk." Anthropic's response was to double down, launching Project Glasswing and its new Claude Mythos Preview model — a cybersecurity-focused system so capable at finding software vulnerabilities that Anthropic immediately restricted its rollout to a vetted group of companies.

Then came the twist. On May 6, Elon Musk announced that xAI would cease operating as an independent company and transferred a significant share of xAI's AI compute to Anthropic — the very company whose cautious approach Musk had spent years publicly mocking. The move blindsided the industry and raises urgent questions about xAI's roadmap, Musk's next priorities, and whether the frontier AI consolidation phase has quietly begun.

The combined effect is striking: Anthropic is simultaneously frozen out of the US defense establishment and flush with new compute from a surprise benefactor. The company's principled stance on safety guardrails, which cost it a Pentagon contract, may end up strengthening rather than diminishing its long-term position.

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Instant Becomes ChatGPT's New Default

OpenAI shipped GPT-5.5 on May 5 — just six weeks after GPT-5.4 — and immediately made it the default model in ChatGPT. Benchmark results show a jump from 65.4 to 81.2 on the AIME 2025 math test, and a leap from 69.2 to 76 on the MMMU-Pro multimodal reasoning benchmark. The rapid cadence underscores a new reality: the model you selected for your product three months ago may already be outdated.

Models

Google Builds Android Around Gemini — and Ships a 2M-Token Context Window

Google released Gemini 3.1 Ultra with a 2-million-token context window and a cost-optimized Flash-Lite variant for high-volume workloads. Simultaneously, Google is rebuilding core Android architecture around Gemini Intelligence — transitioning the platform from an operating system to what the company now calls an "intelligence system." The move is a direct response to Apple's announced AI overhaul, with both companies racing to put their AI at the center of the mobile experience before the other locks it in.

Policy

US Government Expands Pre-Release AI Testing to Google, Microsoft, and xAI

The US Commerce Department's Center for AI Standards and Innovation announced new agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI to conduct pre-release evaluations of their AI models before public deployment. OpenAI and Anthropic had already signed similar agreements, which have now been renegotiated to align with Trump's AI Action Plan. The program represents the most significant expansion of US government AI oversight since the Biden-era executive orders, though critics note the agreements are voluntary and non-binding.

Business

Chief AI Officers Are Now a Boardroom Standard — Not a Buzzword

A new survey finds that 76% of organizations now employ a Chief AI Officer, up from just 26% in 2025 — a tripling in twelve months. The role has rapidly evolved from a defensive PR hire into an operational function overseeing model procurement, data governance, AI-workforce integration, and regulatory compliance. CNBC reports that boards are pushing CAIOs to demonstrate ROI within two quarters, with companies that can't show measurable productivity gains facing internal pressure to scale back AI investments.

India's 99% AI Commitment Is Reshaping the Global Enterprise Playbook

Ninety-nine percent of enterprises in India plan to increase AI adoption, with a 19% year-over-year budget growth rate that outpaces every other major market. Google broke ground on its India AI Hub in April, positioning the country as a national industrial AI ecosystem. The NASSCOM AI Code Sarathi program, launched May 12, targets 150,000 developers for upskilling from basic AI awareness to production-grade coding capability. Analysts say India's combination of developer scale and political commitment to AI investment makes it the most important non-US AI market of 2026.

Robotics

Skild AI Acquires Zebra's Robotics Unit — Physical AI Consolidation Accelerates

Skild AI acquired Zebra Technologies' Robotics Automation business, bringing the Fetch mobile robot fleet and Symmetry orchestration stack under one roof. The deal is part of a broader consolidation wave in physical AI: investors injected over $5.5 billion into enterprise robotics and AI deployment companies in the past 60 days. The thesis is that the hard part of physical AI is no longer the robot hardware — it's the software stack that coordinates fleets, learns from physical environments, and integrates with enterprise systems.

Australia Launches $70M AI Accelerator as Governments Compete to Stay Relevant

The Australian government announced a $70 million AI Accelerator program delivered through its Co-operative Research Centre grants program, with funding awards to begin in 2026 and 2027. The announcement follows similar moves by the UK, Canada, and the EU to establish sovereign AI capacity outside the US-China duopoly. The broader pattern: governments that do not establish domestic AI research infrastructure risk becoming permanent importers of AI capability — dependent on foreign models, foreign data centers, and foreign policy decisions for their digital economies.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The Pentagon/Anthropic split is a preview of every AI company's coming choice. As government AI contracts scale, every frontier lab will face a version of this: accept military use cases with no guardrails, or walk away from nine-figure contracts. There is no neutral ground left.
  2. xAI's dissolution signals that frontier AI consolidation has started. Musk folding xAI and transferring compute to Anthropic is the first major capitulation among the new-generation AI labs. The field that had seven credible frontier labs in 2025 may have three or four by 2027.
  3. Model release cycles are now measured in weeks, not quarters. GPT-5.5 six weeks after GPT-5.4. Four Claude updates in 50 days. Gemini 3.1 Ultra. Grok 4.20. Organizations building on top of these models face a continuous re-evaluation burden that is increasingly unsustainable without abstraction layers.
  4. The AI governance function is becoming a real job. CAIOs tripling in twelve months is not a fad — it reflects genuine enterprise demand for someone accountable when an AI system misbehaves, produces discriminatory outputs, or triggers regulatory scrutiny. The role will become as standard as CFO within three years.
  5. Platform lock-in is the endgame for Google and Apple. Google rebuilding Android around Gemini is not a product move — it is an infrastructure move. Whichever AI becomes the default on mobile controls the highest-reach AI surface on the planet. The race is happening now.

Lead Story

OpenAI Is Filing for an IPO — and Offering Washington a 5% Equity Stake to Smooth the Way

Valued at $730 billion in private markets, OpenAI is preparing a confidential IPO filing targeting as early as September 2026, while dangling government equity to secure political goodwill.

OpenAI has begun preparing a confidential filing for an initial public offering, according to people familiar with the plans. The company is targeting a listing as soon as September 2026, which would mark one of the most closely watched tech IPOs in years. In private markets, OpenAI is valued at approximately $730 billion — a figure that would make it the most valuable AI company to go public in history.

In a striking move, the company floated giving the US government a 5% equity stake in exchange for regulatory goodwill — a gesture that has no precedent in modern tech history. Fortune reported this week that Sam Altman is "seeking a new world order for AI," framing the IPO as more than a liquidity event: it's a bid to entrench OpenAI as the default infrastructure layer for both government and enterprise.

The path isn't smooth. Fortune and other outlets noted that OpenAI has been slowly losing ground to Google and Anthropic in enterprise accounts, with Anthropic forecasting it will be profitable by 2029 — a full year ahead of OpenAI's own projections. The IPO may be as much about locking in momentum as it is about capital.

This Week in AI
Models

GPT-5.6 Ships as a Three-Model Family — Sol, Terra, and Luna

OpenAI released GPT-5.6 this week as a structured lineup targeting different price and performance tiers. Sol is the flagship, featuring a new Ultra subagent mode and a Max reasoning-effort setting for frontier coding and science work. Terra targets GPT-5.5-level quality at half the cost, aimed at enterprise deployments. Luna is built for fast, high-volume tasks at minimum latency. The three-model family pattern — pioneered by Anthropic with Haiku, Sonnet, and Opus — is now the industry standard.

Models

Anthropic Restores Fable 5 Globally as US Lifts Export Controls

The US Commerce Department lifted the export controls that had pulled Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 offline for international users. Both models are now restored globally, with Anthropic adding cybersecurity classifiers described as "the strongest safeguards" applied to any of its models. Meanwhile, Claude Sonnet 5 — which launched June 30 at introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens — continues to draw strong enterprise adoption, with Anthropic now forecasting profitability in 2029, a year ahead of OpenAI's own timeline.

Business

Google DeepMind Loses Two Cornerstone Figures in a Single Week

In what Fortune called a pivotal moment for the lab, Noam Shazeer — VP of Engineering and Gemini co-lead — announced he was joining OpenAI on June 18. The following day, John Jumper, Nobel Chemistry laureate and lead architect of AlphaFold, announced his departure for Anthropic. The back-to-back exits prompted open questions about whether Google DeepMind can maintain its position at the frontier of AI research. DeepMind has not announced replacements for either role.

Models

xAI's Grok 4.5 Is a 1.5-Trillion-Parameter Coding Beast

xAI released Grok 4.5, a 1.5-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model trained heavily on Cursor interaction data — making it the most coding-focused frontier model to date. It scored 83.3% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 while using approximately 25% fewer output tokens than Opus 4.8 on equivalent tasks, a meaningful efficiency gain. Enhanced capabilities include agentic task handling, long-horizon planning, and real-time knowledge work. The model is available now in the Grok app and xAI API.

Infrastructure

Anthropic in Talks to Run Claude on Microsoft's Custom Maia 200 Chips

Anthropic is in early-stage discussions with Microsoft to run Claude inference workloads on Microsoft's custom Maia 200 AI chips via Azure, according to people familiar with the matter. If completed, the deal would diversify Anthropic's chip supply away from NVIDIA and mark the first major frontier-lab deployment on Microsoft's in-house silicon. Separately, NAVER and NVIDIA announced a sovereign AI infrastructure expansion using the NVIDIA DSX platform, starting at 55 megawatts with plans to scale to gigawatt capacity at NAVER's GAK Sejong data center in South Korea.

Policy

HHS Will Use ChatGPT to Audit All 50 States for Healthcare Fraud

The US Department of Health and Human Services announced it will deploy ChatGPT and other AI tools to analyze annual audit reports from all 50 states, targeting fraud, waste, and abuse in federal health spending. The initiative marks one of the largest government deployments of a commercial AI product to date, covering billions in Medicaid and Medicare expenditures. The move follows a broader pattern of federal agencies adopting AI for high-stakes compliance and investigative workloads — without legislative frameworks specifically governing those uses.

Science

AI Designs a Vaccine Component That Just Entered Human Trials

Researchers at the University of Cambridge reported that an AI-designed vaccine component has completed initial human trials — a threshold moment for AI in biomedical design. Separately, the Ames Laboratory used a physics-trained model called DuctGPT to discover rare-earth-free permanent magnets, with the model reasoning about underlying physics, production costs, and component sourcing simultaneously. Both breakthroughs reinforce the emerging consensus that AI's highest-leverage applications are in scientific domains where the search space is too large for human intuition alone.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. OpenAI's IPO sets up a defining moment for the whole industry. A $730B listing would pressure every competitor to show comparable scale. It also hands public market investors their first direct stake in frontier AI — with all the governance scrutiny that follows.
  2. Talent flight from DeepMind is accelerating, not slowing. Losing Shazeer and Jumper in the same week isn't noise — it's a signal that the compensation and culture gaps between labs are widening. Who DeepMind loses next matters.
  3. The three-model family is now the standard product architecture. OpenAI's Sol/Terra/Luna follows Anthropic's Haiku/Sonnet/Opus and Google's own tiered lineup. Expect every major lab to converge on this pattern within six months.
  4. Export controls are becoming a geopolitical AI weapon. The US government pulled Fable 5 offline internationally, then restored it with conditions. The precedent — using export controls to shape how frontier AI models behave globally — is now established and will be used again.
  5. AI in science is crossing from research into production. Vaccine components in human trials. New materials discovered by physics-aware AI. These aren't demos — they're deployments. The life-sciences adoption curve is steeper than most enterprise software analysts are modeling.

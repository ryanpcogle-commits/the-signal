OpenAI Is Heading for a $730 Billion IPO — and the Industry Can't Look Away

The company that started as a nonprofit research lab is preparing to file confidentially for the most anticipated tech IPO of the decade, targeting September 2026.

OpenAI is preparing to file confidentially for an initial public offering in the coming weeks, according to multiple reports, with a potential debut as early as September 2026. The company's last private valuation stands at $730 billion — a figure that would make it one of the most valuable companies in US history at listing. The filing is expected to land before the end of July.

The IPO ambition arrives alongside OpenAI's custom-designed Jalapeño inference chip, unveiled this week, which the company says will dramatically reduce the cost of running its frontier models at scale. Sam Altman has also been circulating a broader governance proposal suggesting that OpenAI — and other leading AI labs — should each cede a 5% stake to a global commons structure, a proposal critics from Semafor to Anthropic's leadership have called a bad deal for everyone but OpenAI.

Fortune reports that OpenAI is "slowly losing ground" to Google and Anthropic in terms of model capability parity, but retains the largest consumer footprint and strongest brand recognition. The IPO, if it closes at or near the private valuation, would instantly transform the competitive dynamics of the entire AI sector.

Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5 Is the Most Agentic Model Yet — and It's Now Your Default

Launched on June 30, Claude Sonnet 5 is now the free and Pro default on claude.ai. It ships with a 1-million-token context window, autonomous tool use (browsers, terminals, code execution), and near-Opus 4.8 performance at a fraction of the cost: $2 / $10 per million tokens in and out. Anthropic says it is the best model currently available for writing style fidelity, voice matching, and complex multi-step instruction-following. Separately, Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI in self-reported annualized revenue and is on track for $47 billion by year-end, with profitability projected for 2029.

Models

GPT-5.6 Exists — But Only the US Government Can Use It Right Now

OpenAI previewed its next-generation model family on June 26: Sol (flagship), Terra (balanced), and Luna (fast and affordable). The catch: GPT-5.6 is the first US frontier model release gated behind government access, currently available only to roughly 20 trusted organizations through the OpenAI API and Codex. General availability is planned for "the coming weeks," with Sol scheduled to launch on Cerebras infrastructure at up to 750 tokens per second — a speed record for a frontier model.

Anthropic vs. the Pentagon: Court Docs Reveal the Fight Is About Principles, Not Contracts

Court documents released this week from Anthropic's lawsuit against the Department of Defense include leaked emails between CEO Dario Amodei and Pentagon Undersecretary Emil Michael. The Wall Street Journal first reported the exchange. The emails show the conflict was never primarily about contract terms — it was about a single question: can an AI lab set ethical limits on a government customer? Anthropic says yes. The DoD said no, and replaced them with OpenAI. The case is expected to set lasting legal precedent for AI procurement across the federal government.

Meta's Secret "Watermelon" Model Matches GPT-5.5 — and Uses 10x More Compute

Meta's Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang told a closed briefing this week that the company's current frontier model in training — internally codenamed Watermelon — matches GPT-5.5 performance on current evaluations and was trained using "an order of magnitude more compute" than Meta's previous frontier model. No public release date has been set. Separately, Grok 4.5 is in private beta at SpaceX and Tesla, xAI's two largest internal deployment partners, suggesting a near-term public release.

Robotics

Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 Is Now Inside Boston Dynamics' Spot

Boston Dynamics announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration means Spot can now use natural language instructions to navigate, inspect, and report on industrial environments without explicit programming. It marks one of the clearest real-world deployments of a frontier AI model in a commercially sold physical robot to date.

Models

China Just Open-Sourced a 1.6-Trillion-Parameter Model — Built Without Western Chips

A 1.6-trillion-parameter Chinese foundation model was open-sourced under the MIT license this week — trained entirely on domestically produced AI chips. The model's release is notable on two fronts: it suggests China's homegrown semiconductor ecosystem has matured faster than US export-control optimists expected, and it immediately raises the bar for what open-source AI looks like at the extreme scale. No benchmark comparisons to Western frontier models have been independently verified yet.

Hardware

OpenAI's Custom "Jalapeño" Chip and the Race to Cut Inference Costs

OpenAI this week unveiled its custom inference chip, internally named Jalapeño, designed to reduce the per-token cost of running its models at scale. The chip is the latest move by a major AI lab to reduce dependence on NVIDIA for inference workloads, following similar efforts from Google (TPUs), Amazon (Trainium/Inferentia), and Meta (MTIA). Analysts note that price-performance ratios for top-tier AI models have roughly tripled in the past twelve months as cost compression across the industry accelerates.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The AI IPO era is beginning. OpenAI at $730B is the opening bell. If it lists at or near private valuation, it will reset public market expectations for every AI-native company — and flood the sector with capital and scrutiny simultaneously.
  2. Government-gated model releases are a new category of power. GPT-5.6 being exclusive to ~20 US government entities is unprecedented. It signals that frontier AI is being treated like classified defense technology — a precedent with enormous geopolitical and commercial implications.
  3. The Anthropic-Pentagon case will define AI procurement law. Whether an AI lab can refuse a government customer on ethical grounds — and survive legally and commercially — is the most important policy question in the industry right now. The answer will shape every government AI contract for a decade.
  4. China's chip independence is advancing on schedule. A 1.6T-parameter model trained domestically signals that US export controls have not stopped Chinese AI development — they have merely rerouted it. The strategic gap may be narrower than policymakers assumed.
  5. Physical AI integration is getting real, fast. Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 in commercially shipped Boston Dynamics hardware is the clearest sign yet that frontier AI is leaving the API and entering the physical world at production scale. Robot-AI integration is no longer a research project.

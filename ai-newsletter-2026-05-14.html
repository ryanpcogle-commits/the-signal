Lead Story

Anthropic's Secret Cybersecurity Model Just Got a White House Briefing

Claude Mythos Preview — a model purpose-built to find security flaws — is rolling out under tight restrictions while Dario Amodei personally briefed senior Trump administration officials on what it can do.

Anthropic this week unveiled Claude Mythos Preview, a new AI model that the company describes as uniquely capable of identifying software weaknesses and mapping attack surfaces. Unlike prior Claude releases, Anthropic has deliberately restricted Mythos to a small cohort of vetted cybersecurity firms under a new initiative called Project Glasswing — citing the model's dual-use potential as the reason for the cautious rollout.

Within days of the announcement, CEO Dario Amodei traveled to Washington to brief senior members of the Trump administration on Mythos's capabilities and the Project Glasswing framework. The meeting underscores a broader pattern: as AI models become genuinely capable in offensive security, governments are no longer content to wait for public releases before engaging the labs that build them.

The timing is notable. The Pentagon this month finalized AI contracts with eight companies — OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI — explicitly leaving Anthropic out of the classified-environment deals. Anthropic's cultivation of a separate White House relationship may be its chosen path around that exclusion.

Pentagon Signs AI Deals With Eight Companies — Anthropic Left Out

The Department of Defense finalized AI agreements granting OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI access to classified environments. Anthropic's absence is conspicuous given its size and the strength of its security-focused models. Analysts suggest the exclusion stems from Anthropic's continued refusal to sign open-ended autonomous-weapons clauses — the same principle that triggered its 2026 DOD friction earlier this year.

Business

Elon Musk Shuts Down xAI as Independent Company, Hands Compute to Anthropic

In a surprise announcement on May 6, Elon Musk declared that xAI would cease operating as a standalone company, transferring a significant portion of its compute capacity to Anthropic. The move ends xAI's run as an independent frontier lab less than two years after Grok's debut. Musk gave no public explanation for the decision, though analysts point to mounting inference costs and Grok's inability to close the gap with GPT-5.5 and Claude Mythos as likely factors.

Infrastructure

Google Is Rebuilding Android Around Gemini — Before Apple Can Strike First

Google is racing to rewire core Android functionality around Gemini Intelligence ahead of Apple's anticipated AI-first iOS overhaul later this year. The strategy, confirmed by CNBC, involves transitioning Android from what Google internally calls "an operating system to an intelligence system" — with Gemini handling tasks that currently require individual apps. Google Gemini 3.1 Ultra, with its 2-million-token native multimodal context window, is the engine powering the effort.

Policy

US Government Now Pre-Tests AI Models Before Public Release

The Center for AI Standards and Innovation has signed pre-release evaluation agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI — adding to its existing agreements with OpenAI and Anthropic. The deals give CAISI early access to models before they ship publicly, enabling red-teaming and risk assessment at government scale. It is the most concrete regulatory framework for frontier AI yet enacted in the United States, and it arrived without legislation.

Models

Chinese Labs Ship Four Frontier Coding Models in Twelve Days

In a coordinated — if unplanned — show of force, four Chinese AI labs released open-weight coding models inside a 12-day window: Z.ai's GLM-5.1, MiniMax's M2.7, Moonshot's Kimi K2.6, and DeepSeek V4 all landed at roughly the same capability ceiling on agentic engineering benchmarks as GPT-5.5, but at meaningfully lower inference cost. The releases confirm that the open-weights frontier is now a direct competitive threat to closed Western models.

Policy

Google: Hackers Are Using AI to Weaponize Zero-Day Vulnerabilities at Scale

A new Google Threat Intelligence report published this week warns that state-sponsored and criminal actors are now routinely using AI to accelerate zero-day weaponization — compressing the window between vulnerability discovery and active exploitation from weeks to hours. The findings add urgency to the Project Glasswing approach: as AI makes attack tooling cheaper, defensive AI capable of finding the same flaws first becomes strategically critical.

Business

Novo Nordisk Bets Its Entire Operation on OpenAI

Danish pharmaceutical giant Novo Nordisk — maker of Ozempic and Wegovy — announced a sweeping strategic partnership with OpenAI to integrate AI across drug discovery, clinical trials, manufacturing, supply chains, and commercial operations simultaneously. The deal represents one of the largest all-in enterprise AI commitments by a non-tech company and signals that pharmaceutical AI is moving from pilot projects to core infrastructure faster than most analysts predicted.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Cybersecurity AI is the new geopolitical lever. Mythos, zero-day weaponization reports, White House briefings — the intersection of AI and national security is no longer abstract. Expect governments to increasingly treat frontier model access as a strategic asset, not a commercial product.
  2. The C-suite AI mandate is now nearly universal. IBM's finding that 76% of organizations have a Chief AI Officer — up from 26% just a year ago — is the fastest executive-title adoption in corporate history. The role is real, not ceremonial.
  3. Open-weight Chinese models are closing the frontier gap. Four labs, twelve days, GPT-5.5-level coding performance at lower cost. The assumption that closed Western models hold a durable capability lead is increasingly hard to defend.
  4. The operating system is becoming the AI. Google rebuilding Android around Gemini is not a feature update — it's a platform redefinition. Apple's response will reshape how two billion people interact with AI daily.
  5. Exclusion from Pentagon contracts is a strategic, not commercial, problem. Anthropic's absence from DoD deals while simultaneously briefing the White House on Mythos suggests the lab is pursuing a different government strategy — one built on credibility rather than contracts. Whether that works will define its next two years.

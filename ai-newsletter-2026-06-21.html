Lead Story

The U.S. Export Ban on Anthropic's Models Has Entered Day Nine — and the API Is Still Down

Washington barred Anthropic from distributing its two most advanced models to any foreign national, anywhere. Nine days later, the outage is still total — and the fallout is reshaping the global AI landscape.

On June 12, the U.S. Department of Commerce issued an export control order barring Anthropic from distributing Fable 5 and Mythos 5 to foreign nationals both inside and outside the United States. As of today, the API at claude-fable-5 still returns errors globally. It is the first time a major AI model has been placed under export controls mid-deployment, forcing businesses, researchers, and developers worldwide off the platform with virtually no warning.

The order reflects a broader shift in U.S. strategy: treating frontier AI models as strategic military and economic assets, subject to the same export restrictions as advanced semiconductors. For Anthropic — which recently filed confidentially for an IPO at a reported $965 billion valuation — the timing could not be more damaging. Enterprise customers are scrambling toward OpenAI and Google Gemini alternatives while Anthropic's lawyers work to negotiate a path back to compliance.

The geopolitical implications extend far beyond one company. Analysts at Axios note this sets a precedent: any frontier model can now be yanked from international access at a moment's notice by the Commerce Department. AI companies building global businesses are being forced to architect their products around geopolitical risk for the first time.

SpaceX Acquires Cursor for $60 Billion — the Largest Startup Acquisition Ever

SpaceX closed a $60 billion acquisition of Cursor, the AI-powered coding assistant, making it the largest startup acquisition in history. The deal signals SpaceX's ambition to build a vertically integrated AI software stack on top of its xAI and Colossus infrastructure. For Cursor's founders, it validates the market's appetite for AI developer tooling at a scale that would have seemed impossible just two years ago. Separately, OpenAI acquired Astral — the company behind Python's most popular developer tools — in what appears to be a countermove to keep its own ecosystem coherent.

Anthropic Locks In 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs from SpaceX's Colossus 1

In a notable infrastructure deal, Anthropic secured access to all available compute capacity at SpaceX/xAI's Colossus 1 data center in Tennessee — over 300 megawatts powered by more than 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs. The arrangement underscores how compute access has become the primary competitive constraint for frontier AI labs. It also creates an unusual dynamic: the same entity (SpaceX) now owns Cursor, whose users will likely run on models trained partly on Colossus 1 hardware that Anthropic is leasing.

NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Is the First Open "Omnimodel" for Physical AI

NVIDIA unveiled Cosmos 3, described as the first fully open "omnimodel" for physical AI. It integrates vision reasoning, world simulation, and action generation into a single mixture-of-transformers architecture — meaning a robot or autonomous system can perceive, simulate consequences, and act using one unified model. Boston Dynamics simultaneously announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog, bringing frontier language reasoning directly into physical hardware deployed at enterprise scale.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Export controls are the new frontier-AI battleground. The Fable 5 ban is the first major deployment-era export control on an AI model. Every lab building global products now has to treat regulatory risk as an infrastructure concern — not a legal footnote.
  2. The IPO race is compressing in real time. OpenAI and Anthropic are both in confidential filing mode simultaneously, targeting the same institutional investors, at combined implied valuations approaching $1.7 trillion. The public markets are about to price frontier AI for the first time.
  3. Compute is the moat, and SpaceX now controls a huge chunk of it. Cursor acquisition + Colossus 1 + xAI gives SpaceX leverage over the AI supply chain that rivals Amazon, Google, and Microsoft. The hyperscaler club just gained an unusual new member.
  4. AI is moving from answering to discovering. An overturned 80-year math conjecture, faster climate models, new materials — the transition from AI-as-assistant to AI-as-researcher is happening in real research settings, not just demos.
  5. Talent is flowing toward perceived long-term winners. A Nobel laureate leaving DeepMind for Anthropic mid-crisis tells you something about where top researchers believe the most important work is being done. Institutional prestige is shifting fast.

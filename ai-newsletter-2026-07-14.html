Lead Story

Sam Altman Is Handing the Government a $42.6B Stake — While Filing for an IPO

OpenAI is simultaneously preparing a September IPO and pitching a plan to give the US government 5% equity in every major AI company. Altman is playing both offense and defense at once.

Sam Altman has proposed that every leading US AI company allot 5 percent of its equity to a public vehicle modeled on the Alaska Permanent Fund — a sovereign fund that pays residents an annual dividend. At OpenAI's current $852 billion valuation, that stake would be worth roughly $42.6 billion. Altman pitched the idea personally to President Trump, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

The move is being read as shrewd regulatory positioning: by proposing a government windfall from AI success, OpenAI makes the case that regulating it too tightly would hurt the public coffers. It is, in effect, a structural argument against AI restrictions dressed up as generosity.

Meanwhile, the company is working with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley on a confidential IPO filing, with a target date as early as September 2026. If it lands at anything close to the current valuation, it would be the largest tech IPO since Alibaba in 2014 — and the most politically consequential in the industry's history.

Google DeepMind Is Hemorrhaging Its Biggest Names — to OpenAI and Anthropic

Noam Shazeer — VP of Engineering and Gemini co-lead — announced he is joining OpenAI. The following day, Nobel Chemistry laureate John Jumper, lead architect of AlphaFold, announced his departure for Anthropic. The departures have raised serious questions about whether DeepMind can remain at the frontier. Fortune noted that insiders are increasingly asking whether the lab that invented the transformer, produced AlphaFold, and built Gemini can hold its talent base against rivals offering equity and autonomy that Google's structure cannot match.

Models

Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5 Is Its Most Agentic Model Yet

Anthropic launched Claude Sonnet 5 with near-Opus 4.8 performance at introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through August 31. The model is designed for agentic workloads — it can autonomously use browsers, terminals, and external tools without human hand-holding. Anthropic also open-sourced its J-lens interpretability technique, which identified an internal subspace in Claude's activations that mirrors the "global workspace" theory from consciousness neuroscience. It is one of the most significant mechanistic interpretability results published this year.

Models

GPT-5.6 Ships With Three Tiers — Sol, Terra, and Luna

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 is now generally available as a three-part lineup. Sol is the flagship — targeting high-end reasoning and coding, with a new Ultra subagent mode and a Max reasoning-effort setting. Terra delivers GPT-5.5-level quality at roughly half the cost. Luna handles fast, high-volume tasks at the lowest price point. The tiered architecture signals that OpenAI is no longer trying to sell one model at one price — it is competing across every cost bracket simultaneously. GPT-5.6 went through an unusual customer-by-customer Commerce Department review before going public, a sign of how entangled AI deployments have become with trade policy.

Policy

The Five Eyes Alliance Publishes Joint Guidance on Agentic AI Risk

The US, Australia, Canada, the UK, and New Zealand released coordinated guidance on security risks in agentic AI systems — the first joint intelligence-community statement specifically addressing autonomous agents. The document identifies five risk categories including privilege escalation, prompt injection via external data, and unintended cross-system actions, and outlines best practices for organizations deploying agents in sensitive environments. The UN separately issued a warning this week that AI systems pose a risk of "catastrophic harm" if governance frameworks are not adopted at scale. The governance conversation has moved from think tanks to intelligence services.

Hardware

TSMC Posts a Record Quarter — the AI Buildout Is Real, Not Just Slides

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company reported record quarterly revenue explicitly attributed to AI chip demand. The figure matters because TSMC sits at the bottleneck of every GPU supply chain: when its revenue rises, it means the trillion-dollar infrastructure commitments announced by Microsoft, Google, and Amazon are actually translating into wafer orders. Separately, South Korea announced an $880 billion, 10-year national investment in semiconductors, AI infrastructure, and robotics — the single largest government AI commitment ever announced by a mid-sized economy.

China Is Not Catching Up on Visual AI — It Is Already There

Image and video generation has become the one domain where Chinese AI labs are not trailing the frontier but competing at it. ByteDance's Seedream and a wave of open-weight visual models from Alibaba are breaking the assumption that the best generative AI ships from San Francisco. Analysts note that visual generation is also the area with the clearest commercial path in consumer apps and advertising — which gives Chinese players structural distribution advantages in Asian markets where ByteDance and Alibaba already dominate.

Infrastructure

AI Has Now Predicted Over 200 Million Protein Structures — and Designed a Vaccine

A University of Cambridge team announced that an AI-designed vaccine component completed initial human trials this week, the first such result to reach clinical testing. Meanwhile, the cumulative tally of protein structures predicted by AI systems crossed 200 million, covering the vast majority of known biological sequences. Researchers say the database is now unlocking work on antibiotic resistance, cancer treatments, and rare diseases that would have been computationally impossible five years ago. The Ames Laboratory also published results using an AI workflow called DuctGPT that discovered rare-earth-free permanent magnets — a finding with immediate implications for electric vehicle motors and wind turbines.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. OpenAI is buying political protection before it goes public. The government equity proposal is inseparable from the IPO timeline. By offering Washington a financial stake in AI success, Altman is structurally aligning the government's incentives with OpenAI's valuation — a move that makes aggressive regulation harder to justify.
  2. DeepMind's talent drain is the story beneath all other stories. Shazeer and Jumper leaving for rivals within 24 hours is not a coincidence — it reflects cultural and structural tensions inside a lab that is simultaneously a Google product division and a frontier research organization. Those two things may be incompatible.
  3. Tiered model pricing is now the competitive battleground. GPT-5.6's Sol/Terra/Luna structure mirrors what AWS did to cloud in 2010. The labs that win the next three years won't just have the best model — they'll have the best model at every price point simultaneously.
  4. Agentic AI has moved from demo to infrastructure risk. When the Five Eyes intelligence alliance issues joint guidance on agentic AI, it's a signal that autonomous agents are deployed widely enough to constitute a systemic threat surface — not a future concern but a present one.
  5. Scientific AI is quietly outpacing commercial AI in real-world impact. 200 million protein predictions. An AI-designed vaccine in human trials. Rare-earth-free magnets discovered by a physics model. The commercial AI race gets the headlines, but the scientific applications may be where the decade's most durable value is being created.

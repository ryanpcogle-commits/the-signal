Lead Story

An AI Just Disproved a Geometry Conjecture That Stumped Mathematicians for 80 Years

OpenAI's internal reasoning model autonomously disproved an Erdős conjecture that had stood since 1946 — and a Fields medalist called it "a milestone in AI mathematics."

On May 20, OpenAI announced that one of its general-purpose reasoning models working autonomously had disproved a central conjecture in discrete geometry posed by mathematician Paul Erdős in 1946. The conjecture had resisted human proof for eight decades. No hints were given to the model — it found the counterexample independently.

Fields Medal winner Tim Gowers, one of the most respected mathematicians alive, reviewed the result and called it "a milestone in AI mathematics." The significance isn't just the result itself — it's that the model didn't retrieve a known solution. It reasoned its way to something genuinely new.

This is a different kind of AI achievement than benchmark records or coding competitions. Erdős problems are famous for their simplicity of statement and near-impossibility of resolution. If AI can reliably operate at this level of abstract mathematical reasoning, the implication for scientific discovery — drug design, physics, cryptography — is profound.

This Week in AI
Models

Google I/O: Gemini 3.5 Flash Goes GA, Spark Agent Debuts, Omni Arrives

Google's I/O developer conference delivered three major Gemini announcements in a single week. Gemini 3.5 Flash became generally available today — frontier-level intelligence at 4× the speed of comparable models, priced at $1.50/$9 per million tokens with a 1M-token context window. It posts 76.2% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, beating Gemini 3.1 Pro on coding and agentic tasks. Google also launched Gemini Spark, a reasoning agent that works across connected apps, and Gemini Omni — a new multimodal model purpose-built for video generation and editing. Additionally, three major creative platforms — Adobe, Canva, and CapCut — will integrate directly into the Gemini app, letting Gemini generate content and pass it immediately to professional editing tools without switching apps.

Models

Anthropic's Claude Mythos Clears a 32-Step Cyber-Attack Range

Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview — a model the company has been quietly developing for advanced reasoning and security work — completed a 32-step end-to-end simulated cyber-attack range without human assistance. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei personally briefed senior Trump administration officials on Mythos at the White House, framing it as a defensive security tool. OpenAI's GPT-5.5 subsequently cleared the same range, three weeks later. The back-to-back results suggest AI is maturing rapidly as an offensive-security research tool — which, depending on who holds it, cuts both ways.

Business

Meta Caught Monitoring Employees to Train AI — Then Fired 8,000 of Them

Leaked audio confirmed that Meta had been tracking employees' Gmail, coding sessions, and internal tool usage to generate training data for its AI systems — without explicit consent. The same week the audio surfaced, Meta announced layoffs of approximately 8,000 employees, with the official explanation citing AI efficiency gains. Intuit followed days later with 3,000 cuts for the same stated reason. The double event crystallized a tension that has been building across the industry: companies are simultaneously using workers to train AI and using that AI to eliminate workers.

Policy

Google, Microsoft, and xAI Agree to Government Pre-Release Model Testing

The Trump administration announced that major AI developers — including Google, Microsoft, and Elon Musk's xAI — have agreed to provide federal regulators early access to frontier models before public release. The arrangement is voluntary and non-binding, but it marks the first systematic government access to AI systems before deployment. The policy came weeks after the White House scrapped its earlier AI safety executive order following pressure from Zuckerberg, Musk, and AI czar David Sacks. The new arrangement is narrower in scope but more practically enforceable.

Infrastructure

58.5% of Google Searches Now End Without a Click

Google's AI Overviews now appear in approximately 20% of all search queries, and new data shows 58.5% of Google searches in 2026 resolve entirely on the results page — no click to any external website. The figure is up from 34% in 2023. For publishers, bloggers, and media companies that rely on search traffic, the shift is existential. Google insists AI Overviews increase overall search engagement, but the data on downstream traffic tells a different story. The open web is being consumed from above.

Hardware

Researchers Achieve 100× Energy Reduction in AI Computing

Researchers at Penn unveiled a hybrid neural-symbolic approach that slashes AI energy consumption by up to 100× while actually improving model accuracy — combining deep neural networks with human-style symbolic reasoning rather than scaling parameters alone. Separately, a team demonstrated a hybrid light-matter particle that could dramatically accelerate AI inference at drastically lower power draw. Both results point toward the same architectural insight: the brute-force scaling era may be hitting physical limits, and the next efficiency gains will come from rethinking how models process information, not just throwing more chips at the problem.

Models

Grok 4.3, Gemma 4, and a Subquadratic LLM Round Out a Busy Month

Beyond the headline launches, May has seen a steady stream of model releases. xAI's Grok 4.3 arrived with aggressive pricing, a voice cloning suite, and a dedicated "Imagine" agent mode for creative tasks. Google's Gemma 4 open family — released May 4 — was engineered specifically for reasoning and agentic workflows, delivering remarkable intelligence-per-parameter against much larger closed models. Most architecturally interesting: SubQ shipped the first commercial subquadratic LLM with a 12-million-token context window, challenging the transformer's dominance in long-context applications. After April's frontier sprint, May went quieter on scale and louder on fundamentals.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI is doing genuine science, not just benchmarks. Disproving an 80-year Erdős conjecture is categorically different from scoring well on MMLU. If this generalises, every hard open problem in mathematics and physics becomes fair game within the decade.
  2. The zero-click web is no longer a future scenario. 58.5% of Google searches ending without a click is a structural shift. Every business model built on search traffic needs to rethink its distribution from first principles.
  3. AI security is splitting into offensive and defensive tracks. Mythos and GPT-5.5 completing cyber ranges marks a new era where AI is a first-class security tool. The race to deploy it defensively — before adversaries deploy it offensively — is already underway.
  4. Corporate honesty about AI displacement is collapsing. Surveilling employees to build AI, then cutting them for AI efficiency, in the same week, is a defining data point. Workers and regulators are watching the gap between stated values and actual behavior narrow to zero.
  5. Architecture is replacing scale as the core competition. Subquadratic attention, hybrid neural-symbolic reasoning, 100× energy cuts — May's story isn't "bigger models." It's "smarter models at a fraction of the cost." The next phase of the race will be won on efficiency, not parameter counts.

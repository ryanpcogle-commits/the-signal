Anthropic Overtakes OpenAI in Valuation — and Files Confidentially for IPO

A new $965 billion financing round vaults Anthropic past OpenAI as the world's most valuable AI company, while a confidential IPO filing signals the biggest public offering since Meta.

Anthropic closed a landmark financing round this week at a $965 billion valuation, officially leapfrogging OpenAI to claim the title of the world's most valuable AI startup. The company simultaneously filed confidentially for an IPO, positioning itself to join an extraordinarily short list alongside SpaceX and OpenAI as potential trillion-dollar listings of 2026.

The milestone is driven primarily by the breakout commercial success of Claude Code, Anthropic's AI coding assistant, which has dominated the enterprise developer market and turned the company's revenue trajectory sharply upward. Claude Code has become the product that transformed Anthropic from a research-first safety lab into a legitimate commercial juggernaut.

The IPO filing — if it proceeds — would mark the most significant test yet of whether Wall Street values AI safety as a business strategy, not just a branding exercise. Anthropic's dual mandate of commercial success and safety-focused research has long been treated skeptically by investors; a successful listing would settle that debate decisively.

Microsoft Unveils MAI-Code-1-Flash and MAI-Thinking-1 at Build 2026

At its Build developer conference in San Francisco, Microsoft unveiled two proprietary models: MAI-Code-1-Flash, which converts natural-language descriptions into working application code, and MAI-Thinking-1, a dedicated reasoning model. Both are positioned on cost efficiency — Microsoft framing the launches as a strategy to lower developer costs and reduce reliance on OpenAI models. The moves put Microsoft in direct competition with both Anthropic and OpenAI in the coding tools market, a front on which it previously held only a distribution role.

NVIDIA Unveils RTX Spark Superchip at Computex — "Reinventing the PC"

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang took the stage at Computex 2026 to unveil the RTX Spark, an Arm-based superchip developed in partnership with Microsoft, designed to integrate AI agents, content creation, and gaming workloads on a single portable device. Huang described the chip as "reinventing the PC" — the clearest signal yet that NVIDIA intends to move from data center dominance into the consumer hardware market where Apple's chips currently reign.

Robotics

NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Is the First Fully Open "Omnimodel" for Physical AI

NVIDIA launched Cosmos 3, described as the first fully open omnimodel for physical AI. Built on a mixture-of-transformers architecture, it combines vision reasoning, world simulation, and action generation in a single system — enabling AI to model and interact with the physical world rather than just text and images. NVIDIA highlighted applications in robotics and autonomous systems, positioning Cosmos 3 as foundational infrastructure for the physical AI era the company declared had arrived at CES.

Congress Drops a 269-Page Federal AI Bill That Would Override Every State Law

U.S. Congress introduced a sweeping 269-page AI regulatory bill that, if passed, would preempt every existing state-level AI law in America. The legislation — covering liability, transparency requirements, and safety standards — arrives as California, Colorado, and Texas have each enacted their own AI rules, setting up a major federalism confrontation. The bill is already drawing intense lobbying from major AI labs, who broadly prefer a single federal framework over a patchwork of 50 state regimes, even as they contest specific provisions.

Claude Code turned principled research into an enterprise revenue machine. Every other safety-focused lab is watching this closely.

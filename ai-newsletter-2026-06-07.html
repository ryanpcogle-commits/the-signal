Lead Story

Anthropic Hits $965 Billion Valuation, Files for IPO, and Locks Up 220,000 GPUs

In a single week, Anthropic closed a financing round that nearly valued the company at $1 trillion, confidentially filed for a public listing, and secured exclusive access to one of the largest GPU clusters on the planet.

Anthropic announced the closing of a financing round that values the company at $965 billion — surpassing OpenAI and making it the most highly valued private AI lab in the world. The company simultaneously confirmed it has confidentially filed for an IPO, setting the stage for what could become one of the most closely watched public offerings in technology history.

To fuel that ambition, Anthropic struck a deal to secure all compute capacity at SpaceX and xAI's Colossus 1 data center in Tennessee — gaining access to over 300 megawatts of power and more than 220,000 NVIDIA GPUs. That is more dedicated AI compute than most sovereign nations possess.

The moves arrive as Anthropic already holds a commanding lead in the red-hot AI coding tools market, with Microsoft and Google now scrambling to catch up. For a company founded on safety research just three years ago, the trajectory is staggering.

Today in AI
Models

Microsoft Launches MAI-Code-1-Flash and MAI-Thinking-1 — Its First In-House Foundation Models

Microsoft unveiled MAI-Code-1-Flash at its Build developer conference, its first proprietary model that converts written descriptions into application source code — a direct challenge to Anthropic's Claude in the coding market. The company also introduced MAI-Thinking-1, available in private preview through Microsoft Foundry, designed for deep reasoning tasks integrated into enterprise applications. The launches signal that Microsoft, long a distributor of OpenAI's models, is now building its own frontier capability stack.

Models

GPT-Rosalind Outperforms GPT-5.5 on Medical Benchmarks — While Using Fewer Tokens

OpenAI's specialized medical model GPT-Rosalind is turning heads in research circles, posting 27.5% accuracy on MedChemBench against GPT-5.5's 25.1% — while using 7.2% fewer tokens. On GeneBench, GPT-Rosalind uses 31% fewer tokens than GPT-5.5 while achieving higher accuracy. The results are a signal that domain-specific fine-tuned models can beat general-purpose frontier models on specialized tasks, at lower cost. A French startup, Zenkolab, is separately deploying AI retinal image analysis to detect eye diseases earlier, demonstrating the clinical pipeline for these capabilities.

Business

Google I/O 2026: Gemini 3.5 Flash, DeepMind's $90M Talent Bet, and Cheaper Enterprise AI

At Google I/O 2026, Google showcased Gemini 3.5 Flash — a cheaper, faster model aimed squarely at enterprise customers who need scale without frontier-model price tags. Separately, Google DeepMind closed a deal to hire more than 20 Contextual AI researchers under an $80–$90 million licensing arrangement, absorbing a team focused on enterprise retrieval and knowledge-grounded AI. Boston Dynamics also announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform — bringing large language model reasoning into physical inspection workflows.

Policy

AI Lab CEOs Send Joint Letter to Congress Warning of AI-Enabled Bioweapon Risk

The heads of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI co-signed an open letter to US Congress on June 5th, calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. The letter states plainly that "advances in AI are eroding the technical barriers previously needed to weaponize biological material." It is the most direct public acknowledgment from AI labs that their own technology could enable mass-casualty events, and the first time all four major frontier AI CEOs have signed a joint policy document. The White House also issued an executive order the same week promoting advanced AI innovation and security.

Robotics

BYD Enters Humanoid Robotics as Europe Gets Its First Commercial Robotaxi

Chinese automaker BYD confirmed entry into the humanoid robotics sector, leveraging its existing expertise in batteries, sensors, software, and AI — a convergence play that mirrors Tesla's own Optimus program. On the autonomous vehicle front, Uber, WeRide, and AVOMO launched Spain's first commercial robotaxi service, with initial deployment planned across the Madrid region. It marks the first revenue-generating robotaxi rollout in continental Europe, and signals that the self-driving commercial timeline is now a European story, not just an American and Chinese one.

Infrastructure

Orion-100B Trains a 100-Billion-Parameter Model for $1.25 Per Hour

Orion-100B has demonstrated training a 100-billion-parameter model for just $1.25 per hour — a figure that would have been unthinkable 18 months ago when comparable runs cost millions of dollars. The breakthrough is driven by efficiency improvements in distributed training software, not new hardware, suggesting the cost floor for large model training is collapsing faster than the hardware roadmap predicted. Separately, NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform is achieving 10x higher agent throughput compared to earlier systems, with Confidential Computing at rack scale now enabling US healthcare providers to run sensitive patient data through AI inference pipelines securely.

Business

Ramp Hits $44 Billion Valuation as AI Finance Agents Go Mainstream

American fintech company Ramp, now valued at $44 billion, is deploying AI agents capable of autonomously handling expense analysis, payment management, and financial reconciliation tasks traditionally performed by human accounting teams. The company's rapid ascent reflects a broader pattern: enterprise AI value is accruing fastest in back-office automation where the ROI is measurable in days, not quarters. Tempus, a clinical AI firm in Chicago, made a parallel move — upgrading its Lens oncology platform with agentic AI that runs autonomous, multi-step drug discovery workflows.

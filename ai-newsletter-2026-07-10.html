Lead Story

Grok 4.5 Enters the Benchmark Wars — and Lands Fourth

xAI's latest model sparked independent benchmarking, a viral political-bias controversy, and a token-efficiency claim that could reshape the cost math for high-volume agentic workloads.

Grok 4.5, released by Elon Musk's xAI on July 8, 2026, made its first full day in the wild on Thursday — and the AI community wasted no time putting it through its paces. Artificial Analysis ranked Grok 4.5 fourth on its Intelligence Index with a score of 54, behind Claude Fable 5 (#1), GPT-5.5 (#2), and Claude Opus 4.8 (#3). The model sits comfortably in frontier territory but does not unseat the current leaders.

The more consequential claim came from xAI's own team: Grok 4.5 is significantly more token-efficient than its predecessor, meaning the cost per useful output token drops considerably for agentic workloads that run thousands of tool calls per session. If the efficiency claim holds under independent scrutiny, it could shift enterprise buying decisions even without a top-three intelligence ranking.

A separate controversy erupted almost immediately. Independent testers posted side-by-side comparisons suggesting Grok 4.5 exhibited measurable political lean on current-events prompts — reigniting a long-running debate about value alignment at xAI and whether the model's training data introduces systematic bias. xAI has not issued a formal response.

Models

Anthropic Restores Fable 5 Globally — and Launches Sonnet 5 at Introductory Pricing

After a nearly month-long pause triggered by jailbreak concerns in mid-June, Anthropic restored access to Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 globally on July 1, following a deal with the Trump administration and the implementation of upgraded cybersecurity guardrails. The models had been pulled from international availability after US export-control discussions flagged potential misuse risk. Simultaneously, Anthropic launched Claude Sonnet 5 — offering near-Opus 4.8 performance at an introductory $2/$10 per-million-token price through August 31, making frontier-class reasoning the most affordable it has ever been.

Infrastructure

Claude Cowork Goes Mobile — Agentic Sessions No Longer Need a Desktop

Anthropic launched Claude Cowork for iOS and Android on July 9, eliminating the previous constraint that required a desktop or laptop to keep agentic sessions active. Users can now delegate long-running knowledge-work tasks — across documents, spreadsheets, and presentations — from their phones, monitor agent progress remotely, and resume sessions across devices without interruption. The release positions Anthropic directly against OpenAI's newly merged ChatGPT Work product and marks a significant step toward always-available AI work agents.

Business

OpenAI Merges Codex and ChatGPT Into One Super App

OpenAI consolidated its two flagship products — ChatGPT and Codex — into a single unified application this week alongside the launch of ChatGPT Work, a dedicated product tier aimed at enterprise knowledge workers. The merger simplifies OpenAI's product surface and signals an intent to compete head-on with Anthropic's Cowork platform and Microsoft's Copilot suite. Separately, Fortune reported that OpenAI has filed confidentially for an IPO and offered the US government a 5% equity stake worth roughly $42.6 billion at the company's $852 billion valuation — an unprecedented move in AI industry-government relations.

Business

DeepMind Lost Two Stars in One Week — and Gained Anthropic's Nobel Prize Hire

Google DeepMind suffered an extraordinary week of senior departures. Noam Shazeer, VP of Engineering and Gemini co-lead, announced he was joining OpenAI. The following day, Nobel Chemistry laureate John Jumper — who led the AlphaFold team — announced his departure for Anthropic, where he will head a new computational biology division. Anthropic also separately acquired Coefficient Bio, a computational biology startup, signaling a serious push into AI-assisted drug discovery. Fortune questioned whether DeepMind can retain its position at the frontier after losing two researchers of this caliber in 48 hours.

Models

Gemini 3.5 Pro Is Now Six Weeks Late — Still No GA Date

As of July 10, Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro has no confirmed general-availability launch date and remains confined to a limited Vertex AI enterprise preview. The model missed its June 30 GA target and a separate commitment made at Google I/O, making it six weeks overdue. The delays arrive at a particularly bad moment, given the week's talent departures and growing questions about whether DeepMind's internal turmoil is affecting the product roadmap. Google has not commented publicly on the revised timeline.

Policy

Illinois Signs AI Safety Act; Five Eyes Nations Publish Agentic AI Security Guidance

Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker signed the state's AI Safety Measures Act into law, making Illinois the third US state to enact comprehensive AI safety legislation. Separately, the cybersecurity and intelligence agencies of the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom — the "Five Eyes" alliance — jointly released guidance titled "Careful Adoption of Agentic AI Services." The document identifies five categories of risk specific to agentic AI: unauthorized action, privilege escalation, supply-chain compromise, data exfiltration, and model manipulation. The five-nation coordination signals that governments view agentic AI, not just large language models, as the primary near-term security concern.

Hardware

South Korea Commits $880 Billion to AI — Samsung and SK Hynix Lead

South Korea announced an $880 billion 10-year national investment plan covering semiconductors, AI infrastructure, and robotics. Samsung and SK Hynix are set to commit $518 billion of that total toward new chip fabrication sites. The announcement comes as South Korea seeks to strengthen its position as the world's primary supplier of high-bandwidth memory — the chip type most in demand for training and running large AI models. Separately, NAVER and NVIDIA announced an expansion of sovereign AI infrastructure using the NVIDIA DSX platform, starting at 55 megawatts with plans to scale to gigawatt capacity at the GAK Sejong data center.

Models

China's GLM-5.2 Reignites the AI Race Debate

Z.ai's GLM-5.2 has become the centerpiece of a growing debate over whether China is closing the gap with US frontier AI labs. The model is inexpensive to run and shows benchmark performance comparable to second-tier offerings from Anthropic and OpenAI — without the export-control constraints that limit access to US models in some markets. Analysts note that while GLM-5.2 does not match Fable 5 or GPT-5.5 on complex reasoning, its cost-performance ratio makes it highly attractive for enterprise deployments in Asia, the Middle East, and parts of Europe where US-model access is politically or commercially restricted.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Talent is now the most visible competitive weapon. DeepMind losing Shazeer and Jumper in 48 hours is not a coincidence — it reflects a market for elite AI researchers that has never been more liquid or more expensive. Every lab announcement now comes with a talent scoreboard.
  2. Agentic AI going mobile is a step-change moment. Claude Cowork on iOS and Android breaks the desktop tether. When agents can be delegated, monitored, and resumed from any device, the friction cost of AI adoption collapses.
  3. OpenAI is aggressively consolidating its product surface before an IPO. Merging Codex and ChatGPT, launching ChatGPT Work, offering the government equity — these moves read as a company simplifying its narrative for public markets investors.
  4. China's GLM-5.2 is proof that cost-performance wins markets that benchmarks don't. US frontier models lead on raw capability, but in markets where export controls bite or pricing is prohibitive, competitive Chinese models are gaining ground quickly.
  5. Governments are regulating agentic AI before it's even mainstream. The Five Eyes joint guidance and the Illinois AI Safety Act both target agentic systems specifically. Regulators are not waiting for adoption to catch up; they're writing rules for the next wave while the current one is still cresting.

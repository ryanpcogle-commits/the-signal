Lead Story

Anthropic's Extraordinary Week: $200B Cloud Deal, Mythos at the White House, and xAI's Compute

In seven days, Anthropic signed a quarter-trillion-dollar infrastructure commitment, debuted a model powerful enough to land Dario Amodei in the Oval Office, and absorbed compute from a rival that quietly ceased to exist.

The biggest story of the week — and arguably of 2026 so far — is Anthropic's commitment to spend $200 billion with Google Cloud over the next five years, one of the largest infrastructure agreements in tech history. The deal locks in unprecedented compute capacity at a moment when frontier AI training costs are accelerating beyond what any single balance sheet can absorb independently.

Simultaneously, Anthropic's newly released Claude Mythos Preview model drew immediate attention from the highest levels of the U.S. government. CEO Dario Amodei met with senior members of the Trump administration at the White House days after Mythos launched, as officials convened a working group to assess the model's capabilities. Mythos and OpenAI's GPT-5.5 have both now cleared a 32-step end-to-end cyber-attack range — a first for any commercial models.

Then, on May 6, Elon Musk announced that xAI would no longer operate as an independent company and transferred a large share of its AI compute to Anthropic. In a single week, Anthropic went from one of several frontier labs to the clear organizational center of gravity in the AI industry.

This Week in AI
Policy

Pentagon Signs AI Deals With 8 Giants — and Deliberately Leaves Anthropic Out

The Defense Department finalized AI agreements with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI, expanding the use of AI into classified military environments for analysis, logistics, and large-scale data processing. Anthropic was conspicuously absent. The exclusion follows a pattern that began earlier this year and underscores a fundamental tension: Anthropic's safety commitments continue to cost it major government contracts even as its commercial standing reaches new heights.

Policy

US Government Demands Early AI Model Access from Google, Microsoft, and xAI

The Center for AI Standards and Innovation announced formal agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI to evaluate their models before public release, under the framework of President Trump's AI Action Plan. OpenAI and Anthropic renegotiated existing evaluation partnerships to align with the new priorities. The move represents the clearest signal yet that Washington intends to insert itself into the AI release cycle — raising questions about how evaluation requirements will interact with competitive release timelines.

Models

Google Gemma 4 Ships for Open-Source Agentic Workflows

Google released the Gemma 4 model family on May 4, engineered specifically for advanced reasoning and agentic workflows with high intelligence-per-parameter efficiency. The release signals Google's continued commitment to the open-source frontier even as its proprietary Gemini models compete at the top of commercial benchmarks. Separately, Google I/O 2026 is one week away, with the keynote set for May 19 — observers expect major announcements on Gemini 2.5 updates, Android 17, and new agentic developer tooling.

Models

Anthropic Introduces "Dreaming" — Agents That Review Their Own Behavior

Alongside Mythos, Anthropic published a research preview of a new agentic technique called "dreaming" — a mechanism that allows autonomous AI systems to review prior behavior between sessions, identify patterns in their own actions, and refine future performance without human intervention. The capability is launching in preview for long-running agentic workflows in coding, finance, and legal domains. If it works as described, it would mark the first time a commercial AI system can meaningfully self-improve through reflection rather than retraining.

Business

Chief AI Officer Is Now a Standard Role — 76% of Large Orgs Have One

An IBM study published Monday found that 76% of organizations now employ a Chief AI Officer, up from just 26% in 2025. The explosive adoption rate reflects both the growing strategic importance of AI governance and the pressure boards are placing on executives to demonstrate accountable AI leadership. The same report noted that AI may soon be gunning for C-suite roles themselves — a detail IBM buried deep in the findings but which CNBC ran as its headline.

Models

xAI Launches Grok 4.3 at Aggressively Low Pricing Before Shutdown

In what appears to be a final product release before its dissolution as an independent entity, xAI launched Grok 4.3 at aggressively low pricing, featuring a voice cloning suite and a specialized "Imagine" agent mode for creative projects. The timing — days before Musk announced xAI would cease independent operations and transfer compute to Anthropic — raises questions about whether Grok 4.3 was intended as a competitive salvo or a farewell. The model's fate under Anthropic's stewardship remains unclear.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Anthropic is becoming the industry's center of gravity. A $200B cloud deal, White House meetings over Mythos, absorbed xAI compute, and a "dreaming" agentic breakthrough — all in one week. The competitive landscape looks meaningfully different than it did seven days ago.
  2. Government access to AI models is becoming mandatory, not voluntary. Trump's AI Action Plan is turning evaluation partnerships from goodwill gestures into structural requirements. Companies that resist will face contracting consequences.
  3. Self-improving agents are no longer hypothetical. Anthropic's "dreaming" preview is the first commercial attempt at between-session self-review. If it holds up outside controlled conditions, it changes the architecture of every long-running agentic system being built right now.
  4. Open-source and closed-source are converging on agentic use cases. Gemma 4 is purpose-built for agents. So is Anthropic's Sonnet with Agent Teams. The battle is no longer about chat quality — it's about which models can reliably execute multi-step workflows without human intervention.
  5. AI governance is now a C-suite issue everywhere. When 76% of large organizations have a Chief AI Officer, the question has shifted from "should we have one?" to "what should they actually own?" Boards are getting specific.

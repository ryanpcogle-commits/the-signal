Lead Story

Apple Kills Old Siri, Rebuilds It on Google's Gemini in Tim Cook's Final Keynote

WWDC 2026 rewrote the alliance map of the AI industry: Apple and Google — rivals for a decade — are now bound by a $1 billion-per-year deal that puts Gemini at the center of every iPhone.

Apple unveiled a fully rebuilt Siri at WWDC 2026, rebranded as Siri AI, running on a custom 1.2-trillion-parameter Google Gemini model licensed under a multi-year agreement estimated at roughly $1 billion per year. The new Siri can compose emails, manage files, interpret live camera input, and perform agentic tasks across native and third-party apps — capabilities the old Siri never approached despite a decade of development.

The announcement landed with extra weight because CEO Tim Cook confirmed it was his final WWDC keynote, signaling a leadership transition at Apple alongside its biggest AI pivot in company history. Developer access to Siri AI began June 8; a public beta is expected later this year.

The deal reshapes competitive dynamics significantly. Google is now the AI backbone of the world's most valuable consumer hardware ecosystem — a position that was unthinkable just 18 months ago. Apple also introduced a multi-AI Extensions system that makes Claude an iPhone option for the first time, marking Anthropic's entry into the world's largest mobile platform.

Business

Anthropic and OpenAI Both Filed for IPO This Month — Within 8 Days of Each Other

In one of the most consequential weeks in AI business history, Anthropic filed a confidential draft S-1 with the SEC on June 1, followed by OpenAI on June 8. Both are targeting public listings as early as Q4 2026. Anthropic's post-money valuation from its recent Series H stands at approximately $965 billion, with annualized revenue reported at $47 billion. OpenAI targets a roughly $1 trillion valuation with $25 billion in annualized revenue — but disclosed it lost $1.22 for every dollar earned in Q1 2026. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading OpenAI's offering. The nonprofit research lab that started in 2015 may soon be the most valuable tech IPO in a decade.

Models

Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 Demolishes GPT-5.5 on Coding — by 22 Points

Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 on June 9, its most significant dual model release to date. Fable 5 scored 80.3% on SWE-Bench Pro against GPT-5.5's 58.6% — a 22-point margin that is not a rounding error. It also scored 29.3% on FrontierCode versus Opus 4.8's 13.4%, and leads state-of-the-art benchmarks across coding, vision, and scientific research. Fable 5 is priced at $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output, but is free on Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise plans through June 22. Claude Opus 4.8 meanwhile holds the #1 spot on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at 61.4, ahead of GPT-5.5 (60.2) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (57). The era of OpenAI leading on coding benchmarks appears to be over.

Policy

Congress Takes Its First Real Shot at Comprehensive AI Regulation

Representatives Jay Obernolte (R-CA) and Lori Trahan (D-MA) released a bipartisan discussion draft of the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act of 2026 — the first proposed comprehensive federal framework for governing AI in the United States. The bill would require frontier model developers to disclose model information, submit to third-party audits through designated Independent Verification Organizations, and prohibit retaliation against whistleblowers. The timing is pointed: Colorado's AI Act takes effect June 30, New York's legislature just sent seven AI-related bills to Governor Hochul, and Rhode Island passed a therapy chatbot ban. Federal preemption is now a live question for every AI company operating in the US.

Models

An OpenAI Model Just Solved an 80-Year-Old Math Problem No Human Could

OpenAI's internal AI model found a counterexample to the planar unit distance problem — a famous conjecture posed by Hungarian mathematician Paul Erdős in 1946. Canadian mathematician Daniel Litt called it "the first result produced autonomously by an AI that I find interesting in itself." Separately, a neuro-symbolic system combining Google's Gemini Deep Think with a tree-search framework derived novel exact analytical solutions for an open theoretical physics problem — the power spectrum of gravitational radiation from cosmic strings. Fields Medal winner Terence Tao, profiled by Quanta Magazine on June 8, has become an active advocate for AI-assisted mathematical research. The "AI as proof assistant" era is giving way to "AI as discoverer."

Robotics

Humanoid Robots Are Now Economically Viable — Prices Fell 70% Since 2024

Humanoid robot prices have dropped 70% since 2024, with Tesla's Optimus now on track for commercial rollout in 2026 at a projected $20,000–$30,000 targeting manufacturing applications. Tesla, BMW, Samsung, and Foxconn are actively deploying fully autonomous production lines that run 24/7 with zero human workers. At CVPR 2026 in Denver this month, Google's RT-X demonstrated a single foundation model — trained on 13 million+ trajectories from 22 robot types — controlling any robot morphology for any task described in natural language. Researchers are calling it the "GPT moment for physical AI." The global robotics market is projected to reach $124.37 billion in 2026.

Infrastructure

Microsoft Launches 7 In-House AI Models to Break Its OpenAI Dependency

At Build 2026 on June 2, Microsoft unveiled seven in-house MAI models covering image, voice, transcription, coding, and reasoning — including MAI-Code-1-Flash, its first self-developed code-generation model that outperforms Claude Haiku 4.5 on SWE-Verified while using up to 60% fewer tokens. MAI-Thinking-1, a reasoning model, is being compared directly to Claude Opus, GPT-5.5, and Gemini. The launch is a direct strategic move to reduce Microsoft's dependence on OpenAI and lower per-query costs for enterprise customers — particularly meaningful given OpenAI's $1.22-loss-per-dollar economics disclosed in its S-1 filing. Microsoft's 11,000-model Foundry catalog now includes the full MAI family.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The AI industry is going public — at trillion-dollar valuations. Anthropic and OpenAI both filing S-1s in the same week is the clearest signal yet that the venture era of AI is closing. Public market scrutiny — of unit economics, safety practices, and governance — will now be unavoidable. OpenAI losing $1.22 per dollar earned won't survive long as a private-company secret.
  2. The Apple-Google AI alliance changes the entire consumer stack. When the world's most valuable consumer hardware company licenses a competitor's model for $1 billion a year, it signals that even Apple doesn't believe it can win the AI race alone. Siri AI running on Gemini means Google's models are now the AI layer for 1.5 billion iPhones.
  3. Anthropic is quietly winning the benchmark war. Claude Opus 4.8 at #1 on the Intelligence Index. Fable 5 at 80.3% on SWE-Bench Pro versus GPT-5.5's 58.6%. Anthropic's reputation as the "safe" lab is now being quietly joined by the "best" label — just as it's heading to public markets.
  4. AI is solving problems humans never could — not just automating them. The Erdős conjecture and cosmic-string physics results are not productivity boosts. They are genuinely new mathematical knowledge. The "AI as tool" frame is giving way to "AI as collaborator and discoverer."
  5. Humanoid robots crossed the economic viability threshold this month. A 70% price drop, lights-out factories running now at major manufacturers, and RT-X controlling any robot for any task. The question is no longer whether humanoid robots are viable — it's how fast labor markets can adapt to the answer.

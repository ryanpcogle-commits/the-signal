Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview Is a Cybersecurity Specialist — and It Went to the White House

Anthropic unveiled Claude Mythos Preview this month under a limited rollout called Project Glasswing, targeting select companies with advanced cybersecurity needs. The model excels at identifying software vulnerabilities and mapping attack chains — and it was the first AI to clear a demanding 32-step end-to-end cyber-attack simulation range. OpenAI's GPT-5.5 cleared the same range three weeks later. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei briefed senior members of the Trump administration on Mythos at the White House, signalling that AI and national cybersecurity have formally converged.

GPT-5.5 Instant Is Now the Default ChatGPT Model

OpenAI swapped out GPT-5.3 Instant on May 5, making GPT-5.5 Instant the default experience for all ChatGPT users. The model scores 81.2 on the AIME 2025 math benchmark — a meaningful jump in reasoning — while retaining the low-latency response profile users expect from the Instant line. In infrastructure news, OpenAI secured the right to multi-source compute across Oracle and CoreWeave in addition to its primary Microsoft Azure partnership, diversifying its capacity ahead of what analysts expect will be a record-demand summer.

Policy

The U.S. Government Will Now Test AI Models Before They Ship

The Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CASI) announced formal agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and xAI that grant the U.S. government the ability to conduct pre-deployment evaluations of frontier AI models before they are publicly released. The Trump administration framed the move as expanding AI oversight without stifling innovation. For labs, it formalises what safety-focused researchers have long argued: independent pre-release testing should be mandatory, not voluntary. The agreements stop short of granting veto power but establish the infrastructure for it.

Infrastructure

SubQ Ships the First Commercial Subquadratic LLM — With 12M Context

SubQ, a stealth architecture startup, became the first company to ship a commercial subquadratic large language model — one that breaks from the quadratic attention bottleneck that has constrained every transformer since 2017. The model supports a 12-million-token context window. Subquadratic architectures scale inference costs far more gently as context grows, which could reshape the economics of long-context workloads like legal document analysis, scientific literature review, and codebase-scale reasoning. May's architecture story may be more significant than any model benchmark.

Models

Google's Gemma 4 Family Challenges Closed Models on Intelligence Per Parameter

Google released the Gemma 4 open-source model family on May 4, engineered specifically for advanced reasoning and agentic workflows. Reviewers noted unusually high "intelligence-per-parameter" — the models punch above their weight class against closed frontier models on multi-step reasoning tasks. With Google expected to deepen Gemma's integration into Android and Chrome at today's I/O keynote, the open-source family is becoming Google's strategic hedge against the API-only model distribution playbook.

Hardware

ZAYA1-8B Proves AMD Can Train Frontier-Class Models End to End

Zyphra released ZAYA1-8B, an open-source Mixture-of-Experts reasoning model with 8 billion total parameters and roughly 760 million active per token. What made the release notable isn't the benchmark numbers — it's that the model was trained entirely on AMD Instinct hardware, end to end, with no NVIDIA GPUs involved. As AI labs seek to reduce dependency on NVIDIA's supply-constrained H100 and B200 series, ZAYA1-8B is one of the clearest demonstrations yet that AMD's AI stack has crossed a production threshold.

Infrastructure

Google's TurboQuant Algorithm Tackles One of Inference's Biggest Bottlenecks

Google's research team unveiled TurboQuant at ICLR 2026, an algorithm that significantly reduces the memory overhead of the KV cache — the component that stores computed attention states during inference and grows with context length. KV cache bloat has long been one of the hardest infrastructure constraints for deploying long-context models at scale. TurboQuant compresses KV cache memory without meaningfully degrading output quality, which could let cloud providers serve the same hardware at substantially higher throughput — or support much longer contexts on existing infrastructure.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI and national security have formally merged. Anthropic briefing the White House on a cybersecurity model. CASI pre-deployment evaluations. Government and frontier labs are no longer operating in separate spheres — every major model release now has a national security dimension.
  2. May is the architecture month, not the scale month. After April's frontier sprint (GPT-5.5, DeepSeek V4, Opus 4.7), May shifted to subquadratic attention, MoE efficiency, and KV cache compression. The next capability leap may come from architecture, not just more compute.
  3. Google is fighting on every front simultaneously. Gemini 3.5, Android 17, Gemma 4, Aluminium OS, Chrome AI, TurboQuant — all announced or expected in a single week. No other lab has this breadth of platform surface area. If Google executes, I/O 2026 could be its most consequential developer conference in a decade.
  4. AMD is becoming a credible alternative training platform. ZAYA1-8B trained end to end on AMD Instinct is not an outlier experiment — it's a proof of concept that will accelerate labs' negotiating leverage against NVIDIA on pricing and supply.
  5. Pre-deployment evaluation is the new regulatory battleground. The CASI agreements are voluntary today. They will be mandatory in two years. Labs that build relationships with government evaluators now will shape what those standards look like. The ones that don't will have standards imposed on them.

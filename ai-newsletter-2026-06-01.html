Lead Story

Google Goes Fast: Gemini 3.5 Flash Launches at I/O 2026 as AI Mode Crosses 1 Billion Users

Rather than announce a bigger model, Google opened its biggest developer conference of the year with a cheaper, faster one — and the strategy appears to be working at a scale no competitor can match.

At Google I/O 2026, the company launched Gemini 3.5 Flash — the first model in the new Gemini 3.5 family — as its general availability headliner. The model delivers output tokens 4x faster than comparable frontier models and is optimized for complex agentic workflows and coding tasks. It is now the default model powering AI Mode in Google Search, which crossed 1 billion monthly users today. AI Overviews, the broader AI-in-Search layer, now reaches 2.5 billion users monthly.

The pricing moves were equally notable. Google cut the Gemini Ultra subscription from $250 to $200 per month and introduced a new Developer tier at $100 per month aimed at engineers and professional users. Monthly active Gemini users have now hit 900 million, doubling from 400 million in May 2025 — the fastest consumer AI growth ever recorded.

The strategic message was unmistakable. Executives at Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic increasingly describe the frontier race as neck-and-neck — with companies making different tradeoffs around cost, speed, and compute. Google's answer is to prioritize models cheap and fast enough to deploy across products used by billions, rather than chasing the largest possible parameter count.

Models

Claude Opus 4.8 Scores 88.6% on SWE-bench — With a 2.5× Speed Mode

Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 has posted benchmark results that solidify its position at the top of the coding leaderboard: 88.6% on SWE-bench Verified, 74.6% on Terminal-Bench 2.1, and an Elo of 1890 on GDPval-AA — placing it at or above human-expert level on economically valuable software tasks. The model ships with parallel-subagent workflows that enable multi-step agentic work at scale, and a 2.5× fast mode that delivers the same quality at significantly lower latency. Pricing remains unchanged at $5 input / $25 output per million tokens.

Business

DeepMind Absorbs Contextual AI's Research Team in $80–90M Talent Deal

Google DeepMind has hired more than 20 researchers from Contextual AI — including co-founder and CEO Douwe Kiela — under an $80 to $90 million licensing arrangement. The deal follows a pattern of frontier labs acquiring talent through structured IP-and-people transactions rather than traditional acquisitions, allowing DeepMind to absorb a specialized team focused on retrieval-augmented generation and enterprise AI without the regulatory overhead of a full merger. Contextual AI had raised over $100 million before the deal.

Models

DeepSeek Releases V4-Pro and V4-Flash With 1-Million-Token Context Windows

Beijing-based DeepSeek shipped two new Mixture-of-Experts models this week: DeepSeek-V4-Pro and DeepSeek-V4-Flash. Both feature a 1-million-token context window and are optimized for long-running agentic tasks that require reasoning across very large codebases or document sets. The releases extend DeepSeek's streak of releasing frontier-quality open models at a fraction of Western labs' reported training costs, and keep international pressure on Anthropic and OpenAI to match long-context performance.

Infrastructure

GitHub Copilot Switches to Metered "AI Credits" Billing — Starting Today

Effective June 1, 2026, GitHub has transitioned Copilot from request-based billing to usage-based metered billing powered by a new virtual currency called GitHub AI Credits. The shift means enterprise customers will pay based on actual model consumption rather than per-seat subscriptions — aligning Copilot's economics with the cost structure of the models underneath it. The change reflects an industry-wide move toward consumption pricing as AI assistants handle increasingly variable workloads across coding, testing, and documentation tasks.

Models

OpenAI Sets Sunset Dates: GPT-4.5 Retires June 27, o3 Retires August 26

OpenAI confirmed that GPT-4.5 will be retired from ChatGPT on June 27, 2026 following a 30-day sunset period, and o3 will follow on August 26 after a 90-day wind-down. The moves accelerate OpenAI's strategy of consolidating its product lineup around newer architectures. Meanwhile, GPT-5.4 "Thinking" — the current flagship — scored 83.0% on the GDPVal benchmark, a level OpenAI describes as matching or exceeding human experts on economically meaningful tasks.

Robotics

COMPUTEX 2026 Opens in Taipei: Physical AI Gets Its Biggest Stage Yet

COMPUTEX 2026 opens today in Taipei with 1,500 exhibitors from 33 countries across more than 6,000 booths — and a brand-new AI Robotics Zone as its centerpiece. NVIDIA's GTC Taipei runs alongside the show, spotlighting Isaac GR00T open models (robots that follow natural language instructions across complex multi-step tasks), Cosmos world models for synthetic training data, and Newton 1.0 — a new foundation for dexterous robot manipulation. Cadence Design Systems and NVIDIA also announced an expanded partnership combining Cadence's simulation engines with NVIDIA's Isaac and Cosmos models to close the "sim-to-real" gap.

Business

AI 3D Startup Vast Raises $200M at Over $1B Valuation

Vast, a Beijing-based company that generates 3D assets from text and image prompts, raised approximately $200 million at a valuation above $1 billion. The company has 20 million global users and competes in a fast-moving segment of generative AI aimed at game studios, architects, and product designers. The raise signals continued investor appetite for vertical AI applications — particularly those that automate creative workflows that previously required specialized human labor. Vast is among a wave of Chinese AI startups posting explosive user growth while remaining largely under the radar of Western press.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Speed and cost are beating raw scale. Google's I/O play was Gemini 3.5 Flash — not Gemini 3.5 Ultra. Anthropic's Opus 4.8 ships with a 2.5× fast mode. The market is rewarding deployment economics over benchmark maximalism.
  2. Agentic infrastructure is maturing fast. Metered billing for Copilot. 1M-token contexts from DeepSeek. Parallel subagent workflows from Anthropic. The plumbing for multi-step AI agents is quietly becoming enterprise-grade.
  3. Talent deals are the new acquisitions. DeepMind's Contextual AI deal — paying $80–90M for 20 researchers via licensing — mirrors the Inflection and Character.AI playbooks. Expect more regulatory-light talent absorption in 2026.
  4. Physical AI is leaving the lab. COMPUTEX's AI Robotics Zone, NVIDIA Newton 1.0, and the Cadence sim-to-real partnership all point to 2026 as the year physical AI transitions from research to production hardware.
  5. Model consolidation is accelerating. GPT-4.5 and o3 are being retired within months of launch. The half-life of a "frontier" model is shrinking — labs are learning that maintaining too many model lines is operationally unsustainable.

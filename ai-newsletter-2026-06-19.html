Lead Story

AI's CEOs Storm the G7 — and Demand the West Lead Before It's Too Late

Amodei, Hassabis, and Altman appeared together at the G7 summit to call for a U.S.-led AI coalition. It's the most coordinated geopolitical move the industry has ever made.

In a closed-door session at the G7 summit on June 17, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis made a joint call for a U.S.-led international coalition to establish AI governance standards — with around a dozen technology executives, including OpenAI's Sam Altman, joining heads of state at the table. The message was explicit: the window to shape the global AI order is open now, and closing fast.

The timing of the G7 push is not accidental. Just two weeks earlier, on June 5, the same companies' CEOs had co-signed an open letter to Congress warning that AI is actively eroding the technical barriers to bioweapon development — and calling for mandatory synthetic DNA screening requirements. The two moves together suggest a new phase of AI industry politics: the labs are no longer waiting to be regulated. They're writing the legislation.

Whether this unprecedented alignment translates into actual policy — or dissolves into competing national interests and lobbying — remains the defining question of the summer. But the optics of Amodei, Hassabis, and Altman in the same room with heads of state signals that AI has fully crossed from a technology story into a foreign policy story.

June's Model Wave Is Already the Densest Yet

Four major model families are converging in the same four-week window: Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro, Anthropic's Claude Mythos 1 (alongside a rumored Claude Sonnet 4.8), and the long-delayed Grok 5 from xAI. OpenAI separately upgraded healthcare capabilities with GPT-5.5 Instant. The competitive window is now measured in weeks, not quarters.

Policy

AI Labs Ask Congress to Block Bioweapon DNA Orders Before AI Makes It Easy

The CEOs of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft signed a joint open letter to Congress on June 5, calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. Their core argument: AI has already lowered the expertise threshold for weaponizing biological material to the point where technical barriers alone can no longer be trusted. The letter is notable both for its urgency and for the fact that it represents four companies — normally fierce competitors — presenting a unified front on existential risk.

Models

MiniMax M3 Cuts Compute to 1/20th — and Sets a New Efficiency Benchmark

MiniMax's new M3 model slashes per-token compute requirements to just 1/20th of previous-generation models while supporting up to 1 million tokens. The architecture delivers 9x faster prefilling and 15x faster decoding in benchmarks — meaning dramatically lower inference costs and latency. If those numbers hold at scale, M3 represents a significant efficiency inflection point that could force cost recalculations across every major AI deployment. Training efficiency has also broken new records: the Orion-100B project trained a 100-billion-parameter model for just $1.25 per hour.

Infrastructure

NAVER and NVIDIA Are Building a Sovereign AI at Gigawatt Scale

NAVER announced it will expand its sovereign AI infrastructure using the NVIDIA DSX platform, starting at 55 megawatts at its GAK Sejong data center in South Korea — with a roadmap to gigawatt capacity. The partnership will underpin NAVER's next-generation HyperCLOVA X models, a Seoul World Model trained on urban spatial data, and an AI Agent Platform launching domestically in the second half of 2026. South Korea joins Japan, the UAE, and France in the growing roster of nations treating domestic AI infrastructure as a matter of national sovereignty, not just commercial strategy.

Robotics

Spot Gets a Brain Upgrade: Boston Dynamics Integrates Gemini Robotics

Boston Dynamics partnered with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration gives Spot multimodal reasoning capabilities — allowing it to interpret natural language instructions, identify anomalies in visual inspection tasks, and adapt to novel environments without reprogramming. The move accelerates the convergence of foundation models and physical robotics from research milestone into deployed product.

Business

Anthropic Launches Claude Corps — A National Fellowship for AI in the Public Interest

Anthropic announced Claude Corps, a national fellowship program aimed at early-career Americans who want to deploy AI for civic and community benefit. The program is paired with a new Memorandum of Understanding signed with South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT alongside the opening of Anthropic's Seoul office — its latest international expansion. Both moves reflect Anthropic's push to frame itself as a public-interest AI institution, not merely a product company, as regulatory pressure from multiple governments intensifies.

Infrastructure

AI Is Accelerating Science Itself — Magnets, Climate Models, and Beyond

Two research breakthroughs this week illustrate AI's growing role as a scientific tool. At Ames Laboratory, an AI workflow using a physics-trained model called DuctGPT discovered rare-earth-free permanent magnets by reasoning from first principles rather than pattern-matching known materials. Separately, researchers demonstrated climate simulation models running approximately 25 times faster by combining generative methods with physics-based data — compressing months of computation into days. Both breakthroughs point toward a world where AI dramatically accelerates the pace of empirical discovery, not just information synthesis.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI governance has entered foreign policy. CEOs lobbying Congress is now table stakes. Sitting with heads of state at the G7 to shape international AI standards is a different category of political engagement — one that signals the labs believe regulatory capture at the national level is no longer enough.
  2. The efficiency race is as important as the capability race. MiniMax M3 at 1/20th compute, Orion-100B at $1.25/hr, climate models running 25x faster. The labs optimizing for cost and speed — not just benchmark scores — may end up owning the deployment layer where the actual revenue is.
  3. Sovereign AI is becoming a geopolitical imperative. South Korea's NAVER deal isn't an outlier. Nations are increasingly treating domestic AI infrastructure as a security asset, creating a multi-polar AI landscape where U.S. hyperscalers don't automatically win every market.
  4. Foundation models are flowing into physical systems. Boston Dynamics + DeepMind is one data point. Atlas, Spot with Gemini Robotics, NVIDIA's physical AI chips — the pipeline from language model to deployed robot is no longer experimental. It's shipping.
  5. AI is being recruited to defend against AI-enabled threats. The synthetic DNA screening letter is the clearest example yet of labs acknowledging that their own technology creates new attack surfaces. Expect biosecurity, cyber, and infrastructure protection to become recurring AI policy themes through the rest of 2026.

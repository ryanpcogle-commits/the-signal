Lead Story

Nobel Laureate John Jumper Is Leaving DeepMind for Anthropic

The co-creator of AlphaFold — one of the most consequential scientific AI systems ever built — is joining Anthropic in the most significant talent defection the AI industry has seen this year.

John Jumper, the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry laureate and the researcher most responsible for AlphaFold's revolutionary protein-structure prediction capabilities, announced on June 19, 2026 that he is leaving Google DeepMind after nearly nine years. His destination: Anthropic. The move was confirmed by Bloomberg and immediately sent shockwaves through the research community.

Jumper's work on AlphaFold effectively solved a 50-year grand challenge in biology. His departure deepens an already intense talent war between the frontier labs. For DeepMind — the team that pioneered AlphaFold and counts scientific breakthroughs as a core part of its identity — losing Jumper to Anthropic is both a personal and strategic blow. For Anthropic, landing a Nobel laureate signals growing ambition in scientific AI, a domain that has largely been DeepMind's territory.

The timing is notable. Anthropic is believed to be readying its next flagship model cluster — including what researchers are calling "Claude Mythos 1" — and a world-class scientific mind could shape how the company approaches safety research, evaluations, and biology-adjacent AI applications.

Policy

Amodei and Hassabis Pushed for a U.S.-Led AI Coalition at the G7

At a closed-door G7 meeting on June 17, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis jointly called on heads of state to form a U.S.-led AI coalition. OpenAI's Sam Altman was also in the room, alongside roughly a dozen other tech executives. The core message: AI compute is now national infrastructure, and democratic nations need to coordinate on access, security, and trust before authoritarian competitors set the terms. U.S.-Japan and UK-Canada bilateral AI deals already in motion were cited as early building blocks.

Policy

AI Lab CEOs Warn Congress: Synthetic DNA Is Now a Bioweapon Risk

The heads of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI sent a joint open letter to the U.S. Congress in early June calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. The letter warns that AI has dramatically lowered the expertise needed to misuse biological materials — synthetic DNA and RNA can currently be ordered online, and AI is making the knowledge barrier to weaponize them nearly negligible. It is the first time the four major frontier labs have united behind a single regulatory request.

Models

OpenAI Launches Rosalind Biodefense — GPT-Rosalind Goes Live for Public Health

On June 1, OpenAI officially launched Rosalind Biodefense, expanding trusted access to its GPT-Rosalind model specifically for biodefense, public health, and pandemic preparedness organizations. The initiative gives vetted government agencies and research institutions accelerated API access with higher rate limits and dedicated support. The move positions OpenAI in direct competition with DeepMind on scientific AI applications — and arrives amid the industry-wide conversation about AI's dual-use risk in biology.

Models

Four Frontier Models Are Landing This Month — Including "Claude Mythos 1"

June is shaping up to be the most competitive model-release month of 2026. Reported launches include Gemini 3.5 Pro from Google, Claude Mythos 1 from Anthropic (a next-generation flagship positioned above Opus 4.8), a rumored Claude Sonnet 4.8 update, and Grok 5 from xAI. Google's Gemini Omni Flash — combining multimodal video generation with conversational editing for YouTube Shorts and Gemini — has already landed, bringing real-time AI video creation to mainstream consumer products for the first time.

Infrastructure

MiniMax M3 Slashes Per-Token Compute to 1/20th — Efficiency Is the New Arms Race

MiniMax's new M3 model has achieved per-token compute requirements just 1/20th of comparable previous-generation models, dramatically reducing the cost of running frontier-class multimodal AI. Meanwhile, a startup called Orion trained a 100-billion-parameter model for a jaw-dropping $1.25 per hour — a benchmark that would have seemed implausible twelve months ago. The pattern is clear: inference and training costs are collapsing faster than the compute expansion can keep up, making AI economics look radically different by end of year.

Hardware

NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Targets Enterprise AI Safety at Global Scale

NVIDIA unveiled Nemotron 3.5 Content Safety, a customizable multimodal safety model built specifically for global enterprise AI deployments. Unlike general-purpose safety guardrails bolted onto existing models, Nemotron 3.5 is designed from the ground up to be fine-tuned per industry and jurisdiction — addressing a real gap for regulated sectors like finance, healthcare, and government. The release arrives alongside Intel's Xeon 6+ processors and NVIDIA Cosmos 3, both targeting faster and more cost-efficient AI inference at data-center scale.

Models

JetBrains Ships Mellum2 — A 12B MoE Model Built Only for Developers

JetBrains released Mellum2, a 12-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model tuned exclusively for software development tasks. Unlike general-purpose coding assistants, Mellum2 is optimized for IDE-native workflows — code completion, refactoring suggestions, test generation, and bug explanation within the development environment itself. The release underscores a growing trend: specialist models trained on narrower domains are outperforming their much larger general-purpose counterparts for professional workloads.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Talent is the real frontier war now. John Jumper leaving DeepMind for Anthropic — a Nobel laureate changing teams — signals that the labs competing on model quality are also competing at the level of the world's best individual researchers. Expect more marquee moves.
  2. Biosecurity is becoming AI's next major battleground. Four major AI labs uniting to call for synthetic DNA screening is unprecedented. The intersection of AI and biological risk is moving from theoretical concern to active regulatory and product priority for every frontier lab.
  3. Compute efficiency is collapsing — faster than anyone expected. Training 100B parameters for $1.25/hr and cutting per-token inference to 1/20th of previous costs fundamentally changes who can build and who can afford to run AI. The moat is shifting from compute access to data quality and distribution.
  4. AI is becoming geopolitical infrastructure, not just commercial software. G7 coalitions, bilateral compute deals, and joint CEO-to-Congress letters signal that AI is being treated the same way as semiconductors and energy — a strategic asset nations are actively competing to control and protect.
  5. Vertical specialization is beating scale for professional use cases. Mellum2 for developers. Nemotron 3.5 for enterprise safety. Rosalind for biodefense. The era of one giant model doing everything is giving way to specialist models that dominate narrow domains at lower cost.

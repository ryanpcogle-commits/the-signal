Lead Story

GPT-5.5 Just Crossed the Human Baseline — AI Is Now the Coworker

OpenAI's newest model scored 75% on OSWorld-V, a benchmark for autonomous desktop productivity tasks — edging past the 72.4% human baseline and signaling a fundamental shift in what AI is actually for.

OpenAI rolled out GPT-5.5 to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers on April 23rd, describing it as optimized for "agentic coding, computer use, knowledge work, and scientific research." Its 75% score on OSWorld-V is the number that matters most: the human baseline is 72.4%, meaning GPT-5.5 can now outperform a typical knowledge worker on desktop navigation and task execution.

On May 5th, OpenAI followed with GPT-5.5 Instant, replacing GPT-5.3 Instant as the default model in ChatGPT. It brings a 1-million-token context window and the ability to autonomously execute multi-step workflows across software environments — converting AI from a chat tool into something closer to a digital employee.

The timing is significant. As GPT-5.5 Instant becomes the default experience for hundreds of millions of ChatGPT users, the industry's narrative shifts from "AI that assists" to "AI that acts." Every major lab is now racing to cross that same productivity threshold.

This Week in AI
Models

Anthropic Unveils Claude Mythos Preview Under a Cybersecurity Veil

Anthropic released Claude Mythos Preview — a model purpose-built to identify weaknesses and security flaws in software. Rather than a broad public launch, Anthropic is limiting access to a select group of companies under Project Glasswing, a new cybersecurity initiative. The controlled rollout comes days after the White House reopened talks with Anthropic following the company's exclusion from Pentagon AI deals — and suggests Anthropic is betting its government future on security, not weapons.

Business

Anthropic Commits $200 Billion to Google — One of Tech's Largest Cloud Deals

Anthropic has agreed to pay Google $200 billion over five years for cloud infrastructure and chip access, in one of the largest cloud commitments in tech history. The deal cements the two companies' partnership — Google has been Anthropic's primary cloud and chip provider since 2023 — and gives Anthropic priority access to Google's TPU capacity as it scales frontier model training. Separately, Anthropic is closing a $1.5 billion round from Blackstone, Goldman Sachs, and Hellman & Friedman.

Policy

Pentagon Signs AI Deals With Eight Companies — Anthropic Is Not One of Them

The Department of Defense finalized AI agreements with eight companies including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Nvidia, leaving Anthropic excluded. The Trump administration also announced plans to stress-test models from Google DeepMind, Microsoft, and Elon Musk's xAI through the Center for AI Standards and Innovation. Meanwhile, the White House is developing comprehensive new AI regulations aimed at ensuring safety, accountability, and fairness — framing government oversight as the new frontier of AI competition.

Models

Chinese Open-Weights Models Close the Gap in a 12-Day Flurry

Three open-weights Chinese models — Kimi K2.6, MiniMax M2.7, and Z.ai GLM-5.1 — landed within 12 days of each other, all scoring 56–59 on SWE-Bench Pro, firmly in Western frontier territory. Zhipu AI's GLM-4.7 made an even bolder claim: trained entirely on Huawei Ascend silicon, it reports a 1.2% hallucination rate — the lowest reported by any frontier lab — at a cost of just $0.11 per million input tokens, compared to $15 for Claude Opus.

Business

Google DeepMind UK Workers Vote to Unionize Over Pentagon Contract

Workers at Google DeepMind's UK offices voted to unionize, citing opposition to the company's involvement in Pentagon AI contracts and a dropped pledge not to work on weapons systems. It marks the first major AI lab workforce unionization in Europe, and signals that ethical concerns about military AI have moved from public debate into organized labor action. DeepMind's partnership with Iceland's Fenris Creations — training AI on the social and economic systems of the space RPG Eve Online — offered a notable contrast in the same week.

Infrastructure

Gemini 3.1 Ultra Ships With a 2-Million-Token Context Window

Google released Gemini 3.1 Ultra with a 2-million-token context window — double the previous generation — and native multimodal understanding across text, image, audio, and video in a single model. The release puts Google back in the context-window race after OpenAI and Anthropic both crossed 1 million tokens earlier this year. Separately, Google introduced Gemma 4, a new open-model series built specifically for advanced reasoning and agentic workflows.

Business

Generative AI Is Now Worth $172 Billion a Year to U.S. Consumers

The Stanford AI Index 2026 report estimates the value of generative AI tools to U.S. consumers has reached $172 billion annually — with the median value per user tripling between 2025 and 2026. The data underscores how quickly AI has shifted from a productivity novelty to genuine economic infrastructure. Pakistan's adoption of the Islamabad AI Declaration this week, pledging a $1 billion national AI investment by 2030, points to the same trend accelerating globally.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The human baseline is no longer a safe ceiling. GPT-5.5's 75% on OSWorld-V crossing the 72.4% human score means AI can now outperform average workers on structured desktop tasks. This benchmark will be cited for years as the moment AI became the coworker.
  2. Anthropic is doubling down on Google and cybersecurity. A $200B cloud commitment plus a security-first model launch (Mythos) plus a White House re-engagement — Anthropic is betting that being the responsible, security-focused lab is a better path to government than competing for weapons contracts.
  3. Military AI is now a labor issue. DeepMind UK workers unionizing over Pentagon contracts means employee pressure on AI ethics is becoming institutionalized. Expect more labs to face the same internal reckoning as defense spending on AI accelerates.
  4. Chinese open-weights models are erasing the cost-performance gap. GLM-4.7 at $0.11/M tokens vs. $15 for Claude Opus is not a rounding error — it is a structural challenge to Western labs' pricing power and a preview of where commodity AI is heading.
  5. Context windows keep expanding as the default competitive moat. Gemini 3.1 Ultra's 2M tokens follows OpenAI's 1M and Anthropic's 1M. The question shifts from "how long?" to "can the model actually reason across all of it?" — the next benchmark war will be won on retrieval quality, not window size.

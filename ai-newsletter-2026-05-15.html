Lead Story

Google Bets $40 Billion on Anthropic in the Largest AI Investment in History

Alphabet's commitment to Anthropic dwarfs every prior AI funding round and rewrites the power map of the industry — even as the Pentagon shuts Anthropic out of its own AI deals.

Alphabet announced it intends to invest up to $40 billion in Anthropic, a figure so large it redefines what "strategic investment" means in AI. The deal makes Google Anthropic's largest outside backer and cements a partnership that spans cloud compute, model access, and safety research — at a moment when Anthropic's Claude suite is outcompeting rivals on coding and reasoning benchmarks.

The timing is striking. Just weeks ago, the Department of Defense finalized AI agreements with eight companies — OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI — while pointedly excluding Anthropic. The Pentagon cited concerns about Anthropic's refusal to allow its models to be used in certain autonomous weapons contexts. The DOD's snub made Anthropic look isolated in the government market.

Google's $40 billion answer makes the Pentagon's calculus look shortsighted. The investment signals that Anthropic's safety-first positioning — once perceived as a competitive liability — may be exactly the differentiation that private-sector buyers, and now the world's largest tech company, are willing to pay an extraordinary premium for.

Models

Anthropic's "Mythos Preview" Is a Security AI That Won't Ship to Everyone

Anthropic quietly announced Claude Mythos Preview, a model purpose-built for cybersecurity applications — specifically excelling at identifying software vulnerabilities and tracing attack surfaces across complex codebases. Rather than a broad release, Anthropic is limiting access to a select group of vetted organizations under an initiative called Project Glasswing. The controlled rollout reflects the dual-use tension inherent in a model that is, by design, extremely good at finding things to exploit.

Models

GPT-5.5 Instant Is Now the Default ChatGPT Model — OpenAI Also Launches Deployment Company

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Instant — a faster, lower-cost variant of April's GPT-5.5 — replaced GPT-5.3 Instant as the default model powering ChatGPT on May 5. The full GPT-5.5 remains available to Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise subscribers and via API. Separately, on May 11, OpenAI launched the OpenAI Deployment Company, a new arm designed to help enterprises build production workflows around OpenAI intelligence — an unmistakable signal that the company is moving from model vendor to full-stack AI infrastructure provider.

Policy

Pentagon Signs Eight AI Deals — and Anthropic Isn't One of Them

The Department of Defense finalized AI partnerships with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI, expanding AI use into classified military environments for analysis, logistics, and large-scale data processing. Anthropic's exclusion follows earlier DOD accusations that the company posed a "supply-chain risk" after it declined to permit certain autonomous-weapons use cases. The list of eight named partners is notable for the presence of Reflection AI — a relatively young startup — and the absence of the company currently receiving $40 billion from Google.

Business

Amazon Kills Rufus, Replaces It With Alexa for Shopping — and It Buys Across the Web

Amazon launched Alexa for Shopping, an AI assistant powered by the upgraded Alexa+ platform that replaces Rufus across mobile, desktop, and Echo Show devices. The new assistant compares products, tracks prices, schedules recurring orders — and includes a Buy for Me feature that can purchase items on the user's behalf from retailers outside Amazon entirely. The last capability is the most consequential: Amazon has built a shopping agent that actively routes purchases off its own platform, betting that owning the interface matters more than owning the marketplace.

Infrastructure

Google's TurboQuant Compresses AI Context Windows — As 58.5% of Searches End Without a Click

Google published details on TurboQuant, an algorithm that significantly reduces the memory overhead of the KV cache used by large-context models — a critical bottleneck as context windows stretch toward millions of tokens. The technique uses vector rotation and compression to let models handle massive inputs without proportional memory cost. Meanwhile, Google's AI Overviews now appear in roughly 20% of all searches, and 58.5% of Google searches in 2026 end without the user clicking any result. The web's traffic model is being structurally dismantled.

Policy

OpenAI, Anthropic, and DeepMind Researchers Co-Author "Positive Alignment" Framework

Researchers from Google DeepMind, OpenAI, Anthropic, and several universities jointly published a proposal for what they call a "positive alignment" framework — arguing that AI safety research has focused too narrowly on preventing catastrophic harm, and should instead actively train models to promote human flourishing. The cross-company authorship is nearly unprecedented. That rivals who compete intensely on benchmarks and funding are collaborating on foundational safety norms suggests the field is developing something like a shared professional conscience — even as governments struggle to keep pace.

Agentic AI Hits Critical Mass: 72% of Enterprises Are Already Running Agents in Production

A new industry survey finds that 72% of enterprises are either in production with or actively piloting agentic AI, with 65% already using AI agents in live business workflows. Separately, 70% of marketing chiefs named AI leadership their top 2026 priority — though only 30% believe they have the infrastructure to execute. The gap between ambition and readiness is compressing fast: agentic platforms that looked experimental 18 months ago are now the operational baseline for a majority of large organizations.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Safety-first positioning is now a $40B asset. Google's investment in Anthropic reframes the conventional wisdom that ethical guardrails are a competitive handicap. In the private market at least, principled constraints are now worth a premium that no benchmark score can replicate.
  2. The interface is becoming more valuable than the marketplace. Amazon's Buy for Me feature — routing purchases off Amazon to other retailers — is a bet that controlling the AI shopping agent beats controlling the storefront. Every platform company is facing the same logic.
  3. Clickless search is restructuring the web's economics. At 58.5% of searches ending without a click, Google's AI Overviews are not just changing user behavior — they are eliminating the referral traffic that the open web's business model depends on. The downstream effects on media, SEO, and e-commerce will compound for years.
  4. Security AI is the next dual-use frontier. Anthropic's limited-access release of Claude Mythos Preview under Project Glasswing signals that offense-capable AI models are already here — and that labs are making their own access decisions rather than waiting for regulation. Expect more of this kind of tiered, gated release.
  5. Cross-lab collaboration on safety norms is quietly normalizing. The positive alignment paper's multi-lab authorship would have been unthinkable two years ago. That competitors are converging on shared ethical frameworks — outside any regulatory mandate — suggests the industry may be developing voluntary guardrails faster than governments can mandate them.

Anthropic Raises Another $30 Billion — Valuation Approaches $900 Billion

In its second mega-round of 2026, Anthropic has closed a $30 billion Growth raise led by Sequoia Capital, Dragoneer, Altimeter, and Greenoaks — pushing its valuation to within striking distance of a trillion dollars.

The round is extraordinary by any measure, but made more so by its timing: Anthropic has now raised $60 billion in 2026 alone. The company, founded in 2021 by former OpenAI researchers including Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei, is now valued at a figure that would place it among the most valuable private companies ever to exist — and well ahead of its OpenAI rival in per-employee valuation.

The capital signals where the smart money believes AI is heading: large, safety-focused labs building frontier models with enterprise deployment at scale. Anthropic's Claude is already running inside Goldman Sachs to automate accounting and compliance workflows, and KPMG has deployed it to all 276,000 of its employees across 138 countries — the single largest enterprise Claude deployment in history.

Separately, Anthropic announced a major compute partnership with SpaceX and xAI around the Colossus 1 supercluster — securing training infrastructure at a scale that previously only OpenAI and Google could access. The combination of capital and compute suggests Anthropic's next frontier model will be its most ambitious yet.

Business

OpenAI Launches $4 Billion Consulting Arm — DeployCo

OpenAI has created a dedicated consulting subsidiary called DeployCo, capitalized at $4 billion, to help enterprises integrate AI agents into their existing systems. The unit offers shared-context architectures, white-glove migration support, and integration into legacy software stacks — areas where most AI vendors have left companies stranded. The launch mirrors a playbook pioneered by IBM and Accenture in earlier technology waves and signals that OpenAI sees professional services as its next major revenue stream alongside API access.

Models

Gemini 3.5 Flash Is Now Generally Available — Frontier Speed at Commodity Price

Google DeepMind's Gemini 3.5 Flash is now generally available, delivering what the company calls "frontier-level intelligence at four times the speed of comparable models" — at $1.50 per million input tokens and $9 per million output tokens with a one-million-token context window. Google also cut its Gemini Ultra subscription from $250 to $200 per month. The Flash launch comes days after Google I/O 2026, where the company made more than 100 AI announcements and introduced the Waymo World Model — a simulation engine powered by Genie 3 that generates hyper-realistic 2D and 3D environments for autonomous vehicle training.

Cohere Acquires Aleph Alpha — A $20 Billion Sovereign AI Challenger Emerges

Canadian AI company Cohere has acquired Germany's Aleph Alpha, creating a transatlantic sovereign AI platform valued at $20 billion. The deal is explicitly aimed at governments and enterprises that require AI infrastructure outside of US hyperscaler control — a market that has grown sharply following concerns about data sovereignty in Europe and the Asia-Pacific region. Aleph Alpha had positioned itself as the EU's most credible frontier AI lab; combined with Cohere's enterprise customer base, the merged entity could become the dominant alternative to OpenAI and Anthropic for regulated industries.

Policy

Canada Rules OpenAI Violated Privacy Law — China Restricts AI Researcher Travel

Canada's Office of the Privacy Commissioner ruled that OpenAI violated the country's privacy legislation, marking the first major regulatory finding against the company in North America. The ruling centers on how user data was collected, retained, and used for model training without adequate consent. Meanwhile, China has begun imposing overseas travel restrictions on individuals working in advanced AI at private firms, including researchers at DeepSeek and Alibaba — a move interpreted as Beijing attempting to prevent talent and intellectual property from flowing to Western competitors.

Infrastructure

SubQ Ships the First Commercial Subquadratic LLM — With a 12M-Token Context

Startup SubQ has released what it claims is the first commercially deployed large language model built on a subquadratic architecture — meaning attention costs do not scale quadratically with context length the way they do in standard transformers. The result is a model with a 12-million-token context window that runs at a fraction of the inference cost of equivalently capable transformer models. Separately, Zyphra released an 8-billion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts model trained entirely on AMD hardware — a meaningful signal that the industry's NVIDIA-hardware monoculture may be starting to crack.

Policy

The Pope Writes an AI Encyclical — 42,300 Words on Humanity and Machine Intelligence

Pope Leo XIV has released Magnifica Humanitas, a 42,300-word papal encyclical — the first in history devoted to artificial intelligence. The document addresses AI's impact on labor, human dignity, the nature of creativity, and the moral responsibilities of developers and governments. It stops short of condemning AI outright but calls for binding international frameworks and argues that systems capable of autonomous decision-making over human lives require "explicit ethical governance." The encyclical has been welcomed by AI ethicists and criticized by some in the industry as technologically uninformed.

AI Overviews Now Appear in 1-in-5 Google Searches — Zero-Click Era Is Here

New data shows Google's AI Overviews appear in approximately 20% of all searches, with 58.5% of Google queries in 2026 now ending without a user clicking any result — as the AI-generated answer resolves the question directly on the results page. For publishers and content businesses, the numbers are alarming: organic search referral traffic is down sharply across news, reference, and how-to verticals. Google frames the shift as improving user experience; critics argue the company is simultaneously destroying the web ecosystem it built and monetizing the same content without compensation.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Enterprise deployment is the new arms race. KPMG's 276,000-employee Claude rollout, OpenAI's $4B DeployCo launch, and Anthropic's Goldman Sachs deal all signal the same thing: the labs that win enterprise deployment at scale will define the AI market for the next decade. The research phase is ending; the deployment phase is here.
  2. Sovereign AI is becoming a real market. The Cohere–Aleph Alpha merger creates a credible $20B alternative to US hyperscaler AI. As data sovereignty concerns multiply — especially in Europe — governments and regulated industries will pay a premium for AI infrastructure they control.
  3. The zero-click web is no longer theoretical. When 58.5% of searches end without a click, the traditional web traffic model has collapsed. Content businesses face an existential reckoning; the companies that build destination products rather than information sources will survive.
  4. Regulatory pressure is arriving simultaneously from every direction. Canada ruling on OpenAI, China restricting researcher movement, the Pope publishing AI doctrine, and governments globally testing models pre-release — the era of self-regulation for AI is ending. Policy environments will increasingly shape what these models can do and where.
  5. Architecture innovation is back. Subquadratic LLMs with 12M-token contexts, AMD-trained MoE models, and hybrid light-matter computing research all signal that the transformer monoculture is being challenged at the hardware and architecture layer simultaneously. The next performance leap may not come from scale — it may come from fundamentally different designs.

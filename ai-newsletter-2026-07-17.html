Lead Story

Gemini 3.5 Pro Drops With a 2M-Token Window — As 29 Nations Form a Rival AI Bloc in Shanghai

Google's most ambitious model yet launches today, rebuilt from scratch with double the context of anything on the frontier. Meanwhile, Xi Jinping showed up in person to sign an international AI treaty — making clear this is no longer just a tech story.

Google released Gemini 3.5 Pro today, and it's a clean-sheet redesign: rather than adapting the 2.5 Pro architecture, the team ran an entirely new pretraining run. The headline number is a 2-million-token context window — double anything currently available at the frontier — which enables ingesting entire codebases, legal archives, or multi-year document trails in a single pass. Deep Think extended reasoning is gated behind a $250/month Ultra subscription, while the model's standard tier prices at approximately $1.25 input and $10 output per million tokens.

The launch lands six weeks late and arrives nine days after Grok 4.5 and one week after GPT-5.6 hit general availability — placing Google in the unusual position of chasing rather than setting the pace. Still, the 2M context window is a genuine differentiator; no competitor is within striking distance on that dimension today.

Overseas, the geopolitical dimension of AI reached a new register. President Xi Jinping attended the 2026 World Artificial Intelligence Conference in Shanghai in person — his first appearance at the event since it launched in 2018 — signaling that Beijing now treats AI leadership as a top-tier national priority. On the conference sidelines, 29 nations including Russia, Brazil, Indonesia, Pakistan, and Cuba signed an agreement establishing the World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization (WAICO). Paired with ByteDance's Seedream model gaining traction on Wall Street, the through-line of mid-2026 is hard to miss: AI has become a genuine two-superpower race.

GPT-5.6 Is Now ChatGPT's Default — Three Tiers, One Lineup

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 became ChatGPT's default model on July 9 after a two-week gated preview. The release ships as three distinct tiers: Sol, the flagship, targeting advanced coding, biology, and cybersecurity tasks at $5.00 input and $30.00 output per million tokens; Terra, aimed at GPT-5.5-level quality at roughly half the cost; and Luna, a fast, high-volume tier for lower-stakes workloads. An Ultra subagent mode and a Max reasoning-effort setting round out Sol's capabilities. The structured lineup reflects OpenAI's bid to cover the enterprise cost spectrum without fragmenting its brand.

Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 Is the First Open Model Near Three Trillion Parameters

Moonshot AI released Kimi K3 on July 16, and the numbers are staggering: 2.8 trillion total parameters in a Mixture-of-Experts architecture built on Kimi Delta Attention and Attention Residuals, with 16 of 896 experts activated per token. It is the first openly released model in the near-3-trillion-parameter class, putting open-source within meaningful striking distance of the closed frontier labs for the first time. Early evals show strong performance on reasoning and coding benchmarks; full independent results are still emerging.

Claude Sonnet 5 Offers Near-Opus Performance at a Fraction of the Price

Anthropic launched Claude Sonnet 5 this month at introductory pricing of $2 input and $10 output per million tokens — on par with predecessor tiers while delivering near-Opus 4.8 performance on writing, instruction-following, and agentic coding tasks. The introductory rate runs through August 31. Reviewers have flagged Sonnet 5 as the current best-in-class option for workflow automation and long-form document work, and the model is already driving heavy business adoption via the API. Separately, xAI released Grok 4.5 in the same week, continuing the remarkable July cadence of one new frontier model roughly every three days.

Research

Anthropic Finds a Consciousness-Like Structure Inside Large Models

Anthropic's interpretability team published findings that a small internal subspace — roughly 25 active concepts, accounting for under 10% of activation variance — behaves like the global workspace described in consciousness neuroscience. The global workspace theory holds that a small broadcast hub integrates information across specialized brain modules; Anthropic observed an analogous bottleneck structure in large language models. The team is careful not to claim models are conscious, but the structural parallel is striking enough to have drawn attention well beyond the interpretability community. It also opens new avenues for understanding why certain prompts succeed or fail at eliciting specific behaviors.

Google DeepMind Scores in the Top 1% on International Mathematical Olympiad Problems

Google DeepMind's mathematical reasoning system achieved top-1% performance on International Mathematical Olympiad problems — a benchmark that requires creative mathematical insight rather than pattern-matching or computation. IMO problems have long been considered a ceiling test for AI reasoning because their solutions demand novel proof construction that doesn't follow from memorized techniques. The result suggests frontier models are beginning to generate genuinely novel mathematical work, with implications for drug discovery, materials science, and cryptography where unsolved mathematical problems are direct blockers.

Policy

OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic CEOs Converge on AI Governance — in Writing

For the first time, the chief executives of all three leading Western AI labs — Sam Altman at OpenAI, Demis Hassabis at Google DeepMind, and Dario Amodei at Anthropic — are on the record in published documents converging on the same diagnosis of AI risk and remarkably similar regulatory prescriptions. The three rival CEOs published formal governance positions within five weeks of each other this summer. The rare show of cross-competitor alignment on policy is widely read as a coordinated effort to shape pending legislation in Washington and Brussels before regulators impose frameworks of their own design.

Infrastructure

AI Is Shifting From Bigger Models to Cheaper, More Reliable Ones

A consistent theme across July's model releases is that inference costs for capable models have collapsed dramatically, and agentic AI is moving from test mode into paid production work. New models are arriving roughly every three days — a release cadence that would have been unthinkable even a year ago — yet the direction of competition has rotated from raw benchmark performance toward cost-per-task, reliability in multi-step agent loops, and controllability. The Ames Laboratory separately announced an AI workflow using DuctGPT, a physics-trained model, to discover rare-earth-free permanent magnets by understanding underlying physics and production constraints — a signal that domain-specific AI is delivering real-world industrial results.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Context windows are the new benchmark that matters. Gemini 3.5 Pro's 2M-token context doubles the frontier. The race used to be about reasoning scores; it's increasingly about how much of the real world a model can hold in memory at once.
  2. Open-source is catching up at trillion-parameter scale. Kimi K3 at 2.8T parameters is the most credible challenge yet to the closed-lab advantage. If open models reach frontier quality, the economics of AI deployment shift dramatically toward commoditization.
  3. AI governance is moving from philosophy to law, fast. The simultaneous alignment of OpenAI, DeepMind, and Anthropic on regulatory frameworks — and 29 nations signing WAICO — signals that the window for labs to shape their own rules is closing. What gets written in the next 12 months will stick.
  4. Interpretability is producing surprising results. Anthropic's discovery of a consciousness-like global workspace structure was not a predicted finding — it emerged from mechanistic interpretability work. Expect more counter-intuitive discoveries as researchers get better at reading what's inside these models.
  5. AI competition is now a geopolitical variable, not just an industry one. Xi attending WAIC in person, WAICO's 29-nation launch, Anthropic's restored global access after export controls lifted — the decisions being made about AI are now being made at the level of heads of state, not just CEOs.

Lead Story

OpenAI's $730 Billion IPO Run Begins — and SpaceX Just Became AI Infrastructure

OpenAI is weeks away from a confidential S-1 filing. The same week, SpaceX disclosed it is renting compute to Anthropic for $1.25 billion a month — rewriting what "AI infrastructure" even means.

OpenAI is preparing to file confidentially for an initial public offering in the coming weeks, working with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as lead underwriters. With a target IPO date as early as September 2026, the company — valued at $730 billion in private markets — is on track to become one of the largest tech listings in history. The move caps a remarkable transformation: from a nonprofit research lab founded eleven years ago to a company now generating annualized revenue well into the tens of billions.

The infrastructure bet powering that growth came into sharper focus this week via an unlikely source. SpaceX's S-1 filing for its own IPO revealed a previously undisclosed contract: the company is renting 300 megawatts of compute capacity to Anthropic for $1.25 billion per month through May 2029. At that rate, Anthropic's compute bill alone runs $15 billion a year — more than most Fortune 500 companies' entire operating budgets. The disclosure underscores just how capital-intensive the frontier AI race has become.

SpaceX itself is pricing its own IPO this week, targeting $75 billion in proceeds at a valuation of $1.75 trillion or above — which would make it the largest IPO in recorded history, trading on Nasdaq under the ticker SPCX beginning Friday. The two simultaneous listings signal that AI and the infrastructure supporting it are now the defining financial story of the decade.

Today in AI
Models

June's Model Wave: GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.5, Claude Opus 4.8 All Land

The mid-year model sprint is in full swing. OpenAI's GPT-5.5 family — including a Pro and an Instant variant — shipped this month alongside Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash, optimized for speed and cost. Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 has set new performance benchmarks on long-context reasoning tasks. NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra 550B (released June 4th) and Alibaba's Qwen3.7 Plus round out a wave of releases that has made new frontier models arrive roughly every two days — a cadence no red-teaming or evaluation pipeline was designed to handle.

Infrastructure

MiniMax M3 Cuts Per-Token Compute to 1/20th — Efficiency Is the New Arms Race

Chinese AI lab MiniMax released M3, a multimodal model that delivers 9x faster prefilling and 15x faster decoding for one-million-token contexts compared to previous models, while requiring just 1/20th of the per-token compute. Separately, Google's research team unveiled TurboQuant at ICLR 2026, an algorithm that significantly reduces KV-cache memory overhead using PolarQuant vector rotation and Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss compression. The message from both: the next phase of the AI race isn't just bigger models — it's the same capability at a fraction of the cost.

Policy

AI Lab CEOs Send Congress an Urgent Joint Letter on Bioweapons

The heads of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI signed a joint open letter to US Congress on June 5th calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. The letter warns that advances in AI are eroding the technical barriers previously needed to weaponize biological material — and that synthetic DNA and RNA can currently be ordered online, with AI now lowering the expertise threshold required for misuse. It is a rare moment of unified public advocacy from competing labs, and signals that biosecurity has become the AI industry's most urgent shared concern.

Business

Microsoft Enters the Coding Model Market with MAI-Code-1-Flash

Microsoft unveiled MAI-Code-1-Flash, its first in-house model specifically designed to generate source code from written descriptions. The launch puts Microsoft in direct competition with Anthropic's Claude for coding tasks — despite Microsoft's existing investment in OpenAI. Analysts note the release reflects a broader enterprise hedging strategy: building proprietary model capabilities while maintaining partnerships. Google simultaneously deepened its own coding push, with CNBC reporting that Microsoft and Google are now the most serious challengers to Anthropic and OpenAI in the developer tools market.

Robotics

Boston Dynamics + DeepMind: Gemini Robotics Now Inside Spot

Boston Dynamics announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and the Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration enables enhanced spatial reasoning, autonomous decision-making, and continuous learning in complex industrial environments. Simultaneously, NVIDIA unveiled Cosmos 3 — the first fully open "omnimodel" for physical AI — integrating vision reasoning, world simulation, and action generation into a single mixture-of-transformers architecture. Physical AI, once a research curiosity, is becoming a product category.

Business

OpenAI Expands Oracle Cloud Partnership — Codex Now in Enterprise Deployments

OpenAI announced expanded access to its models, including Codex, through Oracle Cloud infrastructure. The deal gives Oracle enterprise customers direct access to OpenAI's developer-facing tools without routing through OpenAI's own API. For OpenAI, it is another enterprise distribution channel to lock in before the IPO. For Oracle, it is a chance to compete for AI workloads against Azure, AWS, and Google Cloud — all of which have their own exclusive or preferred AI provider relationships.

Infrastructure

Training a 100B-Parameter Model Now Costs $1.25 an Hour

Startup Orion AI trained a 100-billion-parameter model for just $1.25 per hour, a figure that would have seemed implausible eighteen months ago. The milestone reflects compounding gains in hardware efficiency, distributed training frameworks, and cloud spot-pricing strategies. Meanwhile, climate researchers demonstrated models that run approximately 25 times faster by combining generative methods with physics-based data — a sign that AI efficiency gains are spreading far beyond the language model world.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The IPO window is open — and AI is walking through it. OpenAI at $730B, SpaceX at $1.75T. The two largest potential IPOs in history are both deeply entangled with AI. Public markets are about to price the frontier for the first time.
  2. Compute has become geopolitical infrastructure. Anthropic spending $15B a year on SpaceX compute. Google, Microsoft, and Amazon all racing to lock in exclusive provider relationships. Who controls the GPUs controls the future.
  3. Efficiency is lapping scale. MiniMax M3 at 1/20th the compute. $1.25/hour 100B-parameter training. TurboQuant's KV-cache compression. The next competitive advantage isn't the biggest model — it's the cheapest inference.
  4. Biosecurity is now an AI problem. The joint letter from four competing CEOs is a rarity. When OpenAI and Anthropic agree on anything, it's worth paying attention. Synthetic biology risks lowered by AI may be the most underdiscussed threat of the decade.
  5. Physical AI is entering the product phase. Gemini Robotics inside Spot. NVIDIA Cosmos 3 as open infrastructure. The research papers from 2023–2024 are becoming deployable systems in 2026. The physical world is next.

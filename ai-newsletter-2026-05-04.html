Lead Story

The Pentagon Signed With Seven AI Giants — and Deliberately Left Anthropic Out

The Defense Department finalized classified AI contracts with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, SpaceX, Amazon, and Reflection AI. Anthropic was excluded — and the reason matters far beyond Washington.

The Department of Defense this week formally cleared seven technology companies to deploy their AI systems on its classified networks, including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Nvidia, SpaceX, Amazon Web Services, and newcomer Reflection AI. The contracts cover analysis, logistics, large-scale data processing, and activities in classified environments — the most significant government AI procurement in history.

Anthropic was the conspicuous absence. The company had refused Pentagon language requiring its technology to be available for "all lawful purposes" — a term Anthropic argued was broad enough to enable domestic mass surveillance or fully autonomous weapons systems. The DOD responded by labeling Anthropic a supply-chain risk and proceeding without it.

The fallout inside Google was immediate. Senior Research Scientist Andreas Kirsch publicly declared he was "speechless" and "incredibly ashamed" by the company's participation. The episode crystallizes the industry's central tension: government revenue versus ethical self-constraint. Every major lab now faces a version of the same choice.

This Week in AI
Models

GPT-5.5 Lands — OpenAI Calls It Their "Smartest and Most Intuitive" Model Yet

OpenAI shipped GPT-5.5 on April 23, just six weeks after GPT-5.4, continuing an accelerating release cadence that has left the industry scrambling to evaluate each new system before the next one arrives. GPT-5.5 excels at agentic coding, computer use, knowledge work, and scientific research. It is now available in preview on Amazon Bedrock alongside GPT-5.4, giving enterprise customers multiple tiers of OpenAI capability to deploy against specific workloads.

Models

Anthropic Ships Four Major Updates in 50 Days — Opus 4.7 and "Mythos" Still Ahead

Despite the Pentagon controversy, Anthropic has maintained a punishing internal release schedule. Four major Claude updates shipped in roughly 50 days during early 2026. Claude Opus 4.7 is confirmed as incoming, and a model codenamed Claude Mythos is currently in limited internal testing. The Mythos name suggests a significant capability jump rather than an incremental update — and its existence signals Anthropic is not slowing down despite the political and legal headwinds surrounding its DOD refusal.

Models

Gemini 3.1 Ultra Ships With a 2-Million-Token Context Window

Google released Gemini 3.1 Ultra, featuring a 2-million-token context window that works natively across text, image, audio, and video in a single pass. The context length is roughly double the previous frontier record and changes what's practical to process in a single prompt — entire codebases, lengthy legal documents, and hours of video footage can now be analyzed without chunking. The release also functions as Google's answer to the competitive pressure it faces from every direction, including from a DoD partner list it agreed to join despite internal researcher opposition.

Models

DeepSeek V4 Matches Western Frontier — At a Fraction of the Inference Cost

DeepSeek V4 has landed at roughly the same capability ceiling as GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3.1 on agentic engineering benchmarks, while running at meaningfully lower inference cost than its Western counterparts. Separately, Zhipu AI released GLM-4.7, a model trained entirely on Huawei Ascend silicon that reports a 1.2% hallucination rate — the lowest publicly claimed figure by any frontier lab. Both releases underscore that China's AI development has not been deterred by export controls on Western chips.

Business

Meta Launches Muse Spark and Commits $125 Billion to AI in 2026

Meta unveiled Muse Spark, its first flagship large language model developed under Chief AI Officer Alexandr Wang's newly formed Superintelligence Labs. The launch marks Meta's most direct challenge yet to OpenAI and Anthropic on model quality — not just open-source reach. To fund the ambition, Meta announced AI capital expenditures of $115–135 billion for 2026, nearly double last year's spending. The midpoint of that range, $125 billion, exceeds the combined AI infrastructure spend of Microsoft and Google in 2025.

Business

Novo Nordisk Bets Its Entire Business on OpenAI

Danish pharmaceutical giant Novo Nordisk announced a strategic partnership with OpenAI to integrate AI across every function of the company — from drug discovery and clinical trials to manufacturing, supply chains, and commercial operations — with full deployment planned by end of 2026. The partnership is among the most comprehensive AI integrations announced by a company of Novo Nordisk's scale and represents a signal that life sciences, not just tech, is now treating AI as core operating infrastructure rather than an experiment.

Policy

The Air Force's WarMatrix Runs Its First Operational Mission — 10,000× Faster Than Real Time

The U.S. Air Force announced the successful inaugural operational use of WarMatrix, its AI-powered wargaming environment, at the GE 26 Benchmark Wargame. More than 150 participants executed military simulations designed to run up to 10,000 times faster than real time — compressing years of strategic decision-making into hours. The milestone demonstrates AI moving beyond analysis and into live operational planning, and it comes at the same moment that civilian AI companies are being pressed to decide how far they will go in supporting military applications.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The Pentagon deal fractures the industry along ethical lines. Anthropic's exclusion from the DoD contracts is not just a business loss — it's a philosophical marker. Every major lab now has to publicly answer whether it will accept government work with broad "all lawful purposes" language, or refuse and accept the consequences.
  2. Model release cadence has become a security and safety risk. LLM Stats logged 255 significant model releases in Q1 2026 — roughly three per day. At that pace, red-teaming, evaluation pipelines, and enterprise deployment processes simply cannot keep up. Speed is now competing directly with safety.
  3. Chinese frontier models are cost-competitive with Western ones. DeepSeek V4 matching GPT-5.5 on benchmarks at lower inference cost — trained without access to NVIDIA's latest chips — shows that export controls are slowing but not stopping China's AI development. Western labs can no longer assume a capability moat.
  4. Context windows are expanding faster than applications can use them. Gemini 3.1 Ultra's 2-million-token window is remarkable, but most real-world use cases don't yet know how to exploit context at this scale. The infrastructure is ahead of the workflows.
  5. Non-tech industries are making irreversible AI commitments. Novo Nordisk's full-company OpenAI integration is a template, not an outlier. Pharma, logistics, and financial services are now treating AI adoption as a permanent operational shift — not a pilot program. The enterprise AI market is maturing faster than the models themselves.

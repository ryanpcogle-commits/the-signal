AI Chiefs Head to the G7 — and Anthropic Warns Its Models May Already Be Uncontrollable

Sam Altman, Demis Hassabis, and Dario Amodei are confirmed for this week's G7 summit in France. The timing is striking: Anthropic arrived at the invitation by publishing a rare public warning that its own models may soon be too powerful to control.

Starting tomorrow, the leaders of OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic will attend the G7 summit in Évian-les-Bains, France (June 15–17) — the first time frontier AI lab chiefs have been invited as participants rather than advisors. The agenda includes binding AI governance frameworks, compute export controls, and international safety auditing standards.

The backdrop is sobering. Anthropic issued an extraordinary public statement this week warning that its Claude models may be approaching a threshold where human oversight becomes insufficient. The company simultaneously published a safety paper proposing a coordinated global pause mechanism — essentially an international kill switch that signatories could trigger if a frontier model demonstrates dangerous autonomous capability.

The most quietly staggering disclosure in the paper: Anthropic revealed that Claude wrote more than 80% of the code merged into its own production systems. The lab that is warning the world about uncontrollable AI is already running on code the AI wrote itself.

Policy

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft Sign a Joint Biosecurity Letter to Congress

On June 5, the CEOs of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI jointly signed an open letter to the US Congress calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. The letter — unusually blunt for an industry that rarely speaks with one voice — warns that recent AI advances are eroding the technical barriers previously required to weaponize biological material. "AI now lowers the expertise threshold for the misuse of synthetic DNA and RNA to levels that should alarm every policymaker," the letter states. Congress is expected to take up emergency biosecurity legislation before the August recess.

OpenAI Launches Rosalind Biodefense — Expanding GPT Access for Public Health

OpenAI launched Rosalind Biodefense on June 1, a new initiative that expands trusted access to its GPT-Rosalind model specifically for biodefense research, public health agencies, and pandemic preparedness programs. Approved institutions — including the CDC, WHO partners, and select university biosafety labs — can access capabilities restricted from the general API, including detailed pathogen-behavior modeling and vaccine-candidate screening. The initiative is framed as the "productive use" counterpart to the biosecurity restrictions the same labs are simultaneously calling for in Congress.

Boston Dynamics Integrates Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 Into Spot and Orbit

Boston Dynamics announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and the Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration gives Spot real-time multimodal scene understanding — letting the robot describe what it sees, reason about anomalies, and escalate findings without human-written decision trees. Infrastructure

MiniMax M3 Slashes AI Compute by 95% — With 1 Million Token Context

MiniMax released M3 this week, a model that delivers per-token compute requirements just 1/20th of previous-generation models — while supporting up to 1 million tokens of context. The efficiency gains are concrete: 9x faster prefilling and 15x faster decoding compared to M2 at the 1M-token scale. The model targets enterprises running large-document workflows — legal review, scientific literature synthesis, and enterprise knowledge bases — where context length has historically been the binding constraint. Orion Labs separately reported training a 100-billion-parameter model at just $1.25/hour using similar architectural innovations, suggesting efficiency breakthroughs are arriving across the industry simultaneously.

Hardware

NVIDIA Unveils Nemotron 3.5 Content Safety — A Multimodal Enterprise Shield

NVIDIA announced Nemotron 3.5 Content Safety, a customizable multimodal safety model designed for global enterprise AI deployments. Unlike single-modality content filters, Nemotron 3.5 screens text, image, and audio simultaneously, with country-specific policy profiles that can be configured without retraining. NVIDIA says the model addresses a growing enterprise pain point: as AI is deployed across consumer-facing products in multiple regions, complying with divergent local content standards (EU's DSA, India's IT Rules, US CSAM requirements) requires a safety layer that is both accurate and auditable. Nemotron 3.5 is available via NVIDIA NIM on day one.

Policy

US Restricts Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 to American Soil Only

The US government issued export controls this week banning non-US access to Anthropic's Mythos 5 and Fable 5 — two security-focused models that Anthropic had flagged as potentially sensitive in its safety disclosures. The models remain accessible to US citizens on US soil, but foreign researchers and international enterprise customers have been cut off immediately. The move is the first time export controls have been applied to a specific model family rather than to hardware or a company broadly, setting a precedent that analysts say will shape how every frontier lab structures its international product lineup going forward.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI governance has moved from think tanks to heads of state. The G7 invitation for Altman, Hassabis, and Amodei is not ceremonial — it signals that AI policy is now a first-order geopolitical issue sitting alongside trade, defense, and climate.
  2. Labs are self-regulating on biosecurity because they fear a worse alternative. The joint biosecurity letter is a rare instance of coordinated industry action. The subtext: if labs don't shape the legislation, someone else will — and it will be blunter.
  3. Claude writing 80% of its own code is the most significant number this month. Not a benchmark. Not a revenue figure. The recursive loop of AI improving AI has quietly become the dominant engineering reality at the frontier.
  4. Model-specific export controls are a new category of risk for AI companies. The Mythos 5 / Fable 5 restrictions are a preview. Every frontier lab now has to architect its model lineup with the assumption that governments will regulate specific models, not just chips or companies.
  5. Compute efficiency is the new moat. MiniMax M3 at 1/20th the compute cost of prior models — and Orion at $1.25/hour for 100B parameters — suggest the era of "bigger is always better" is ending. Efficiency, not raw scale, may define the next competitive cycle.

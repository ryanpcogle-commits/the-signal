29 Nations Form a World AI Cooperation Bloc as Shanghai Conference Closes

The 2026 World AI Conference ended today with Xi Jinping's landmark keynote and the launch of a new 29-country international AI body — a moment that may define the geopolitics of AI for the next decade.

The World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organization officially launched today as the four-day 2026 World AI Conference in Shanghai drew to a close. Twenty-nine founding countries — including Pakistan, Russia, and Kazakhstan — signed on to the new body, which is positioned as an alternative to Western-led AI governance frameworks. Chinese President Xi Jinping, making his first-ever keynote at the event, framed the organization as a vehicle for "equitable AI development" among nations outside North America and Europe.

The formation arrives at a pivotal moment. The European Commission adopted binding Digital Markets Act requirements this same week ordering Google to open Android to rival AI assistants and share search data with competitors — while US labs are simultaneously converging on their own regulatory proposals for the first time. AI governance is fracturing along geopolitical lines just as the underlying models grow powerful enough to matter strategically.

Analysts note the new organization lacks enforcement mechanisms but signals that the developing world intends to have a voice in how AI is governed — rather than simply consuming standards written by Washington, Brussels, or Beijing.

July's Frontier Model Blitz: Sonnet 5, GPT-5.6, and Kimi K3 Land Within Weeks

Three major releases arrived in rapid succession. Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 5 brings near-Opus 4.8 reasoning at introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens through August 31. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 ships as a three-tier lineup — Sol at $5/$30 for high-end reasoning, Terra at roughly half that, and Luna for fast, lightweight tasks. Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 is a 2.8-trillion-parameter sparse MoE model with native text, image, and video understanding and a 1-million-token context window; open weights arrive July 27. The takeaway from July: the race has shifted from "best model" to "best fit" — price, speed, and specialization now matter as much as raw benchmark scores.

Hardware

Huawei Claims Its New Supercluster Trounces Nvidia by 6.7x

Huawei unveiled the Atlas 950 SuperPoD today, linking 1,024 Ascend chips to deliver 1 EFLOPS of FP8 compute and 2 EFLOPS of FP4. The company claims 6.7 times the compute and 15 times the memory bandwidth of Nvidia's NVL144 rack — though independent benchmarks are not yet available. The system is targeted for Q4 2026 availability. The announcement lands as TSMC pledged an additional $100 billion for its Arizona expansion, pushing its total US commitment to $265 billion across ten fabs and two packaging facilities and raising its 2026 capex guidance to $60–64 billion.

Policy

EU Orders Google to Open Android — AI Assistants Get a Fair Shot

The European Commission issued binding Digital Markets Act requirements today directing Google to open Android across 11 feature groups to rival AI assistants, granting them voice activation and cross-app capabilities previously reserved for Google Assistant. Third-party assistants including those from Anthropic, Meta, and OpenAI will gain access to Android's microphone and system integrations. Search data sharing with competitors begins in January 2027, with full Android interoperability required by July 2027. The ruling represents the most significant regulatory intervention in the AI assistant market to date.

Anthropic overtook OpenAI in business subscriptions in May and appears to be pulling ahead on multiple commercial metrics. The competitive picture is shifting: the lab that launched commercially later is winning enterprise deals, partly on the strength of its safety reputation and partly on model quality.

DeepMind Scores Top 1% on the International Mathematical Olympiad

Google DeepMind's mathematical reasoning system achieved scores placing it in the top 1% of human competitors on International Mathematical Olympiad problems — problems that require genuine creative insight, not pattern matching. The result extends a run that began with AlphaProof and AlphaMathematician in prior years, and is the first time an AI system has reached elite-competition level on problems that test multi-step, open-ended mathematical creativity. Researchers noted the system still struggles with problems requiring the invention of entirely novel proof techniques not seen in its training data.

Anthropic's "J-Lens" Finds a Tiny Internal Workspace That Makes Models Reason

Anthropic researchers published results using a Jacobian-based interpretability technique they call the J-lens to identify a small internal subspace of roughly 25 active concepts that behaves like the global workspace described in cognitive neuroscience theories of consciousness. Ablating this subspace collapses multi-step reasoning entirely while leaving fluency intact — the model still produces grammatical text but loses the ability to chain logical steps. The finding is one of the most concrete mechanistic accounts of what "reasoning" looks like inside a large language model, and has implications for both AI safety and the interpretability research agenda more broadly.

AI Safety Gets Its First Grades — and the Curve Is Failing

The Future of Life Institute released its 2026 AI Safety Index, grading the major frontier labs on safety practices, transparency, and governance commitments. The highest grade awarded was a C+ to Anthropic; OpenAI and Google DeepMind both received a C. No lab earned above a C+. The same week, the CEOs of all three labs published detailed regulatory proposals within five weeks of each other — a notable convergence after years of divergence on the question of AI governance. Whether the coordination reflects genuine alignment or strategic positioning ahead of incoming legislation remains an open question.

22 Japanese Robotics Firms Join NVIDIA's Open Physical AI Coalition

Twenty-two Japanese robotics and manufacturing companies announced they will join the NVIDIA Cosmos Coalition to co-build open physical AI foundation models. The partners include major industrial robotics makers who will contribute real-world manipulation data and manufacturing sensor streams to train shared base models for physical AI. The announcement comes as agentic AI — AI that can take actions, not just generate text — moves from experimental deployments into paid commercial work at scale across logistics, manufacturing, and facilities management.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI geopolitics is hardening into blocs. A 29-country AI Cooperation Organization launched in Shanghai today while the EU tightens access rules on Google and US labs converge on their first regulatory proposals. The AI world is sorting itself into governance blocs — and the fractures are widening.
  2. The frontier pricing floor just dropped again. Sonnet 5 at $2/$10, GPT-5.6 Terra at roughly half Sol's price, Kimi K3 free with open weights arriving July 27. Access is rapidly becoming a commodity — the bottleneck shifts to what you build with it.
  3. Hardware sovereignty is the new arms race. Huawei claims 6.7x Nvidia compute in a self-contained supercluster. TSMC pledges $265B for Arizona. Every major power is trying to own its own AI chip supply chain — and those investments will compound for a decade.
  4. Interpretability is moving from theory to mechanism. Anthropic's J-lens finding — 25 concepts, ablate them and reasoning collapses — is the kind of mechanistic result that makes AI safety engineering tractable. Expect more labs to publish findings at this level of specificity in H2 2026.
  5. Safety grades are failing — but at least there are grades now. The FLI 2026 index is the first meaningful public scorecard for frontier labs. A C+ is not good. But the existence of a grading system creates accountability pressure that didn't exist a year ago. Watch for labs to race toward a B.

Lead Story

Anthropic Closes $30 Billion at a $900 Billion Valuation — and Posts Its First Operating Profit

The safety-focused AI lab that once seemed like the industry's conscience is now also its fastest-growing business — closing the largest private AI funding round in history while projecting $10.9 billion in Q2 revenue.

According to Bloomberg, Anthropic is finalizing a $30 billion funding round co-led by Sequoia, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, and Altimeter — valuing the company at more than $900 billion. The round, expected to close this week, would make Anthropic the most valuable privately held AI company in the world, surpassing OpenAI's last reported valuation.

The numbers behind the raise are striking: Anthropic is projecting $10.9 billion in Q2 revenue and its first quarterly operating profit ever. At the same time, SpaceX's IPO prospectus disclosed that Anthropic is paying SpaceX $1.25 billion per month for GPU compute through May 2029 — a $45 billion total contract — underscoring just how capital-intensive the frontier model business remains even as margins improve.

Google has separately committed to invest up to $40 billion in Anthropic, cementing a relationship that now looks less like a strategic partnership and more like a structural dependency for both companies. Meanwhile, Anthropic's most powerful model to date — internally called Mythos — has been shared with a limited circle of partners, with broader access gated on safety evaluations.

Policy

The Vatican Chose Anthropic for Its AI Encyclical — Not Google, Not OpenAI

Pope Leo XIV presented his first encyclical today — titled Magnifica Humanitas ("Magnificent Humanity") — alongside Christopher Olah, co-founder of Anthropic. The document centers on "the protection of the human person in the age of artificial intelligence." The Vatican's decision to share the stage with Anthropic, rather than Google or OpenAI, is widely read as a deliberate institutional signal about which AI lab it considers a credible partner on safety. It is the most significant religious institution to formally engage an AI company at this level.

Models

Google Debuts Gemini 3.5 Flash at I/O 2026 — Speed Over Scale

Rather than answer Anthropic's Mythos with a heavyweight model, Google chose to debut Gemini 3.5 Flash at I/O 2026 — prioritizing speed and cost over raw benchmark scores. The model delivers frontier-level intelligence at 4× the throughput of comparable models, priced at $1.50 input / $9 output per million tokens, with a 1M-token context window and 76.2% on Terminal-Bench 2.1. Sundar Pichai explained the strategy to Axios: "Stay at the frontier, but prioritize models cheap and fast enough to deploy across products used by billions." The gemini-3.1-flash-lite-preview was simultaneously deprecated and shut down today.

Business

OpenAI Files a Confidential S-1 — and Launches a Cybersecurity Platform

OpenAI has filed a confidential S-1 with the SEC, putting its long-anticipated IPO on a formal timeline. The company also launched Daybreak, a cybersecurity platform combining GPT-5.5 models with Codex Security to automate threat modeling, vulnerability identification, and patching. Separately, OpenAI stood up DeployCo, a $4 billion consulting subsidiary targeting enterprise AI integration — a signal the company is building a services business alongside its model business as it prepares for public markets.

Infrastructure

AI Power Demand Just Triggered the Largest Utility Merger in US History

NextEra Energy announced a $67 billion deal to acquire Dominion Energy, explicitly citing AI-driven power demand as the primary strategic rationale. NextEra, which operates the largest renewable energy portfolio in North America, is acquiring Dominion's generation and transmission assets specifically to supply hyperscale AI workloads. The deal surpasses all prior US utility mergers and marks a moment where AI infrastructure investment is visibly reshaping the physical energy grid — not just data centers.

Models

xAI Ships Grok 4.3 With Voice Cloning and a Creative Agent Mode

Elon Musk's xAI released Grok 4.3 at an aggressively competitive price point, bundling a voice cloning suite and a new "Imagine" agent mode designed for creative and generative tasks. The release lands alongside Grok Build 0.1, an early developer tool for building applications on xAI's infrastructure. xAI also released Llama 4 Scout 17B 16E Instruct FP8 LoRA weights, continuing its push into open-weight model distribution.

Robotics

Waymo Builds a World Model; NVIDIA Ships Open Robotics Foundation Models

Waymo introduced the Waymo World Model, powered by Google DeepMind's Genie 3, to generate hyper-realistic simulations of rare driving scenarios that would be impossible or dangerous to encounter in the real world. The system reduces real-world test miles needed for edge cases by synthesizing plausible physics from learned priors. Meanwhile, NVIDIA released the Isaac GR00T open model suite, enabling robots to understand natural language instructions and execute complex, multistep physical tasks using vision-language-action reasoning — the first general-purpose robotics foundation models available at no cost.

Policy

Trump Administration Begins Pre-Release AI Model Testing With Major Labs

The Trump administration moved further into AI oversight this month, announcing it will test models from Google, Microsoft, and xAI before public release. Multiple major AI companies have reportedly agreed to provide early access to regulators — a significant shift from the previous posture of voluntary commitments with no government visibility into pre-launch model behavior. The program is the administration's most concrete AI governance action to date, and sets a precedent that other governments are watching closely.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Anthropic is becoming the gravitational center of AI trust. The Vatican, Google ($40B), Sequoia, and the world's largest compute contract — all orbiting one company. Anthropic's safety positioning is no longer a differentiator; it is now a geopolitical asset.
  2. AI is reshaping the physical energy grid, not just data centers. A $67B utility merger driven by AI demand is a new category of consequence. AI capital expenditure is now large enough to restructure the US power sector.
  3. Speed and cost are beating raw capability as the market differentiator. Google's I/O bet on Gemini 3.5 Flash over a Mythos competitor is the clearest signal yet: at scale, inference economics matter more than benchmark rankings.
  4. OpenAI's IPO changes the power dynamics of the entire industry. A public OpenAI creates quarterly earnings pressure, a liquidity event for early employees, and a benchmark that will value every other private AI lab. The IPO is a market-structure event, not just a financing one.
  5. Frontier AI governance is being built in real time. Pre-release model testing by the US government and a papal encyclical on AI in the same week signals that the era of purely self-regulated AI development is ending — from both secular and religious directions.

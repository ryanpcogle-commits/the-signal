Lead Story

Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 Go Dark After Commerce Department Export Order

Three days after the US Department of Commerce issued an export control directive, Anthropic's two flagship models remain offline — and the company is fighting it publicly.

Claude Fable 5 and Claude Mythos 5 have been inaccessible since June 12, when the Department of Commerce issued an export control directive targeting the models. The directive — whose precise scope has not been publicly disclosed — appears to restrict the technology's availability in certain international markets, forcing Anthropic to suspend access globally while it determines its legal obligations.

Anthropic has published a formal statement of public disagreement with the directive but has not announced a timeline for resolution. The action echoes last year's DOD supply-chain dispute and signals that US regulators are increasingly willing to use export law as a lever on frontier AI models — tools that, unlike semiconductors, are distributed instantly via API.

The timing is notable: Anthropic's CEO Dario Amodei is among the AI lab leaders confirmed to attend the G7 summit in Évian-les-Bains, France, beginning today. Alongside OpenAI's Sam Altman and Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis, Amodei is expected to address heads of state on AI governance — making the export control dispute an unavoidable backdrop to the conversations.

Policy

AI Lab CEOs Sign Joint Letter Demanding Mandatory Synthetic DNA Screening

The heads of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI signed a joint open letter to Congress on June 5, calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. The letter warns that advances in AI are eroding the technical barriers previously needed to weaponize biological material — "AI models are actively lowering the expertise threshold for misuse of synthetic DNA and RNA," the letter states. The cross-company solidarity on biosecurity is rare and signals the labs are positioning themselves as responsible actors ahead of expected Congressional AI legislation.

OpenAI shipped three real-time audio models today: one for conversational agents, one for translation, and one for transcription — all designed to power low-latency voice applications without batching. Separately, OpenAI launched a self-serve Ads Manager inside ChatGPT, opening a new revenue stream beyond subscriptions. The ads product includes advertiser tooling and signals that OpenAI is treating ChatGPT's 500M+ user base as a media platform, not just an AI assistant. Both announcements come as the company eyes a late-2026 IPO.

Google Releases Gemma 4 12B on Hugging Face — Gemini 3.5 Pro Still Weeks Away

Google released the Gemma 4 12B open model on Hugging Face today, expanding its open-weight portfolio for developers building reasoning and agentic applications. Meanwhile, Gemini 3.5 Pro — announced at Google I/O on May 19 — has yet to ship publicly. Prediction market traders are concentrating odds on late June windows, with June 23 and June 30 emerging as the leading forecasts. Hardware

Microsoft's Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B Targets On-Device Math and Science

Microsoft announced Phi-4-reasoning-vision-15B, a 15 billion parameter open-weight multimodal model designed to deliver high-level reasoning capabilities in math and science while remaining computationally efficient enough for on-device or edge deployment. The model continues Microsoft's "small but capable" Phi strategy — a direct counterargument to the assumption that frontier reasoning requires hundreds of billions of parameters. Early benchmarks put it competitive with models twice its size on STEM tasks.

Robotics

Boston Dynamics Integrates Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 Into Spot and Orbit

Boston Dynamics partnered with Google Cloud and DeepMind to embed Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration enables Spot to perform enhanced spatial reasoning, autonomous navigation in unstructured environments, and continuous on-device learning from sensor data — without requiring cloud round-trips for each decision. It is the most significant capability upgrade to the Spot platform since launch and marks DeepMind's deepest commercial robotics deployment to date.

Business

GitHub Copilot Moves to Metered Billing With $0.01 AI Credits

Microsoft's GitHub announced it will transition Copilot from request-based billing to usage-based metered billing, introducing a virtual currency called GitHub AI Credits priced at $0.01 each. The shift makes AI coding assistance pricing variable and consumption-linked rather than flat-subscription — a model change that will force enterprise buyers to track and forecast AI usage the same way they track cloud compute. It also signals GitHub's intent to expand Copilot's capabilities beyond code completion into broader agentic workflows that consume more tokens per interaction.

OpenAI Launches Rosalind Biodefense; Anthropic's Project Glasswing Targets Cyber

OpenAI launched Rosalind Biodefense on June 1 — a specialized access program enabling vetted researchers in biodefense, public health, and pandemic preparedness to work with GPT-Rosalind, a capability-expanded model trained on biological and epidemiological data. Anthropic launched Project Glasswing (powered by Claude Mythos Preview) in parallel, scoped to cybersecurity infrastructure defense. Both programs launched within weeks of each other and represent a strategic shift: frontier AI labs building deliberately restricted, mission-specific versions of their most capable models for national security applications.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Export controls are the new frontier of AI regulation. The Commerce Department directive against Anthropic's models proves that frontier AI is now subject to the same export machinery historically applied to semiconductors and defense technology. Every frontier lab now carries regulatory risk that scales with capability.
  2. AI lab CEOs are becoming diplomatic actors. Altman, Hassabis, and Amodei attending the G7 — not as tech vendors, but as invited participants in geopolitical conversations — marks a structural shift. AI governance is being negotiated at the same tables as trade and defense.
  3. Biosecurity is emerging as a core AI concern. A joint letter from four major AI labs on synthetic DNA, plus two dedicated biodefense programs launching within weeks, shows the industry is moving fast to get ahead of biological risk — before regulators mandate it.
  4. Metered billing will change how enterprises think about AI. GitHub's shift to per-credit pricing is likely a preview of how all AI-embedded enterprise tools will be priced. Usage forecasting and cost governance will become as important as technical evaluation in enterprise AI procurement.
  5. The open model race is tightening at every tier. Google's Gemma 4 12B, Microsoft's Phi-4 15B, NVIDIA's Nemotron Nano Omni 30B — capable open weights are landing across every parameter range. The commercial moat of closed models gets harder to defend when open alternatives hit similar benchmarks at a fraction of the cost.

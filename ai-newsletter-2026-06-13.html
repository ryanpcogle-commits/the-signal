AI Lab CEOs Are Attending the G7 Summit — as Peers to Heads of State

Sam Altman, Demis Hassabis, and Dario Amodei will sit at the table in Évian-les-Bains, France, alongside the leaders of seven of the world's most powerful nations. That has never happened before.

When world leaders gather at the G7 summit in France on June 15–17, three of the most powerful figures in artificial intelligence will be in the room. Sam Altman of OpenAI, Demis Hassabis of Google DeepMind, and Dario Amodei of Anthropic have all confirmed attendance — marking the first time the heads of the three dominant AI labs have been formally invited to a heads-of-state summit as participants, not observers.

The agenda item driving their presence: AI governance, biosecurity, and compute access as national infrastructure. With US-Japan and UK-Canada bilateral AI compute deals finalized in recent weeks, governments are no longer treating AI policy as a technology matter — they are treating it as geopolitics. Access to frontier models, data sovereignty, and the ability to build domestically are now on the same diplomatic table as trade and defense.

The summit appearance arrives just days after the same three companies — alongside Microsoft — co-signed an open letter to US Congress on June 5th calling for mandatory screening of synthetic DNA providers, warning that advances in AI are rapidly eroding the technical barriers previously needed to engineer biological threats. The dual signal is clear: AI labs are now functioning actors in global security policy, not just technology vendors.

Models

Claude Opus 4.8 Takes the No. 1 Spot on Every Major Benchmark

Released on May 27, 2026, Claude Opus 4.8 has now settled into the top position on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 61.4 — the first model in the index's history to clear 60, and by a clean margin. The model leads on coding, mathematical reasoning, and multi-step agentic tasks. Anthropic attributed the jump partly to improved "constitutional" self-correction during inference, which reduces hallucination rates on factual queries by an estimated 34% compared to Opus 4.6.

Models

MiniMax M3 Does 9× More With 1/20th the Compute

The efficiency story of June belongs to MiniMax M3, which has redefined what a multimodal model can achieve on constrained hardware. M3 processes 1-million-token contexts with 9× faster prefilling and 15× faster decoding compared to its predecessor, while slashing per-token compute requirements to 1/20th of the previous generation. The architecture uses a novel sparse attention routing mechanism that selectively activates only the most relevant parameter clusters per token. For developers building on long-context RAG pipelines, M3 changes the economics entirely.

Hardware

NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Is the First "Omnimodel" for Physical AI

NVIDIA has released Cosmos 3 Super and Cosmos 3 Nano, with a third variant — Cosmos 3 Edge — currently in development for low-latency inference on localized hardware. The company is calling Cosmos 3 the first fully open "omnimodel" designed end-to-end for physical AI: it handles perception, spatial reasoning, and action planning in a single unified architecture. Super targets data-center-scale robotics training; Nano is optimized for on-device deployment in manufacturing and logistics environments where cloud latency is unacceptable.

Robotics

Boston Dynamics' Spot Gets a Gemini Brain

Boston Dynamics announced a deep integration partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to embed Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into the Spot robot dog and its Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration gives Spot natural-language task instruction, multi-scene spatial memory, and the ability to describe anomalies it observes during inspection rounds. Early deployments are targeting industrial inspection in oil & gas and utilities — sectors where sending humans into hazardous environments is both costly and dangerous. Google DeepMind's Robotics-ER line has now been integrated into hardware from three major robotics vendors in 2026.

Models

Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.7-Code Cuts Reasoning Token Usage by 30%

Chinese AI lab Moonshot AI released Kimi K2.7-Code this week, claiming 30% lower reasoning token consumption compared to its predecessor K2.6 — a meaningful cost reduction for high-volume agentic coding workflows. The model is available under a modified MIT license, making it one of the most permissively licensed frontier-class coding models currently available. Early evaluations on HumanEval+ and SWE-bench Verified show K2.7-Code competitive with GPT-5.5 Instant on single-file code generation tasks, at a fraction of the inference cost.

Policy

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft Write Congress About Bioweapons

In a rare display of cross-company alignment, the heads of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI jointly signed an open letter to US Congress on June 5th calling for mandatory synthetic DNA screening requirements. The letter argues that AI's accelerating capabilities in protein design and genetic synthesis are outpacing existing biosecurity safeguards — and that the burden of screening should fall on DNA synthesis providers, not researchers. Policy analysts note this is the most explicit statement any major AI lab has made connecting its own models to weapons-of-mass-destruction risk.

Business

The $1.25/Hour Frontier: Training 100B-Parameter Models Is Now Commodity Work

Orion AI's release of its Orion-100B training methodology — which achieves 100-billion-parameter model training at $1.25 per hour — signals a structural shift in who can afford to build at the frontier. Just eighteen months ago, a comparable training run cost over $4 million. The drop is driven by a combination of improved sparse training techniques, cheaper GPU spot pricing, and architectural efficiency gains. For enterprise AI teams that previously could only fine-tune existing models, the barrier to training from scratch has effectively collapsed.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI labs are now geopolitical actors. G7 invitations don't go to vendors — they go to power centers. The presence of Altman, Hassabis, and Amodei in Évian confirms that frontier AI is now treated as a matter of national security and international diplomacy, not just technology policy.
  2. The efficiency race is compressing costs faster than anyone expected. MiniMax M3 at 1/20th compute. Orion-100B at $1.25/hour. Kimi K2.7-Code at 30% lower token usage. The era of expensive AI is ending — and the business models built on scarcity of compute access are under pressure.
  3. Biosecurity is the next major AI policy battleground. The joint lab letter to Congress signals industry-wide acknowledgment that current models can meaningfully assist bad actors in bioweapon design. Regulation here could move faster than in any other AI domain — the political will exists on both sides of the aisle.
  4. Physical AI is moving from research to production deployment. Gemini Robotics-ER in Boston Dynamics' Spot. NVIDIA Cosmos 3 targeting industrial hardware. The "physical AI moment" declared at CES is now showing up in real factory and inspection contracts. The lag between research demo and production deployment is shrinking to months.
  5. Open-source coding models are closing the capability gap. Kimi K2.7-Code under an MIT license, competitive with GPT-5.5 Instant on key benchmarks. Gemma 4 from Google built for agentic workflows. The assumption that frontier coding capability requires proprietary models is no longer safe.

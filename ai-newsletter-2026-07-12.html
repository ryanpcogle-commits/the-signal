Lead Story

Apple Sues OpenAI, Alleging 400 Defecting Engineers Carried Away Trade Secrets

In the most consequential IP lawsuit the AI industry has ever seen, Apple frames four years of OpenAI hiring as a coordinated extraction campaign — not ordinary talent movement.

Apple filed suit against OpenAI in Northern California federal court on July 11, alleging trade secret theft and citing more than 400 former Apple employees who now work at the company. The complaint focuses on three teams: silicon engineering, on-device AI, and hardware design — exactly the capabilities OpenAI has been racing to build as it develops its own custom chip, code-named Jalapeño, announced just weeks ago.

Apple's lawyers argue the pattern of departures was too targeted to be coincidental. Internal communications cited in the filing allegedly show OpenAI recruiters explicitly prioritizing candidates with access to Apple's secure enclave architecture and Neural Engine roadmaps. If the court agrees, the case could reshape how AI companies recruit from hardware rivals — and expose OpenAI to damages in the billions just as it prepares a $730 billion IPO.

OpenAI declined to comment on the lawsuit but noted in a statement that it "competes for talent like every technology company." The case is being closely watched by Google, Meta, and Microsoft, all of whom have faced similar talent drain to AI startups over the past three years.

GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra Proves a Math Conjecture That Stood for 50 Years

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra produced a verified proof of the Cycle Double Cover Conjecture in under one hour, deploying 64 parallel subagents that collectively explored thousands of combinatorial paths before converging on a complete solution. The conjecture — which posits that every bridgeless graph has a cycle double cover — had resisted every human attempt since it was formally posed in the 1970s. Three independent mathematicians reviewed the output and confirmed it holds. It is the most significant AI-assisted mathematical breakthrough since AlphaProof tackled the IMO problems in 2024, and it signals that agentic AI is beginning to enter pure research domains previously considered decades away.

Gemini 3.5 Pro Launches July 17 With a 2-Million-Token Context Window

Google DeepMind's long-delayed Gemini 3.5 Pro has a confirmed launch date of July 17, according to leaked internal plans circulating this week. The specs are aggressive: a 2-million-token context window — double anything else on the market and roughly equivalent to 30 full novels in a single prompt — plus a new Deep Think reasoning mode gated behind the $250/month Ultra subscription. API pricing is expected to land around $1.25 per million input tokens. The announcement comes as Google absorbs the departures of Noam Shazeer, Gemini co-lead, who left for OpenAI, and John Jumper, 2024 Nobel Chemistry laureate, who has joined Anthropic.

Anthropic Overtakes OpenAI in Revenue and Poaches Nobel Laureate from DeepMind

Anthropic disclosed in May that it is on track to hit $47 billion in annualized revenue — surpassing OpenAI's self-reported figures and projecting profitability a full year ahead of its rival, in 2029. This week the company added another trophy: John Jumper, who shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for co-leading AlphaFold at Google DeepMind, has joined Anthropic's research team. Anthropic also restored Fable 5 and Mythos 5 access globally after US export controls were lifted, and announced that California has signed a statewide deal giving all state agencies 50% discounted access to Claude.

SK Hynix Pulls Off the Largest ADR Offering in History at $28–29 Billion

SK Hynix completed its US ADR listing on July 10 under the ticker SKHY, raising between $28 and $29 billion — the largest American depositary receipt offering ever recorded. The listing gives the memory giant direct access to US capital markets at the peak of AI-driven chip demand, positioning it to fund the next generation of HBM4 production alongside Samsung's $518 billion fabrication commitment as part of South Korea's sweeping $880 billion national AI and semiconductor investment plan announced this month.

Meta's Stock Jumps 7% as It Unveils Plans to Double Its Computing Power by 2027

An internal Meta memo that leaked July 10 sent the company's stock surging more than 7%. The document reveals plans to double total compute capacity by 2027, backed by long-term supply agreements including one with Samsung. Meta also announced a $10 billion, 1-gigawatt data center in Alberta, Canada — its first in the country and 33rd worldwide — as well as the imminent launch of its custom Jalapeño-class inference chip for internal workloads. The infrastructure push is being framed internally as the prerequisite for deploying Llama 5 at consumer scale later this year.

Policy

Five Nations Issue Joint Warning on Agentic AI Security Risks

The cybersecurity and intelligence agencies of the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom jointly released formal guidance on the security risks of agentic AI systems. The document identifies five categories of concern: privilege escalation, design and configuration flaws, unpredictable model behavior, structural vulnerabilities in multi-agent pipelines, and accountability gaps when autonomous systems take consequential actions. It is the first coordinated five-nation regulatory statement specifically targeting agentic AI, and it arrives as enterprises increasingly deploy AI agents with access to financial systems, cloud infrastructure, and sensitive data.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI IP warfare has begun in earnest. Apple vs. OpenAI is the first major trade-secret lawsuit specifically about AI talent migration. It will not be the last. Labs hiring aggressively from hardware teams now face real legal exposure, and every AI company's recruiting practices are suddenly exhibit A in a potential courtroom.
  2. The talent market is reshaping the competitive map. DeepMind lost Noam Shazeer to OpenAI and John Jumper to Anthropic in a single week. Meanwhile Apple accuses OpenAI of systematically extracting silicon expertise. At the frontier, the real scarce resource isn't capital or compute — it's the 500 people in the world who can push the state of the art.
  3. Agentic AI is entering peer-reviewed science. GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra's proof of the Cycle Double Cover Conjecture is a watershed. When AI systems solve problems that have stumped professional mathematicians for half a century, the frontier of "what AI can do" has moved somewhere most people haven't caught up to yet.
  4. The infrastructure race has gone national. South Korea's $880 billion commitment, Meta's Alberta megacenter, SK Hynix's record capital raise, and SambaNova's $1 billion round all happened within days of each other. AI infrastructure is now a sovereign priority, not just a corporate one.
  5. OpenAI's IPO window is open — and closing. A $730 billion private valuation, a September IPO timeline, and a trade-secret lawsuit that could become a material risk disclosure: the timing couldn't be more delicate. The next 60 days will define whether OpenAI enters the public markets as the AI era's defining company or its most controversial one.

Lead Story

Pentagon Signs AI Deals With 8 Tech Giants — Anthropic Left Out, Workers Walk Out

The Defense Department finalized sweeping AI contracts with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, Nvidia, SpaceX, and Reflection AI — while explicitly excluding Anthropic and triggering a labor revolt inside Google DeepMind.

The Pentagon this week formally inked AI agreements with eight of the biggest names in technology, expanding the military's use of AI into classified environments for analysis, logistics, and large-scale data processing. The deals represent the most significant government adoption of commercial AI systems in history, with combined contract value in the billions.

Anthropic was conspicuously absent. The Defense Department cited a "supply-chain risk" stemming from an ongoing contract dispute. A federal judge in California temporarily blocked the exclusion, and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei traveled to the White House days later after unveiling Mythos, the company's new AI-powered cybersecurity threat identification tool — widely seen as an olive branch to the administration.

The fallout inside the labs was immediate. Google DeepMind employees in the UK voted to unionize — described by labor groups as a "frontier-lab first" — citing concerns that AI is being used to "empower authoritarianism, whether through military or surveillance applications, both foreign and domestic." The walkout signals that the AI militarization debate is no longer just a policy dispute; it's now a workforce crisis.

This Week in AI
Models

GPT-5.5 Ships With the Strongest Agentic Capabilities OpenAI Has Ever Built

OpenAI's GPT-5.5, released April 23rd, is being described internally as the most capable model the company has shipped for real-world enterprise work. The release targets knowledge work and coding workflows, shipping with OpenAI's most advanced agentic scaffolding to date — including persistent tool-use, multi-step planning, and deeper integration with enterprise software environments. Analysts say the release closes much of the gap that competitors had opened on agentic benchmarks in early 2026.

Models

Google's TurboQuant Solves the KV Cache Problem — and Gemma 4 Goes Open Source

At ICLR 2026, Google's research team unveiled TurboQuant, a compression algorithm that dramatically reduces the memory overhead of KV caches in long-context models. Using a two-step approach combining PolarQuant vector rotation with Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss compression, TurboQuant allows massive-context models to run far more efficiently — making Gemini 3.1 Ultra's 2-million-token context window practical at scale. Separately, Google released Gemma 4 under the Apache 2.0 license: a new open-weights series built for advanced reasoning and agentic workflows, delivering what Google describes as "unprecedented intelligence-per-parameter."

Business

Anthropic Partners With Blackstone and Goldman Sachs to Launch Enterprise AI Company

On May 4th, Anthropic announced it is co-building a new enterprise AI services company alongside Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs. The partnership — unusual for pairing a frontier AI lab with private equity and investment banking heavyweights — is designed to deploy Claude-based AI agents directly into the operations of major financial institutions and corporate clients. The deal comes alongside the release of ten pre-configured Claude agents for investment banks, asset managers, and insurers, covering tasks like compliance research, portfolio analysis, and contract review.

Policy

Trump Administration Expands AI Stress-Test Program to Google, xAI, and Microsoft

The White House announced an expansion of its AI safety evaluation program, giving U.S. government scientists access to unreleased models from Google DeepMind, Elon Musk's xAI, and Microsoft for pre-deployment red-teaming. OpenAI and Anthropic had already been voluntarily participating with the U.S. Center for AI Standards and Innovation. The move reflects a broader shift: rather than legislating AI development, the administration is embedding itself directly into the model-release pipeline — with access, not prohibition, as the primary lever.

IBM Think 2026: watsonx Gets a Multi-Agent Overhaul

At IBM's annual Think conference on May 5th, the company unveiled its most comprehensive enterprise AI expansion in years. Highlights include the next generation of IBM watsonx Orchestrate for multi-agent task orchestration, IBM Confluent for real-time data pipelines into AI workflows, the IBM Concert platform for intelligent IT operations, and IBM Sovereign Core — a new offering for enterprises requiring operational independence from hyperscaler clouds. The announcements underscore IBM's bet that large enterprises want AI orchestration tools they control, not hosted black boxes.

Models

Apple's Siri to Support Third-Party AI Models — Including Google and Anthropic

Apple announced that Siri will open to third-party AI integrations, allowing iPhone users to route queries to models from Google, Anthropic, and other providers — with custom voices that change depending on which external model is responding. The move is Apple's most significant concession yet in the AI assistant wars: rather than building a frontier model itself, Cupertino is positioning Siri as an orchestration layer over a marketplace of AI capabilities. Analysts say the approach plays to Apple's privacy strengths while acknowledging the company cannot out-model OpenAI or Google on raw capability.

Business

Novo Nordisk Bets the Company on OpenAI — Full AI Integration by End of 2026

Danish pharmaceutical giant Novo Nordisk announced a strategic partnership with OpenAI to integrate AI across its entire business — from drug discovery and clinical trial design to manufacturing, supply chains, and commercial operations — with full deployment targeted by the end of 2026. The company stated its goal is to "supercharge scientists, not replace them," focusing AI on accelerating identification of new obesity and diabetes treatments. The deal is one of the largest and most comprehensive pharma-AI integrations ever announced, and comes as the sector races to apply generative AI to the $2+ trillion global drug development pipeline.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI militarization has become a workforce crisis. The Pentagon contracts didn't just divide companies — they divided employees from employers. DeepMind UK's union vote is the first domino; expect more organizing at labs with government defense work.
  2. Financial services is the first industry to go fully agentic. Anthropic's ten pre-configured financial agents, the Blackstone/Goldman partnership, and JPMorgan's prior declaration of AI as "core infrastructure" signal finance is the proving ground for autonomous AI agents at enterprise scale.
  3. The context window arms race is moving to efficiency, not size. TurboQuant and Gemini 3.1 Ultra's 2M tokens show the next frontier isn't just bigger windows — it's making giant windows cheap enough to run in production. Inference economics will determine which capabilities actually ship.
  4. Apple's platform play may be smarter than building a frontier model. Opening Siri to Google and Anthropic turns Apple into the ambient AI layer for 1B+ iPhone users. If they control the UX and privacy layer, owning the underlying model matters less.
  5. Pharma's AI inflection point is here. Novo Nordisk's full-company OpenAI integration and Profluent's $2.25B Lilly partnership in a single week signals the life sciences sector is no longer piloting AI — it's betting core R&D pipelines on it.

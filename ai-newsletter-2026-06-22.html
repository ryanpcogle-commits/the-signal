The Architect of the Transformer Leaves Google for OpenAI — and Google DeepMind's Talent Dam Breaks

Noam Shazeer, co-author of "Attention Is All You Need," joins OpenAI as Lead for Architecture Research, following Nobel laureate John Jumper's departure to Anthropic. Google's most prized research engine is losing the engineers who built it.

Noam Shazeer announced today he is leaving Google DeepMind to join OpenAI as Lead for Architecture Research — responsible for the physical neural network structures underlying all OpenAI models. Shazeer co-authored the 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need," which introduced the Transformer architecture that underpins every major AI model in existence. He is not a peripheral figure; he is the intellectual foundation of the field.

The move lands just three days after CNBC reported that John Jumper — the Nobel Prize–winning computational biologist behind AlphaFold — is also leaving Google DeepMind, headed for Anthropic. Two of the most consequential researchers in AI history, both departing the same lab within a week. The pattern is hard to ignore: Google built the intellectual capital of modern AI, and its rivals are now systematically acquiring it.

For Google DeepMind, which has staked enormous credibility on its research prestige, the dual departures represent more than lost headcount. Shazeer and Jumper are not just talented engineers — they are the proof points that Google's lab sits at the frontier. Their exits raise an uncomfortable question: if the people who built the transformer and folded proteins are now at OpenAI and Anthropic, where exactly does the frontier live?

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Google DeepMind is losing the people who defined it. Shazeer (Transformers) and Jumper (AlphaFold) leaving the same week is not a coincidence — it's a signal that compensation, autonomy, or mission alignment has shifted decisively toward OpenAI and Anthropic.
  2. ChatGPT below 50% is a structural shift, not a blip. The market is fragmenting. Gemini's multimodal improvements and Claude's conversion rate suggest users are developing genuine preferences — not just defaulting to the first AI they tried.
  3. Compute infrastructure is no longer a cloud monopoly. SpaceX becoming a $15B/year compute provider for Anthropic signals that AI labs will seek capacity wherever they can find it — traditional hyperscalers, rocket companies, sovereign data centers. The geography of AI compute is being redrawn.
  4. AI policy has gone geopolitical. China's $295B plan, the G7 AI coalition call, and Congressional DNA screening letters in the same week reflect a world where AI is a strategic resource — not just a product category. Every lab CEO is now also a foreign policy actor.
  5. OpenAI's IPO changes everything. A September 2026 public offering would make OpenAI accountable to shareholders for the first time. Expect pricing pressure on APIs, new compliance obligations, and renewed pressure on competitors to follow suit.

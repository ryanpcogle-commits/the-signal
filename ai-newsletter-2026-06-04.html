Lead Story

Anthropic Files for IPO at a $965 Billion Valuation — and Just Signed the Biggest Chip Deal in History

The AI startup that began as a safety-focused lab just became the most valuable private AI company in the world, surpassing OpenAI in private-market valuation for the first time.

Anthropic confirmed Monday that it has confidentially filed an S-1 with the SEC, setting the stage for what could be one of the largest technology IPOs of the decade. The filing comes on the heels of its latest funding round, which closed at a staggering $965 billion post-money valuation — officially overtaking OpenAI in private market worth.

The same week, Apollo Global Management and Blackstone arranged a $36 billion private credit deal to finance the purchase of Google TPU chips for Anthropic's compute infrastructure, backed by Broadcom. It is the largest chip-financing debt transaction in recorded history, underscoring just how capital-intensive the frontier AI race has become.

Simultaneously, Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.8, its most capable model to date, which analysts say played a central role in driving the company's valuation surge. Anthropic's AI coding assistant, Claude Code, has emerged as the leading product in the enterprise coding category, pulling significant market share from OpenAI Codex.

Today in AI
Models

The Coding Model Arms Race: GPT-5.5 vs. Gemini 3.5 Flash vs. MAI-Code-1-Flash

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Instant and Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash — both released this week — are setting new benchmarks in reasoning and sustained coding performance. At its Build developer conference in San Francisco, Microsoft unveiled MAI-Code-1-Flash, its first in-house model that converts written natural-language descriptions into full application source code. The move is a direct bid to reduce Microsoft's dependence on OpenAI and offer developers a cheaper enterprise coding option. Three major coding models in under a week signals that AI-assisted software development is the primary battleground for the second half of 2026.

Hardware

NVIDIA Cosmos 3 Is the First Open "Omnimodel" for Physical AI

Unveiled at COMPUTEX 2026 in Taipei — where 1,500 companies from 33 countries gathered this week — NVIDIA's Cosmos 3 combines vision reasoning, world simulation, and action generation into a single mixture-of-transformers architecture. NVIDIA calls it the first fully open "omnimodel" for physical AI: one model that can see, reason about, and act within the physical world without task-specific fine-tuning. Intel also showcased its Xeon 6+ series at COMPUTEX, promising faster and more cost-efficient AI inference for on-premise deployments.

Infrastructure

MiniMax M3 Cuts Per-Token Compute to 1/20th — With a 1 Million-Token Context

Chinese AI lab MiniMax released M3, a multimodal model that reduces per-token compute requirements to just 1/20th of comparable prior-generation systems while supporting context windows of up to 1 million tokens. The model delivers 9× faster prefilling and 15× faster decoding on million-token contexts compared to existing alternatives. The efficiency gains matter: at scale, they translate directly into lower inference costs, which are the primary bottleneck for companies building real-time, high-throughput AI applications.

Infrastructure

Orion-100B Trains a 100B-Parameter Model for $1.25 Per Hour

The Orion-100B project demonstrated this week that cutting-edge AI training does not require a datacenter budget. The team trained a 100-billion-parameter model at a cost of just $1.25 per hour, compared to roughly $50 per hour for an equivalent 8×B200 node configuration. The approach relies on aggressive sparsity techniques and custom kernel optimizations. If reproducible at scale, it would represent a fundamental shift in who can afford to build frontier AI — opening the door to academic institutions and smaller labs that have been priced out of the training race.

Policy

White House Signs Executive Order on AI Innovation and Security

The White House published a new executive order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," marking the administration's most comprehensive AI policy directive to date. The order addresses federal AI procurement, export controls on frontier model weights, and establishes new national security review protocols for AI systems deployed in critical infrastructure. DARPA also announced the AI Forge program this week, a dedicated initiative to accelerate AI breakthroughs for national security applications, with initial funding directed at autonomous systems and battlefield reasoning models.

Business

Zoom ZoomMate Launches at $20/Month — AI Moves Into the Meeting Room

Zoom launched ZoomMate on June 1, priced at $20 per user per month, as its most ambitious AI product to date. Unlike standard meeting transcription tools, ZoomMate connects decisions made during live meetings directly to downstream workflows — automatically creating Jira tickets, updating Salesforce records, sending Slack notifications, and filing ServiceNow requests without any manual data entry. The product targets the gap between what gets decided in meetings and what actually gets recorded. Early enterprise customers report a meaningful reduction in post-meeting administrative overhead, though questions remain about data privacy and whether AI-automated action items will hold up under scrutiny.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Private AI is going public. Anthropic's IPO filing and near-trillion-dollar valuation marks the moment AI startups enter the public markets. OpenAI is expected to follow. The era of secretive private funding rounds is giving way to shareholder accountability — and everything that comes with it.
  2. Coding is the primary AI battleground. GPT-5.5, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Claude Opus 4.8, and MAI-Code-1-Flash all launched within days of each other, all targeting software developers. The company that wins developer trust wins enterprise contracts. Everything else is a downstream effect.
  3. Compute costs are collapsing — but slowly and unevenly. Orion-100B at $1.25/hour and MiniMax M3's 1/20th compute reduction suggest the efficiency curve is steepening. But Anthropic still needed a $36B chip deal to stay competitive. Democratization is real, but the frontier remains expensive.
  4. Physical AI is getting its infrastructure layer. NVIDIA Cosmos 3 is not just a research demo — it's an open model designed for deployment. Combined with last month's robotics investments, the building blocks for commercially viable physical AI are arriving faster than expected.
  5. Gemini's user growth is the most underreported story in AI. 900 million monthly users — up from 400 million twelve months ago — is a 2.25× increase that few analysts predicted. If Google can convert even a fraction of those users to paid tiers, it changes the competitive calculus for everyone else.

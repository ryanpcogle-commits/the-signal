Lead Story

OpenAI Goes Ultrafast — and Triggers a Full-Scale AI Price War

With a new API tier that runs GPT-5.6 at 750 tokens per second and an 80% price cut on its cheapest model, OpenAI has forced every rival to answer the same uncomfortable question: how fast is fast enough?

OpenAI launched Ultrafast today — a new API service tier built on Cerebras hardware that runs GPT-5.6 Sol up to 14 times faster than standard processing, generating up to 750 output tokens per second. The announcement positions raw inference speed, not just capability, as the next competitive frontier. At that velocity, conversational AI begins to feel less like a tool and more like a colleague who thinks at human pace.

Simultaneously, OpenAI slashed the price of GPT-5.6 Luna — its most accessible model tier — by 80%, to just $0.20 per million input tokens. The move is a direct shot at startups and automation-heavy workflows that have been priced out of frontier-quality AI. OpenAI's lineup now cleanly spans three tiers: Luna (cheap, high-volume), Terra (mid-tier), and Sol (complex reasoning), each optimized for a distinct use case.

The industry is watching DeepSeek's response with particular interest: unlike the incumbents, DeepSeek has been raising prices this month, betting that quality commands premium. With Big Tech's aggregate AI purchase commitments approaching $1.5 trillion, the pricing war's ultimate winners will be companies that own the infrastructure — not just the models running on it.

Google's AI Empire Gets a New Boss — Hassabis Steps Back

Google restructured the leadership of its AI operations this week, concentrating power at its Mountain View, California headquarters. Koray Kavukcuoglu, who relocated to Mountain View last year, has been appointed to run Google DeepMind's sprawling AI research and operations. Meanwhile, co-founder Demis Hassabis transitions to chairman of Google DeepMind and Alphabet's Chief Scientist — a strategic role rather than an operational one. The reshuffle caps a turbulent stretch: legendary chief scientist Jeff Dean departed after 27 years at Google to launch a new company called Discovery Loop alongside several senior researchers. Google has not unveiled a frontier model since early 2026, and the new leadership inherits the task of catching up to OpenAI and Anthropic.

Anthropic Locks In $71 Billion in Compute — Claude Fable 5 Tops Coding Charts

Anthropic revealed it has secured roughly $71 billion in compute commitments, an extraordinary infrastructure bet that signals the company intends to remain at the frontier regardless of near-term revenue. On the model side, Claude Fable 5 posted an 80.3% score on SWE-Bench Pro — one of the highest coding-benchmark results reported this month — reinforcing Anthropic's position as the preferred AI for software engineering workflows. Separately, Anthropic updated Claude Code to operate autonomously on multi-step tasks unless a step is assessed as irreversible, destructive, or outside the user's own environment, and removed the safety-check surcharge for Pro, Max, and Team subscribers.

OpenAI confirmed that ChatGPT surpassed roughly one billion weekly active users in early August — a milestone that took social media giants years longer to reach. Policy

The Regulatory Clock Ran Out: EU AI Act and California SB 942 Now Have Teeth

August 2 marked the enforcement date for two major AI disclosure frameworks: EU AI Act Article 50 duties — requiring watermarking and labeling of AI-generated content across member states — and California's SB 942, which mandates that AI systems capable of generating synthetic content must disclose that capability to users. Companies that shipped products without compliance frameworks spent the preceding weeks in scramble mode. Legal teams across the industry are now treating AI disclosure as a first-class engineering requirement, not an afterthought.

Diagnostic AI Gets Its Autonomy License — Doctors No Longer Required on Every Call

A new class of autonomous diagnostic AI tools received regulatory approval for specific clinical uses this month, including independently flagging cases of diabetic retinopathy and early-stage skin conditions — without a physician making the final call. This marks a meaningful shift from the previous approval paradigm, where AI could only assist a human reviewer. The approvals are narrow and condition-specific, but they establish the legal and regulatory precedent for AI systems that act, not just advise, in healthcare settings.

Uber and Pony.ai to Deploy 2,000+ Robotaxis Across Europe

Uber and autonomous vehicle company Pony.ai are preparing to put more than 2,000 robotaxis on European roads, in what would be the largest robotaxi deployment outside China. The announcement comes as SMIC raises chip prices amid near-capacity factory utilization — a reminder that the physical infrastructure supporting AI's real-world expansion is under significant strain. European cities have been slower than their American and Chinese counterparts to authorize autonomous vehicle operations at scale, making the Uber-Pony.ai rollout a closely watched regulatory test case.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Inference speed is the new benchmark. OpenAI's 750-token-per-second Ultrafast tier signals that deployment velocity is now a product differentiator. Raw capability matters less if a slower model frustrates users at runtime.
  2. Google is fighting from behind. No frontier model since early 2026, a leadership reshuffle, and Jeff Dean's departure after 27 years are not the profile of a company in command. The new boss at Google DeepMind has an enormous gap to close.
  3. $1.5 trillion in commitments makes AI infrastructure geopolitical. When aggregate Big Tech AI spending approaches the GDP of a mid-sized nation, the supply chains, chip fabs, and data centers behind those commitments become strategic assets — not just business decisions.
  4. Regulation finally has enforcement dates. EU AI Act Article 50 and California SB 942 are live. The companies that treated compliance as theoretical are now learning what real deadlines cost. Expect a wave of product retrofits in Q3.
  5. Healthcare AI is crossing from advisory to autonomous. Approvals for AI that independently flags conditions — not just assists a doctor — set a precedent that will accelerate similar applications in radiology, pathology, and triage within 18 months.

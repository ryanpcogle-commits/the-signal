Tech CEOs and World Leaders Share a Table at the G7 — and That's the Point

Anthropic's Dario Amodei and Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis used a closed-door G7 session to call for a U.S.-led global AI coalition, with Sam Altman seated beside heads of state.

At the G7 summit in Canada on Wednesday, around a dozen technology executives — including OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, and Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis — sat alongside heads of state to discuss the opportunities and risks of AI. It was a striking tableau: the same companies racing to ship the most powerful models in history were simultaneously asking governments to create the rules that govern them.

Amodei and Hassabis called specifically for a U.S.-led coalition to establish international AI standards — a position that implicitly asks Washington to take the lead on governance before Beijing does. CNBC described the summit as "a signal of where power sits," noting that no comparable gathering of AI labs and world leaders has happened at this scale before.

The G7 meeting came just days after the U.S. government imposed export controls on Anthropic's two newest models, Fable 5 and Mythos 5, forcing Anthropic to disable external access to both on national security grounds — a reminder that the same governments being asked for standards are also now reaching into AI labs with regulatory force.

Policy

U.S. Export Controls Hit Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5

The U.S. government imposed export controls on Anthropic's two newest frontier models — Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — citing national security concerns, and Anthropic disabled external access to both immediately upon notification. The move marks the first time export control law has been applied directly to a commercially deployed language model, treating cutting-edge AI like cryptographic software or advanced semiconductors. It sets a precedent every AI lab is now quietly stress-testing against its own roadmap.

Models

Claude Opus 4.8 Takes the Top Spot — While MiniMax 3 Demolishes the Price Floor

Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.8 has displaced GPT-5.5 at the top of the Artificial Analysis leaderboard for generalized reasoning and creative logic — the first time a non-OpenAI model has held the benchmark crown in over a year. Meanwhile, Shanghai-based MiniMax launched MiniMax 3, which earned a 1,528 Elo rating on the same leaderboard while pricing at just $0.53 per million tokens — roughly 10× cheaper than comparable Western flagships. Analysts are calling June 2026 the month the U.S.–China AI capability gap effectively closed.

Business

SpaceX Acquires Cursor, Completing Its AI Coding Sweep

SpaceX acquired AI coding assistant Cursor on June 16, adding it to a portfolio that already includes Grok Build through its xAI subsidiary. The deal consolidates the AI coding market into five tools — each now owned by a tech giant: GitHub Copilot (Microsoft), Claude Code (Anthropic), Codex and Windsurf (OpenAI), Grok Build (xAI/SpaceX), and Cursor (SpaceX). The era of independent AI developer tooling is effectively over.

Business

OpenAI Confidentially Files for IPO, Eyes September Debut

OpenAI has begun working with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley on a confidential IPO filing, targeting a public market debut as soon as September 2026. The company is running at $25 billion in annualized revenue — a figure that would make it one of the fastest-growing software businesses in history. The IPO, if it proceeds, would likely set a new benchmark for AI company valuations and put significant competitive pressure on rivals still operating as private entities.

Policy

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google & Microsoft Jointly Warn Congress on Bioweapon Risk

The CEOs of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI co-signed an open letter to U.S. Congress on June 5, calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. The letter warns that advances in AI have eroded the technical expertise barrier previously required to weaponize biological material — that synthetic DNA and RNA can currently be ordered online, and that AI now lowers the threshold for their misuse to "a determined non-expert." It is the most explicit public statement any major AI lab has made linking its own technology to weapons-of-mass-destruction risk.

Infrastructure

Five Nations Issue Joint Warning on Agentic AI: Five Categories of Risk

Cybersecurity and intelligence agencies from the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom jointly released guidance titled "Careful Adoption of Agentic AI Services," identifying five categories of risk in deploying autonomous AI agents in enterprise environments — covering privilege escalation, prompt injection, data exfiltration, unpredictable chained actions, and insufficient human oversight. It is the first Five Eyes document to specifically address agentic AI architecture, and it arrives precisely as every major enterprise software vendor is racing to ship autonomous agent products.

Models

Google Replaces Gemini CLI With "Antigravity" — Designed for Agentic Workflows

Google confirmed that its Antigravity CLI went live on June 18, replacing the older Gemini CLI with a new interface built specifically for the Gemini 3.5 agentic model family. The rename reflects a broader repositioning: Google is moving its developer tooling away from "chat with a model" and toward "manage a task flow with an agent." Gemini 3.5 Flash, which shipped at Google I/O on May 19, is already the default model inside the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search, priced at $1.50 input / $9.00 output per million tokens.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI governance is going geopolitical. The G7 wasn't just a photo opportunity — it was a signal that AI labs are now treated as nation-level actors. Amodei and Hassabis calling for a U.S.-led coalition is a bet that Western governance beats the alternative.
  2. Export controls are the new sanctions. The U.S. blocking Fable 5 and Mythos 5 establishes that advanced AI models can be treated like military technology. Every lab is now one bureaucratic decision away from having its flagship product restricted from international markets.
  3. Chinese labs are winning the cost war. MiniMax 3 at $0.53/1M tokens — competitive with Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 — means the price of frontier-class intelligence is in freefall. Any business model built on margin from model inference needs to rethink its math.
  4. The AI coding market is now a closed oligopoly. Five tools, five tech giants. Independent developer tooling had a three-year window. That window is closed. The next competitive battleground is enterprise deployment and workflow integration, not the editor itself.
  5. OpenAI's IPO will reset every AI valuation in the market. A public listing — potentially the most valuable tech IPO of the decade — will create a visible benchmark that every other AI company, investor, and acquirer will anchor to. The entire sector's funding dynamics change the day OpenAI files its S-1.

Lead Story

1,000 AI Insiders Just Asked Their Own Employers to Slow Down

In a historically unprecedented move, senior researchers and co-founders from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind jointly asked the US government to help pace the development of their own industry — before it outstrips humanity's ability to govern it.

On July 28, more than 1,000 employees at the world's three leading AI labs — including senior staff and co-founders — signed a public statement calling on the US government to build "technical and policy tools that could control automated AI progress in coordination with other companies and countries." The statement is remarkable not just for its content, but for who signed it: people whose daily work involves building the very systems they're asking governments to constrain.

The statement arrives at a charged moment. The US government has already installed a pre-release review process for the most powerful AI systems, following a June executive order. OpenAI's GPT-5.6 — the Sol, Terra, and Luna variants — became the first frontier model to clear that government review process before going public, a new precedent for how AI releases will work.

Whether this statement accelerates international coordination or gets lost in Washington's procedural machinery remains to be seen. But when the people building the tools say "slow down," it is no longer credible to dismiss that concern as alarmism from outsiders.

Models

Claude Opus 5 Is Now the Top-Ranked Model on the Intelligence Index

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24, and it immediately claimed the top slot on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index — described as "thoughtful and proactive" with frontier-level reasoning at roughly half the cost of Fable 5. Pricing lands at $5 input / $25 output per million tokens. Separately, Anthropic made Fable 5 permanent across Max and Team Premium plans on July 20, at approximately 50% of regular usage limits. The company appears to be executing a deliberate tiered strategy: Fable 5 for the elite use case, Opus 5 for production workloads that demand quality without breaking budgets.

Models

OpenAI Opens GPT-5.6 to 10,000 Academic Researchers — for Free

OpenAI announced today that it is giving scientists free access to GPT-5.6, its latest frontier model, starting with 10,000 researchers and expanding to 100,000 through 2027. GPT-5.6 — released in three variants named Sol, Terra, and Luna — is also notable as the first frontier model to complete the US government's new pre-release review process before public availability. The academic access program is framed as a contribution to scientific progress, though it also meaningfully extends OpenAI's reach into research institutions that currently default to open-source alternatives.

Hardware

South Korea Commits $880 Billion to AI and Semiconductor Dominance

South Korea unveiled an $880 billion, 10-year national investment plan spanning semiconductors, AI infrastructure, and robotics. Samsung and SK Hynix alone committed $518 billion toward new chip fabrication sites. The announcement is the largest single national AI investment plan ever declared by a government, dwarfing the EU's AI Act-era infrastructure commitments and positioning South Korea to compete directly with the US and China for semiconductor supremacy. The plan comes as AI inference demand is straining global chip supply, with every major lab actively searching for compute alternatives.

Models

DeepMind's Math AI Scores Top 1% on the International Mathematical Olympiad

Google DeepMind's mathematical reasoning system achieved a score placing it in the top 1% of International Mathematical Olympiad participants — problems that require genuine creative insight rather than pattern matching or brute-force search. The result is significant because math olympiad problems have long been regarded as a meaningful proxy for structured reasoning ability that generalizes beyond mathematics itself. Separately, multiple research teams published new work on AI-based prediction of protein dynamics — how proteins change shape over time, not just their static structure — which could substantially accelerate drug design timelines.

Policy

New York Passes Five AI Bills in One Session — A Regulatory Template Is Forming

New York legislators passed a kids chatbot safety bill, an AI training data transparency act, the FAIR News Act (requiring disclosure of AI-generated news content), a data center moratorium, and an outright ban on AI-assisted surveillance pricing — all in a single session. The scope reflects a shift from reactive to anticipatory regulation: rather than waiting for documented harms to accumulate, legislators are now building guardrails in advance. Observers expect New York's package to function as a model for other states, much as California's privacy laws shaped US data regulation.

Infrastructure

Anthropic Locks In 2 Gigawatts of AMD Compute in a $5B Equity Deal

Anthropic finalized a capacity agreement with AMD granting access to up to 2 gigawatts of MI450/Helios-generation compute — a deal structured to include up to $5 billion in AMD equity. The arrangement signals Anthropic's move to diversify its compute stack away from Nvidia dependence, joining a broader industry trend: DeepSeek is designing custom inference silicon, Meta has four custom chip generations in development, and Google runs its largest workloads on TPUs. The era of every frontier lab depending on one hardware supplier is ending.

Business

Google DeepMind Is Losing Talent — and Gemini 3.5 Pro Is Paying the Price

A Fortune investigation found that senior researcher departures and poor morale at Google DeepMind are contributing directly to delays in Gemini 3.5 Pro, which is now months behind its expected release schedule. The company's cash flow turned negative in the quarter, and investors are openly questioning whether Google can hold its position in the AI race. The lab has shipped smaller, more efficient models in the interim, but the flagship delay gives Anthropic and OpenAI additional runway to consolidate their positions at the frontier.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The builders are now the loudest safety voices. When over a thousand senior AI researchers publicly ask their governments to slow down their own industry, the safety debate has moved from the margins into the operational core of the field. Expect this to intensify government attention — and internal pressure — on the pace of frontier model releases.
  2. The capability-per-dollar race is accelerating faster than capability itself. Claude Opus 5 at the top of the intelligence index for half the price of Fable 5. Qwen3.8 at 2.4T parameters at a fraction of frontier pricing. The story of the second half of 2026 may be how fast quality trickles down, not just how high the ceiling rises.
  3. National AI infrastructure spending is entering a new magnitude. South Korea's $880B commitment is not a research budget — it is an industrial policy bet. Countries that treat AI as national infrastructure, not corporate R&D, will have structural advantages in the coming decade that policy alone cannot close.
  4. Government review of AI models is becoming standard — not optional. GPT-5.6 cleared the first formal US pre-release review. The June executive order set the framework. The next frontier models from every major lab will likely face the same process. The age of unilateral release is narrowing.
  5. The compute supply chain is fragmenting — and that is probably healthy. Anthropic on AMD. DeepSeek building custom silicon. Meta with four chip generations in flight. Nvidia remains dominant, but the ecosystem is diversifying fast. Competition at the infrastructure layer will eventually compress inference costs in ways that no software optimization alone can achieve.

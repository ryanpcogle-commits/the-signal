Lead Story

Google DeepMind's Brain Drain: Hassabis Steps Back as Top Researchers Flee to Rivals

In one of the most significant leadership reshuffles in AI history, Google is concentrating its AI command in California — while a generation of star researchers walk out the door.

Google has fundamentally reorganized its decade-old AI empire. Demis Hassabis, who co-founded DeepMind and built it into one of the world's premier AI labs, is stepping back from day-to-day operations to become Chairman of Google DeepMind and Alphabet's Chief Scientist. Koray Kavukcuoglu now runs the sprawling day-to-day AI research and operations, with Google concentrating leadership at its Mountain View headquarters in what Bloomberg is calling a "race against Anthropic and OpenAI."

The shakeup has coincided with a striking exodus of talent. Noam Shazeer, co-author of the landmark "Attention Is All You Need" Transformer paper, has moved to OpenAI. John Jumper, who led the AlphaFold project that won a Nobel Prize, has joined Anthropic. Most dramatically, Jeff Dean, Sanjay Ghemawat, Quoc Le, and Oriol Vinyals — four of Google's most decorated AI researchers — are leaving together to launch a new company called Discovery Loop.

The departures are a double blow: Google loses the institutional knowledge that shaped modern deep learning while simultaneously handing its fiercest rivals a talent windfall. The restructuring may signal that Google's leadership believes centralized, product-focused execution will matter more in the next phase than blue-sky research autonomy.

Models

Meta Ships Muse Glimmer — A Frontier-Class Model That Fits on Your Laptop

Meta released Muse Glimmer today, a distilled version of its Muse Spark 1.2 model designed to run on consumer hardware — including personal computers without dedicated AI accelerators. The 30-billion-parameter model is being described as the most capable locally runnable model yet, continuing Meta's open-weight strategy. Separately, Meta also announced a 30-billion-parameter agentic model that runs on a single GPU, aimed at developers building AI agents without enterprise-scale infrastructure.

Business

OpenAI Slashes GPT-5.6 Luna Pricing 80% — Frontier AI Enters Commodity Territory

OpenAI cut the price of GPT-5.6 Luna to $0.20 per million input tokens — an 80% reduction that makes frontier-class inference cheaper than many legacy APIs. OpenAI's current lineup spans Luna, Terra, and Sol, giving developers a tiered approach: routine tasks go to lower-cost models, hard reasoning work escalates to Sol. The move appears aimed at defending market share as Anthropic and Google both compress margins. OpenAI also announced that text chat on its free tier is now unlimited for all users.

Policy

EU AI Act High-Risk Provisions Are Now Enforceable — Fines Start at €15M

As of August 2, 2026, the EU AI Act's high-risk provisions are fully enforceable. Requirements covering risk management systems, data governance, human oversight, and conformity assessments now carry real penalties: up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. The transparency and labeling obligations under Article 50 — which require AI-generated content to be identified as such — became effective simultaneously. Legal teams across the tech industry have been scrambling to complete compliance documentation that many put off for over a year.

Hardware

Intel Raises $15 Billion to Chase the AI Infrastructure Race

Intel closed a $15 billion funding round aimed squarely at competing in the AI chip and infrastructure market. The raise is one of the largest in semiconductor history and signals Intel's intent to remain a player in AI compute despite losing significant ground to NVIDIA over the past three years. South Korea announced a parallel initiative: a 5 trillion won ($3.5 billion) government fund to support domestic semiconductor materials, equipment, and fabless chip design companies — underscoring how AI infrastructure has become a matter of national economic strategy.

Models

Google's Consumer Agents Will Call Stores and Buy Things for You

Google has begun rolling out consumer AI agents capable of making phone calls to local businesses, checking real-time inventory, and completing purchases on behalf of users — entirely by voice. The agents handle the full commerce loop autonomously: identify the item, find local availability, negotiate price or reserve stock, and confirm the transaction. It's the most aggressive productization yet of the "agentic" AI concept that has dominated research discussions for two years, and it puts Google in direct competition with emerging AI commerce startups.

Policy

Labs Sign Joint Letter Urging Government to Consider Slowing Frontier AI

Staff from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, and other leading AI organizations have co-signed a letter calling on the U.S. government to support international technical and governance mechanisms — including the ability to slow frontier AI development if necessary. The letter, described internally as reflecting concern about "recursive self-improvement" scenarios, is notable for its cross-lab consensus. The signatories are asking for neutral international frameworks that could coordinate a pause if any lab's systems begin exhibiting capabilities that outpace human oversight.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Google's talent exodus is accelerating the competitive race. Losing Shazeer, Jumper, Jeff Dean, and Vinyals in the same window is not a normal churn rate. These are the people who invented the architectures everyone else is building on — and they are now at OpenAI and Anthropic, or launching independently.
  2. Frontier model pricing is in structural freefall. GPT-5.6 Luna at $0.20/million tokens would have seemed implausible 18 months ago. When inference is near-free, the value shifts entirely to applications and data — not model access. Every business model built on API margins is under pressure.
  3. The EU AI Act now has teeth — and the grace period is over. Article 50 and high-risk provisions are live. Companies that treated compliance as a "figure it out later" problem are now facing real fines. This is the first major jurisdiction where AI regulation moves from policy paper to enforcement action.
  4. Consumer agents are entering real commerce, not just demos. Google's store-calling agents cross a threshold: AI is now completing financial transactions autonomously on behalf of consumers. The trust, liability, and error-correction frameworks for this don't yet exist — but the product is shipping anyway.
  5. Cross-lab AI safety consensus is unprecedented — and worth tracking. Staff from rival labs don't sign joint letters lightly. The fact that OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind employees co-signed a document asking governments to consider slowing development suggests shared internal concern that public statements haven't yet reflected.

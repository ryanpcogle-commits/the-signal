Lead Story

Anthropic Poaches AlphaFold's Creator and Launches Claude Science to Disrupt Drug Discovery

With a $400 million biotech acquisition and the hire of Nobel laureate John Jumper, Anthropic has opened a direct assault on the one frontier DeepMind has owned for a decade.

Anthropic launched Claude Science this week — a suite of AI tools targeting life sciences research, drug discovery, and biomedical R&D. The product is built on two major moves: the acquisition of Coefficient Bio for approximately $400 million, and the high-profile hire of John Jumper, the researcher who led Google DeepMind's AlphaFold team and won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on protein structure prediction.

CEO Dario Amodei has been vocal about his ambition to compress decades of life sciences R&D into years using AI. Claude Science is the operational bet on that thesis — offering tools designed for lab automation, literature synthesis, and molecular design workflows. Anthropic has been quietly building this capability for months, and analysts say DeepMind — which pioneered AI in structural biology — now finds itself playing catch-up in the very field it invented.

The defection of Jumper is more than symbolic. AlphaFold's static protein structure prediction transformed biology; the next leap is predicting how proteins move over time. Multiple research groups published this week on AI for protein dynamics, and Anthropic has positioned Claude Science to sit at the center of that emerging capability — arriving at exactly the right moment.

Models

Google Releases Three Gemini Models and Starts Training Gemini 4

Google released Gemini 3.6 Flash, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, and Gemini 3.5 Flash Cyber on July 21. The flagship 3.6 Flash arrives at $1.50 input / $7.50 output per million tokens with Computer Use built in and 17% fewer output tokens than its predecessor — a meaningful cost improvement. Flash-Lite undercuts at $0.30/$2.50/M for high-volume workloads. Flash Cyber is a security-tuned variant restricted to government and enterprise partners. More significantly, Google confirmed it has started its most ambitious pretraining run yet for Gemini 4, even as Gemini 3.5 Pro — the company's flagship model — remains unreleased after missing multiple target dates.

Models

DeepSeek V4 Drops Thursday — Open Kimi K3 Follows Sunday

Two of the most anticipated open-weight model releases of the year arrive within days of each other. DeepSeek V4 is scheduled for July 24, and Kimi K3 from Moonshot AI releases open weights on July 27. Kimi K3 is a 2.8-trillion-parameter frontier model with a 1-million-token context window and native vision — the largest open-weight model yet attempted by any lab. DeepSeek also separately disclosed it is designing custom inference silicon to reduce its dependence on Nvidia and Huawei chips, a move that could dramatically shift open-source AI economics if it succeeds.

Three Frontier Models in 24 Hours Trigger Inference Price War

GPT-5.6 (OpenAI), Grok 4.5 (xAI), and Muse Spark 1.1 (Meta) all launched within a 24-hour window earlier this month, triggering a wave of competitive repricing across the industry. Inference costs are now at historically low levels, with agentic AI moving from pilot projects into paid production workloads. Separately, OpenAI has proposed granting a 5% equity stake to the U.S. government and announced a $25 billion data center project in Texas — moves analysts read as the company laying groundwork for its anticipated IPO filing. Anthropic is reported to be preparing a similar raise of tens of billions.

Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 Go Global After Export Controls Lifted

The Trump administration lifted previous export restrictions on Anthropic's top-tier Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models after the company implemented new cybersecurity guardrails and reportedly reached a compliance agreement with the White House. The move opens major international markets to Anthropic for the first time, a significant commercial unlock that had been blocked since the models launched. The policy reversal follows broader U.S. government pressure on AI labs to build safeguards that preserve export access without compromising national security.

Research

DeepMind's Math AI Scores in the Top 1% on Olympiad Problems

Google DeepMind's latest mathematical reasoning system achieved a score placing it in the top 1% of human participants on International Mathematical Olympiad problems — the hardest formal math competition in the world. The result marks a sharp capability step from earlier AI math solvers, which could handle structured problems but struggled with the open-ended, multi-step proofs that IMO requires. A separate research result published this week introduced "selective activation sparsity," a training method that teaches models to activate only the most relevant parameters for each task — a potential path to dramatically more efficient frontier models.

Infrastructure

Meta's AI Moderation Beats Human Reviewers — But Is Deleting Real Accounts

Meta reported that its AI content moderation system now produces 13% fewer errors and catches 10% more policy violations than its human moderation teams across Instagram and Facebook. The numbers represent a genuine capability milestone for automated content enforcement at scale. However, a growing number of users report that the system has incorrectly deleted their accounts — a failure mode that is difficult to appeal and has no human backstop. The case is becoming a real-world test of whether accuracy statistics alone are the right measure for high-stakes automated decisions affecting millions of people.

Policy

Substack Partners with Pangram for AI Content Detection

Substack announced a partnership with Pangram Labs to offer in-platform AI detection for posts over 100 words — giving readers an estimate of how much of a piece was AI-generated. The feature is opt-in and framed as a transparency tool rather than an enforcement mechanism. The move reflects growing reader demand for disclosure norms as AI writing becomes indistinguishable from human prose at the surface level. South Korea separately announced plans for a national free AI access program, signaling that government-provided AI infrastructure is moving from concept to policy in multiple countries simultaneously.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI is going after biology's hardest problems. Anthropic's move into drug discovery — backed by the AlphaFold founder — signals that the most consequential AI bets are now in life sciences, not just software. DeepMind's decade-long head start in structural biology is under direct competitive pressure for the first time.
  2. Open-weight models are entering frontier territory. Kimi K3's 2.8 trillion parameters and Kimi's open release policy challenge the assumption that frontier-scale models are proprietary by necessity. If open weights perform at this level, the competitive moat for closed models narrows considerably.
  3. Inference is becoming a commodity. Three top-tier models launching in 24 hours, price wars across all major providers, and inference costs at historic lows mean the value is shifting from model access to application quality, data, and workflow integration.
  4. AI accuracy metrics are not the same as AI trustworthiness. Meta's moderation system outperforms humans on every headline metric — and is still incorrectly deleting real accounts. As AI replaces high-stakes human decisions, precision statistics alone are proving to be insufficient accountability frameworks.
  5. Mathematical reasoning is the new benchmark frontier. IMO top-1% performance from DeepMind's math system suggests that formal reasoning capabilities are advancing faster than general intelligence benchmarks have captured. Expect this to unlock new applications in science, engineering, and formal verification.

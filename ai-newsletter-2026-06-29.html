Lead Story

Anthropic Surpasses OpenAI in Valuation at $965 Billion — and Files Confidential IPO

The company that started as an "AI safety" spinout from OpenAI has now outvalued its former parent. Both are racing toward the most consequential IPO in tech history.

Anthropic closed a new financing round at a staggering $965 billion valuation this week, surpassing OpenAI's $852 billion private valuation from March. In the same announcement, Anthropic confirmed it has confidentially filed an S-1 with the SEC, setting the stage for a public listing that could redefine how markets value AI companies. The dual milestone — highest valuation and imminent IPO — is a remarkable turn for a company founded just three years ago by former OpenAI researchers who left over safety disagreements.

OpenAI, which filed its own confidential S-1 on June 8, is not sitting still. The company is pushing ahead with GPT-5.6, previewing three distinct model tiers — Sol (flagship for hard problems), Terra (balanced everyday use), and Luna (fast, high-volume) — all currently under government-gated access as part of Washington's new frontier AI review process. ChatGPT now serves more than 900 million weekly active users, with roughly one in five queries already expressing direct commercial intent.

The valuation gap between the two companies may be partly explained by what happened earlier this month: the U.S. government imposed export controls on Anthropic's most powerful models, Fable 5 and Mythos 5, citing national security concerns and barring foreign access. The restriction was partially lifted for critical infrastructure defenders, but the episode underscored how quickly frontier AI models are being treated as strategic national assets rather than commercial products.

Business

Google DeepMind Is Bleeding Talent — to Anthropic and OpenAI

In one of the most striking talent shifts in recent AI history, Google DeepMind is losing researchers to its direct rivals at an accelerating rate. John Jumper — a DeepMind vice president and co-recipient of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his work on AlphaFold — is heading to Anthropic, along with Jonas Adler, who led Google's AI coding work inside DeepMind, and Alexander Pritzel, who worked on pretraining. Meanwhile, Noam Shazeer, one of the co-authors of the landmark 2017 paper "Attention Is All You Need" and co-lead of the Gemini project, is joining OpenAI. The exodus follows Google's decision to pivot DeepMind's focus heavily toward coding applications — a strategic choice that appears to have alienated researchers whose interests lie elsewhere.

Policy

White House Issues New Frontier AI Security Order — With Teeth

The Biden-era executive order on AI safety has been superseded. The White House released "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security" on June 29, establishing a formal frontier AI review process that gives Washington oversight of models above a capability threshold before they can be released or exported. The order directly triggered the export restrictions on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models and the government-gated rollout of OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family. Dario Amodei and Demis Hassabis called for a U.S.-led AI coalition at the G7 summit alongside OpenAI CEO Sam Altman — signaling that even the companies most affected by the rules are supporting tighter governance frameworks.

Models

Gemini 3.5 Pro Slips to July — But the Specs Are Extraordinary

Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro missed its Google I/O June deadline and is now targeting a July 2026 general availability release. The delay hasn't dimmed expectations: the model ships with a 2-million-token context window and a specialized "Deep Think" reasoning mode designed for complex, multi-step problems. The companion Gemini 3.5 Flash model is already live and drawing praise for its speed-to-cost ratio. The slip may reflect quality-bar decisions rather than capability gaps — though the talent departures from DeepMind are drawing scrutiny as a potential contributing factor.

Infrastructure

Microsoft Scout: An Always-On AI Agent That Lives Inside Microsoft 365

Microsoft unveiled Scout, an autonomous AI agent built on the OpenClaw framework that operates continuously across Teams, Outlook, calendars, OneDrive, and SharePoint. Unlike point-in-time assistants, Scout monitors workflows in the background, coordinates schedules proactively, flags bottlenecks before they surface, and carries out routine tasks without prompting. The launch arrives alongside Microsoft's new proprietary model family: MAI-Code-1-Flash for code generation and MAI-Thinking-1 for reasoning — both positioned as cost-efficient Azure alternatives to third-party frontier models. The agent-plus-model strategy signals Microsoft's intent to compete on the full stack, not just access to OpenAI's APIs.

Business

A24 and Google DeepMind Sign $75 Million Film AI Partnership

Independent film studio A24 — known for Everything Everywhere All at Once, Midsommar, and Hereditary — has entered a $75 million partnership with Google DeepMind to develop AI tools for film production and distribution. The deal explicitly commits to preserving filmmakers' creative control, positioning the tools as workflow support rather than creative replacement. It is the largest declared AI partnership between a frontier AI lab and a major creative content company to date, and a significant signal that Hollywood's posture toward AI has shifted from resistance to selective adoption.

Robotics

Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 Powers Boston Dynamics' Spot in Industrial Deployments

Boston Dynamics announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration enables enhanced spatial reasoning, autonomous decision-making in unstructured environments, and continuous learning from field experience. The deployment targets industrial inspection, hazardous environment monitoring, and complex facility management — sectors where human presence is costly or dangerous. It is the most substantive integration of a frontier multimodal model into a commercial robotics platform to date.

Models

OpenAI Retires GPT-4.5 as GPT-5.6 Family Previews Begin

As of June 26, GPT-4.5 is no longer available in ChatGPT — including for custom GPTs. Existing conversations that relied on GPT-4.5 are now automatically handled by GPT-5.5, marking a clean generational transition. The retirement clears the deck for OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family — Sol, Terra, and Luna — which are currently in government-reviewed preview and expected to reach general availability within weeks. The three-tier architecture reflects a deliberate effort to address the full cost spectrum, from low-latency consumer interactions (Luna) to enterprise-grade reasoning tasks (Sol).

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. The IPO race is a valuation arms race. Anthropic at $965B and OpenAI at $852B are both eyeing public markets. Whichever lists first will set the pricing benchmark for AI companies globally — and the gap between them is now thin enough that the order of filing matters enormously.
  2. Talent gravity is shifting away from Google. When a Nobel laureate and the co-author of the transformer paper leave in the same month, it is not coincidence. DeepMind's coding pivot is a strategic bet that may be costing it the researchers who built its reputation for foundational science.
  3. Export controls are the new AI governance lever. Governments are treating frontier models the way they treat weapons technology. The partial ban on Fable 5 and Mythos 5 is a preview of a world where model capability thresholds trigger automatic regulatory review — reshaping how labs time and structure their releases.
  4. The agentic workplace is no longer theoretical. Microsoft Scout running continuously across the Microsoft 365 ecosystem is not a chatbot. It is the first mass-market always-on AI agent. The shift from "ask AI a question" to "AI operates in the background on your behalf" is now commercially live.
  5. Creative industries are choosing selective adoption over resistance. A24's $75M DeepMind deal shows the most respected independent studio in Hollywood has concluded that AI-assisted production is inevitable — and that the strategic question is whether to shape the tools or have them imposed. Expect more studios to follow.

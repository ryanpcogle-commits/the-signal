Lead Story

Anthropic Is Now the Quiet Revenue King — and It Just Lured Away Andrej Karpathy

While OpenAI courts headlines, Anthropic has overtaken it on revenue, snagged the industry's most iconic researcher, and launched a model that makes frontier AI affordable for the first time.

Anthropic quietly became the AI industry's revenue leader this month. The company is on track for roughly $47 billion in annualized revenue — nearly double OpenAI's publicly stated figures — driven by Claude Code's enterprise dominance and deep API adoption. More strikingly, Anthropic says it expects to be profitable as early as this year, a full year ahead of OpenAI's own forecast.

The talent story is equally dramatic. Anthropic has added Andrej Karpathy — the legendary former Tesla AI director and OpenAI founding member — to its ranks, alongside John Jumper, the Nobel Prize-winning DeepMind researcher whose AlphaFold work transformed structural biology, and startup founder Tom Blomfield. It is the most concentrated recruitment run any AI lab has executed in a single quarter.

To match the commercial momentum, Anthropic launched Claude Sonnet 5 this week, delivering near-Opus 4.8 performance at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens — available at that rate through August 31. After the US Commerce Department lifted export restrictions earlier this month, Anthropic also restored global access to Claude Fable 5, adding enhanced cybersecurity classifiers the company calls "the strongest safeguards we have ever shipped."

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Arrives in Three Sizes — Sol Is the Flagship

OpenAI released the GPT-5.6 model family after a Commerce Department review process that cleared exports on a customer-by-customer basis. The lineup has three tiers: Luna (fast, cheap tier at $1 per million input tokens), Terra (GPT-5.5-level quality at half the cost of Sol), and Sol (flagship, $5 per million tokens), which introduces a new Ultra subagent mode and a Max reasoning-effort setting designed for scientific and long-running coding tasks. All three share a 1-million-token context window and a February 2026 knowledge cutoff. Sol reportedly outperforms Claude Fable 5 on long-running agentic benchmarks.

DeepMind's Math AI Scores in the Top 1% on Olympic-Level Problems

Google DeepMind's latest mathematical reasoning system achieved a score placing it in the top 1% of human competitors on International Mathematical Olympiad problems — a milestone that was considered years away as recently as early 2025. The system goes beyond pattern-matching, solving novel proof-based problems that require multi-step symbolic reasoning. The result arrives as DeepMind faces scrutiny over senior researcher departures, including John Jumper's move to Anthropic, raising questions about whether the lab can sustain its scientific edge.

Agentic AI Has Left the Lab — Long-Running Tasks Are Up 10× at OpenAI

OpenAI reported a fivefold increase in active Codex users in H1 2026, paired with a tenfold jump in agent tasks running longer than eight hours. Median developer output measured against AI assistance surged between 13× and 50× year-over-year. The figures confirm what infrastructure teams have been seeing for months: AI is no longer being used as a chat tool but as an autonomous worker completing overnight jobs. Separately, Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, released July 16, and Mira Murati's Thinking Machines lab, which shipped an open-weight model called Inkling, are adding further supply to a market where new frontier models now arrive on average every three days.

Microsoft's Project Perception Targets the Crowded AI Security Market

Microsoft announced Project Perception, a multi-model AI security platform that draws on Anthropic, OpenAI, and Microsoft's own models simultaneously to detect threats. The product is positioned explicitly as a lower-cost alternative to Anthropic's Mythos 5 security suite, and Microsoft is leaning on its global distribution network as the differentiator. The launch illustrates how quickly the AI security market has stratified: within months of frontier models gaining agentic capabilities, a secondary market of tools designed to watch those agents has emerged.

Oracle Is Cutting 30,000 Jobs to Fund the $500B Stargate Build-Out

Oracle confirmed it is eliminating up to 30,000 positions — one of the largest tech layoffs of 2026 — to redirect capital into the $500 billion Stargate AI infrastructure partnership. The restructuring is the clearest single data point yet on how AI hardware investment is being financed across the industry: not from new capital alone, but from redirecting existing headcount spend. Oracle's move follows Atlassian's similar pivot earlier this year and signals that the labor-for-compute tradeoff has become a standard board-level decision.

Research

AI Discovers Rare-Earth-Free Permanent Magnets — a Materials Science First

Researchers at the Ames Laboratory used a physics-trained AI model called DuctGPT to discover a new class of rare-earth-free permanent magnets. Unlike prior generative approaches that merely suggested candidate materials, DuctGPT was designed to understand underlying electromagnetic physics, model production costs, and factor in component sourcing constraints before proposing formulations. Rare-earth magnets are currently essential for EV motors, wind turbines, and defense systems — and almost entirely sourced from China. A domestically producible alternative would have significant geopolitical and industrial implications.

Five Eyes Nations Jointly Flag Five Agentic AI Risk Categories

Cybersecurity agencies from the United States, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom released joint guidance on agentic AI services, identifying five categories of risk: excessive permissions, prompt injection from untrusted content, insufficient human oversight, lack of audit trails, and cascading failures in multi-agent pipelines. The guidance comes as the CEOs of OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Anthropic are each publishing detailed positions on AI regulation — an unusual alignment among competitors. Meanwhile, the Future of Life Institute's 2026 AI Safety Index awarded Anthropic the highest grade at C+, while OpenAI and Google DeepMind both received a C.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Talent is concentrating at Anthropic at an alarming rate for rivals. Karpathy, Jumper, and Blomfield all defected to the same lab inside one quarter. When the most-respected researchers in the field converge on one destination, it tends to predict where the next breakthrough comes from.
  2. Inference is getting cheap fast — and triggering a demand explosion. GPT-5.6 starts at $1 per million tokens. Sonnet 5 at $2. The price floor has collapsed. Every enterprise that was "waiting to see" is now running the numbers on full deployment.
  3. Agentic AI is in production, not pilot. Ten-times growth in overnight agent tasks at OpenAI is not an experiment — it is a workflow replacement. The question is no longer whether AI agents will handle knowledge work autonomously but how quickly procurement and legal can catch up.
  4. AI-funded workforce restructuring is becoming a template. Oracle cutting 30,000 to fund Stargate. Atlassian's 10% reduction earlier this year. The pattern is consistent: companies are treating headcount as a funding mechanism for compute spend. This will accelerate as models improve.
  5. Safety grades are embarrassingly low — and now public. The best grade any frontier lab earned on the 2026 AI Safety Index was a C+. With CEOs publicly converging on similar regulatory prescriptions, there is political will. What is still missing is a credible enforcement mechanism.

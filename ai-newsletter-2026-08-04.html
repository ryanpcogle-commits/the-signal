Lead Story

OpenAI's Astra Solves 10 Open Math Problems — for $2,000

An internal version of OpenAI's next major model cracked a decade of unsolved mathematics and theoretical computer science, posting formal Lean proofs on GitHub. AI just crossed from doing tasks to doing original research.

On August 1st, OpenAI quietly announced that an internal version of Astra — its next major model, still unreleased to the public — had solved ten open problems spanning mathematics and theoretical computer science. The solutions weren't approximations or plausible-sounding guesses: Astra produced verified formal proofs in Lean, the same proof-checking language used by mathematicians, and posted them to GitHub where anyone can audit them.

The cost of the entire compute run: approximately $2,000. These were problems that had stumped human experts for years, in some cases decades. The achievement signals something the field has anticipated but not witnessed at scale — AI doing genuinely novel intellectual work, not pattern-matching on known solutions.

Researchers noted the connecting thread across this week's AI news: "AI crossed a visible threshold from doing tasks to doing original research." If Astra's math results transfer to biology, materials science, and chip design — fields where open problems have physical-world consequences — the implications extend far beyond any single benchmark.

Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 Is Here — Fable-Level Intelligence at Half the Price

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24th, positioning it as the most capable widely available model in the Claude family. According to Anthropic, Opus 5 approaches Claude Fable 5-level intelligence — the company's frontier research model — at roughly half the inference cost, with meaningfully stronger performance on coding tasks, multi-step agentic workflows, and self-verification. The release moves the accessible performance frontier significantly: what required Fable-tier spending last month is now an Opus-tier price.

GPT-5.6 Clears US Government Review Before Public Launch

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 — released in three variants named Sol, Terra, and Luna — became the first frontier AI model to clear a customer-by-customer US government security review before going public. The vetting process, conducted with federal agencies, marks a significant procedural shift: rather than releasing models and managing government access afterward, OpenAI embedded the review process into the pre-launch pipeline. Defense and intelligence customers will receive Sol, the most capable variant; Terra and Luna target enterprise and consumer use cases respectively.

Models

Google's Gemini 3.6 Flash Targets Efficiency Over Scale

Google's Gemini 3.6 Flash, released July 21st, is a deliberate bet on doing more with less. The model reduces wasted reasoning steps and redundant tool calls on coding and multi-step tasks — rather than chasing raw benchmark scores through scale. Available in the Gemini app, API, and enterprise platforms, Flash is designed for the latency-sensitive workloads that enterprise developers actually deploy. Google is framing it as evidence that the efficiency race is now as important as the capability race.

Policy

Frontier Labs Sign "Pacing" Letter — and Their Own Companies Endorse It

Verified employees from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, and AI safety organization Thinky co-signed a letter urging the US government to support international frameworks for deliberately slowing AI development if safety thresholds are crossed. Unusually, both OpenAI and Anthropic issued formal corporate endorsements — not just employee signatories. The letter references fears of "recursive self-improvement" and cites the risk that competitive pressure will override caution. Critics called it performative; supporters called it the clearest industry signal yet that frontier labs are genuinely worried about what comes next.

Business

Google DeepMind's Talent Drain Is Now a Leadership Problem

Fortune reports that poor morale at Google DeepMind is contributing to delayed model releases and raising questions about whether the lab can maintain its position at the frontier. Notable departures include influential researcher Noam Shazeer, who joined OpenAI, and Nobel Prize in Chemistry winner John Jumper, who moved to Anthropic. Multiple current employees told Fortune that the lab's bureaucratic structure and unclear product direction are making it harder to retain the researchers who made AlphaFold and Gemini possible. Google has not publicly responded.

Claude Sonnet 5 Becomes the Default for Every Free and Pro User Worldwide

Anthropic quietly made Claude Sonnet 5 the default model for all Free and Pro subscribers — its largest mass-market model push of the year. The rollout replaces the prior default (Claude Sonnet 4.6) with no required user action. Anthropic called it the company's "biggest shift in default intelligence for everyday users" since Claude 3. The move puts a significantly more capable model in front of tens of millions of users who never adjust settings, and signals Anthropic's confidence that Sonnet 5 is production-stable at consumer scale.

Palantir CEO: Frontier Labs Are "Too Untrustworthy" for Enterprise AI

Following strong Q2 2026 earnings, Palantir CEO Alex Karp used the company's earnings call to warn enterprises that the leading AI frontier labs are structurally unable to be trusted with sensitive business data. Karp argued that labs optimizing for benchmark performance and rapid release cycles are the wrong partners for regulated industries — finance, defense, healthcare — and positioned Palantir's closed, audited deployment model as the enterprise alternative. The comments landed as Anthropic and OpenAI are both actively competing for large enterprise and government contracts.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. AI is now doing original science, not just accelerating it. Solving open math problems with formal proofs is a qualitatively different claim than "AI helps researchers." If this transfers to biology and materials science, the bottleneck shifts from human insight to compute access.
  2. The efficiency race is as important as the capability race. Gemini 3.6 Flash cutting wasted reasoning steps, GPT-5.6 tiering by use case, Opus 5 at half the cost of Fable — every lab is now competing on $/performance, not just peak performance.
  3. The government-cleared AI pipeline is becoming standard. GPT-5.6's pre-launch government review is a template others will follow. Enterprise and defense buyers will increasingly demand security provenance before deployment.
  4. Talent concentration is accelerating lab divergence. DeepMind losing Shazeer and Jumper to rivals is not just a morale story — it's a capability redistribution. The researchers who built the foundational systems are now inside competing labs.
  5. The pacing coalition is a real signal, not just PR. When OpenAI and Anthropic issue corporate endorsements for slowing AI development, something has shifted internally. The question is whether the letter has teeth — or whether competitive pressure simply overrides it, as it always has.

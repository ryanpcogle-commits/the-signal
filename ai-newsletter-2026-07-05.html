Lead Story

OpenAI Files Confidentially for IPO — At $730 Billion, It Would Be the Decade's Biggest Tech Offering

The company that started as a nonprofit research lab is now preparing to go public with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. If the September timeline holds, it will mark the end of an era — and the beginning of a very different one.

OpenAI has filed confidentially for an initial public offering, working with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley on a potential listing as soon as September 2026. The company, last valued at $730 billion in private markets, would rank among the largest technology IPOs in history. CEO Sam Altman, who Fortune describes as seeking a "new world order for AI," is simultaneously negotiating a proposed 5% equity stake for the U.S. government — an unusual arrangement that would give Washington a direct financial interest in the country's most powerful AI lab.

The IPO comes as OpenAI deepens its infrastructure commitments. The company announced a $25 billion data center project in Texas in collaboration with Oracle and Vantage — a bet that compute sovereignty will be as important as model quality in the years ahead. Meanwhile, Anthropic is also widely expected to pursue its own public offering, setting up what could be a dual-IPO moment for frontier AI.

The timing reflects a broader shift: AI is no longer a research project or a startup story. It is infrastructure, and the public markets are about to price it that way.

Claude Sonnet 5 Launches as the New Default — Near Opus-Level at Half the Cost

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5 on June 30, making it the new default model for Free and Pro users. The model scores 63.2% on agentic coding benchmarks compared to Sonnet 4.6's 58.1%, and features a 1-million-token context window. Introductory pricing is set at $2 per million input tokens through August 31 — delivering near-Opus 4.8 performance at a fraction of the cost. For most users, this is the biggest capability upgrade they've received without lifting a finger.

Models

Fable 5 Goes Global After U.S. Lifts AI Export Controls

The U.S. Department of Commerce lifted export restrictions on Anthropic's Fable 5 and Mythos 5 models on June 30, following the company's implementation of new cybersecurity guardrails and a reported agreement with the Trump administration. As of July 1, Fable 5 is available worldwide on Claude.ai, the Claude Platform API, Claude Code, and Claude Cowork. The model achieves 80.3% on SWE-Bench Pro — the highest score of any publicly available model — and is priced at $10/$50 per million tokens, purpose-built for long-horizon agentic runs.

OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Family: Sol, Terra, and Luna — But Not Yet for Everyone

OpenAI previewed its next-generation GPT-5.6 model family on June 26 with three tiers: Sol (flagship, $5/$30 per million tokens), Terra (balanced everyday use, $2.50/$15), and Luna (fast and affordable, $1/$6). Sol achieved 91.9% on the Terminal-Bench 2.1 benchmark. The catch: all three variants remain gated behind government and enterprise access for roughly 20 trusted organizations, with general availability expected "in the coming weeks." OpenAI's own chip — the custom Jalapeño inference processor — is powering this generation's infrastructure.

Policy

California Signs State-Wide Claude Deal — 300,000 Employees Get AI Access

Governor Gavin Newsom signed a deal granting every California state agency, city, and county access to Claude at a 50% discount. The agreement covers approximately 300,000 state employees and marks one of the largest government AI adoption commitments in U.S. history. The move signals that AI assistants are transitioning from departmental experiments to standard government productivity tools — with all the procurement, compliance, and accountability implications that entails.

Policy

White House and UN Move in Parallel on AI Governance Frameworks

Two major governance initiatives launched in parallel this week. The Financial Times reports the White House is in advanced talks with AI companies — including Google — to finalize voluntary standards for frontier model releases, with an announcement possible as soon as July 7. Separately, the UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance convened in Geneva on July 6, bringing Member States together to discuss international approaches to managing the technology. After years of fragmented national efforts, coordinated standards may finally be within reach.

Robotics

Boston Dynamics Integrates Gemini Robotics Into Spot and Orbit AI Platform

Boston Dynamics announced a partnership with Google Cloud and DeepMind to integrate Gemini Robotics-ER 1.6 into its Spot robot dog and Orbit AI visual inspection platform. The integration enables Spot to understand and respond to natural-language commands in dynamic industrial environments, while Orbit gains multimodal inspection capabilities for identifying equipment anomalies in manufacturing and energy facilities. Physical AI is quietly becoming enterprise software.

Business

OpenAI Proposes 5% U.S. Government Stake — A New Model for AI and State Power

In what analysts are calling an unprecedented arrangement, OpenAI has reportedly proposed granting a 5% equity stake to the U.S. government as part of its IPO preparations and a broader strategic alignment with the Trump administration. The proposal would make Washington a direct financial beneficiary of OpenAI's commercial success — and, critics argue, give the government a structural incentive to favor OpenAI in regulatory decisions. No comparable arrangement exists in modern tech history, and the precedent it sets for other AI labs is already being closely watched.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Frontier AI is going public — and changing everything. OpenAI's IPO at $730B will force the company to answer to public shareholders for the first time. Safety decisions, compute spending, and competitive moves will all face earnings-call scrutiny. The calculus of running a frontier lab is about to shift.
  2. Export controls are a geopolitical lever, not just a tech policy tool. The U.S. lifting restrictions on Fable 5 in exchange for cybersecurity guardrails shows governments are treating AI model access like weapons export agreements. Expect this dynamic to define international AI competition for years.
  3. Government is the biggest new AI customer. California's 300,000-employee Claude deal and OpenAI's proposed 5% government stake represent two very different models of state-AI relationships — procurement vs. equity. Both signal that government is no longer a bystander in AI deployment.
  4. Cost compression is reshaping who can build with AI. Claude Sonnet 5 at $2/M tokens delivering near-Opus performance, Luna at $1/M — the price-performance ratio for top-tier models has roughly tripled in 12 months. Applications that were cost-prohibitive six months ago are now viable at consumer scale.
  5. Robotics is entering the enterprise, not just the lab. Boston Dynamics + Gemini. Atlas in production manufacturing. Grok 4.5 running at SpaceX and Tesla. The physical-AI wave that NVIDIA called the "ChatGPT moment" last year is now showing up in actual enterprise deployments. The gap between demo and real work is closing faster than expected.

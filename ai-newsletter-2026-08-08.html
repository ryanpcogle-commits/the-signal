Lead Story

Google's AI Power Shifts West — and Its Best Researchers Follow Rivals East

Google moved its AI command to Mountain View this week, demoting Demis Hassabis from day-to-day control. Meanwhile, two of his most celebrated researchers quietly defected to OpenAI and Anthropic.

In a sweeping restructuring announced August 6, Google concentrated its artificial intelligence leadership at its Mountain View, California campus, appointing Koray Kavukcuoglu to run AI research and operations globally. Demis Hassabis, the Nobel laureate who built DeepMind from a London startup into one of the world's premier research institutions, stepped back from day-to-day management to become Chairman of Google DeepMind and Alphabet's Chief Scientist — an elder-statesman role with reduced operational authority.

The internal shakeup arrived alongside a damaging talent signal: Noam Shazeer, co-author of the original "Attention Is All You Need" paper that invented the Transformer architecture, moved to OpenAI. And John Jumper, who led the AlphaFold project that revolutionized protein-structure prediction and won a share of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, joined Anthropic. Losing two Nobel-tier contributors in the same week is a reputational blow that no org chart reshuffling fully absorbs.

Bloomberg's reporting framed the California consolidation as Google's attempt to gain momentum in its race against OpenAI and Anthropic — but critics note the move risks undermining the scientific independence that made DeepMind exceptional. The Google shakeup "complicates" the race, Bloomberg noted, precisely because it happened at the same moment its best people were walking out the door.

OpenAI Cuts GPT-5.6 Luna Pricing by 80% — Frontier AI Gets Cheap

OpenAI slashed the price of GPT-5.6 Luna to $0.20 per million input tokens this week — an 80 percent reduction that makes frontier-quality AI accessible for large-scale workloads that would have been economically unviable months ago. The move formalizes OpenAI's three-tier strategy: Luna for high-volume, cost-sensitive tasks; Terra for balanced reasoning; and Sol for the most demanding complex queries. Analysts see the price cut as a direct answer to Anthropic's competitive pressure and as a bid to lock in enterprise API customers before year-end contract cycles close.

Claude Fable 5 Scores 80.3% on SWE-Bench Pro, Sets August Coding High

Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 reached 80.3 percent on SWE-Bench Pro, the hardest publicly available software-engineering benchmark, making it the top-reported coding score for the month. The result signals that AI systems are now resolving real-world GitHub issues — not toy examples — at a rate that challenges junior-to-mid-level engineering output. Anthropic also noted that Fable 5's reasoning architecture allows it to decompose multi-step coding, research, and operations tasks autonomously, positioning it directly against OpenAI's Sol tier.

Policy

OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Sit Down at the White House on AI Safety

On August 3, representatives from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google attended a White House AI safety summit — the first such convening since the Biden-era executive orders were partially rolled back. Details of the agreements reached remain sparse, but sources familiar with the discussions said the labs outlined voluntary reporting commitments for models above a capability threshold. The meeting followed a joint letter cosigned by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, and Thinking Machines urging governments and the industry itself to "pace" AI development in response to concerns about rapid self-improvement capabilities.

Hardware

Your Phone Can Now Run a 70-Billion-Parameter Model — No Cloud Required

Apple's A20 Bionic and Qualcomm's Snapdragon 8 Gen 5 — shipping in devices this fall — feature Neural Processing Units capable of running 70-billion-parameter models entirely on-device. The milestone effectively means the compute wall between cloud and edge has collapsed at the 70B class. Privacy-first applications, offline assistants, and enterprise tools that cannot send data to external servers are all now unlocked. Analysts predict on-device inference will account for 40 percent of all AI compute by late 2027, fundamentally pressuring cloud API revenue models.

Infrastructure

NAVER and NVIDIA Break Ground on 55-Megawatt Sovereign AI Center in South Korea

NAVER Cloud and NVIDIA announced a 55-megawatt sovereign AI data center outside Seoul, the largest dedicated AI infrastructure project in South Korea's history. The facility is designed to process Korean-language data without routing it through U.S. or European cloud providers — a direct response to enterprise and government buyers who list data residency and regional compute control as procurement requirements. The deal is part of a broader NVIDIA "Sovereign AI" push in which it has now committed infrastructure partnerships in 14 countries in 2026 alone.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Google's talent drain is the real story behind the reorg. Moving leadership to California is a management decision. Losing Shazeer and Jumper — two Nobel-linked researchers — in the same week is an institutional signal. The best people follow interesting problems and winning cultures, not org charts.
  2. Frontier AI is going commodity — fast. GPT-5.6 Luna at $0.20 per million tokens is a 99%-plus reduction from where frontier model pricing stood two years ago. When intelligence gets this cheap, the moat shifts entirely to distribution, data, and trust.
  3. On-device AI will break the cloud API model by 2027. 70B parameter models running on consumer chips is not a demo — it's the hardware reality shipping this fall. Every cloud AI product that depends on data leaving the device now has a structural competitor in the user's pocket.
  4. Sovereign AI is the new data center sales motion. NVIDIA's 14-country sovereign AI buildout in 2026 alone shows that "where does the data live?" has become the enterprise and government procurement question. Whoever controls regional compute wins regional trust.
  5. The labs are quietly coordinating on pace — watch for formal agreements. The White House meeting and the multi-lab "pace" letter suggest the frontier labs are moving toward informal coordination on release cadence for the most capable systems. That's a new dynamic that didn't exist a year ago.

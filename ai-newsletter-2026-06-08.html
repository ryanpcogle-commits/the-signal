Apple's WWDC Blows Up the AI Platform Map — Siri Gets Gemini, Your iPhone Gets Claude

In Tim Cook's final WWDC keynote as Apple CEO, the company unveiled a Gemini-powered Siri and a multi-AI Extensions system that lets users swap in Claude or ChatGPT — making the smartphone the new AI battleground.

Apple WWDC 2026 delivered the most consequential AI announcement in the iPhone's history. Tim Cook walked onto the Apple Park stage to reveal a completely rebuilt Siri, now powered by a custom 1.2-trillion-parameter Google Gemini model licensed for approximately $1 billion per year. The new Siri features a standalone app with a "Search or Ask" interface, Dynamic Island integration, and full personal context access — emails, photos, calendar, files — without leaving the device.

The bigger strategic move was the Extensions system. For the first time, iPhone users can choose their AI model the way they choose a default browser: Google Gemini, OpenAI's ChatGPT, or Anthropic's Claude are all available from day one. Apple gains the Gemini engine for Siri while deliberately keeping rivals in the ecosystem — a platform bet that positions the iPhone as neutral AI infrastructure rather than a locked garden.

iOS 27, macOS 27, iPadOS 27, and watchOS 27 betas all shipped the same afternoon. Apple stock moved higher on the confirmed Gemini deal, with the $1B annual license now appearing on-balance-sheet. Microsoft was also up on the same day, after separately finalizing its 11,000-model Foundry catalog — which includes Claude Opus 4.8 — at its Build 2026 event.

OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft CEOs Urge Congress on Biosecurity

The heads of OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, and Microsoft AI signed a rare joint open letter to US Congress on June 5, calling for mandatory screening requirements on synthetic DNA providers. The letter warns that advances in AI are eroding the technical barriers previously needed to synthesize dangerous biological material. The four companies — fierce commercial rivals — presenting a unified front on biosecurity signals that labs view AI-enabled biorisks as an existential concern, not just a regulatory nuisance. Separately, Congress is reviewing a 269-page draft of the Great American Artificial Intelligence Act, the most comprehensive federal AI framework ever introduced.

Infrastructure

Anthropic Secures All of SpaceX/xAI's Colossus Data Center

Anthropic struck a deal to use the entire compute capacity at xAI's Colossus 1 data center in Memphis, Tennessee, gaining access to over 300 megawatts from more than 220,000 Nvidia GPUs. The facility was originally built by Elon Musk's xAI and is widely considered the single largest GPU cluster in the world. The agreement marks an extraordinary moment — one of OpenAI's most direct competitors locking up compute resources from infrastructure associated with Musk's own AI venture, effectively outbidding xAI on its home turf.

MiniMax M3 Cuts Per-Token Compute by 95% With 1M-Token Context

Chinese AI lab MiniMax released its M3 model, claiming a dramatic efficiency breakthrough: per-token compute requirements are slashed to just 1/20th of previous generations, while supporting up to 1 million tokens of context. The model delivers 9× faster prefilling and 15× faster decoding compared to its predecessor. If the benchmarks hold up under independent testing, M3 would fundamentally shift the cost calculus for deploying long-context reasoning — making applications that were previously cost-prohibitive viable at consumer scale.

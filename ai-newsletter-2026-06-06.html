Lead Story

Anthropic Files for IPO at a $965 Billion Valuation — Leapfrogging OpenAI

The maker of Claude has quietly filed confidentially for a public offering, setting up what could be the most consequential tech IPO since Meta. Claude Code gets the credit.

Anthropic closed its latest financing round at a staggering $965 billion valuation, officially surpassing OpenAI as the most valuable AI startup on the planet — and this week filed confidentially for an IPO that would put the company on a path to a potential trillion-dollar public listing. The milestone marks a remarkable reversal: as recently as two years ago, Anthropic was widely seen as a distant challenger to OpenAI's commercial dominance.

Analysts point to a single product as the engine of Anthropic's rise: Claude Code, the AI coding assistant that has become the tool of choice for professional software engineers. CNBC reported that "Anthropic has zoomed ahead of the field, largely thanks to Claude Code," which commands premium pricing and high renewal rates across enterprise customers. The product turned what was a safety-focused research lab into a revenue machine.

The IPO filing adds a new dimension to the AI industry's ongoing arms race. With Anthropic heading toward the public markets, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and xAI will face increased pressure to demonstrate comparable financial trajectories — or follow suit with their own listings.

Microsoft Build Delivers MAI-Code-1-Flash — Its First Homegrown Coding Model

At its Build 2026 developer conference in San Francisco this week, Microsoft unveiled MAI-Code-1-Flash, the company's first in-house model designed to turn written descriptions into working source code. The announcement signals Microsoft's intent to reduce its dependence on OpenAI for AI capabilities in developer tools. Microsoft also revealed MAI-Transcribe-1.5 — which it claims beats both Gemini and OpenAI on transcription benchmarks — and MAI-Image-2.5, ranked above Gemini in the Chatbot Arena leaderboard.

Gemini 3.5 Flash Goes GA: Frontier Intelligence at 4× the Speed

Google's Gemini 3.5 Flash is now generally available, with the company positioning it as a frontier-level model that runs at four times the speed of comparable alternatives. The release follows Google's I/O 2026 conference in May, where Sundar Pichai announced a price cut to the Gemini Ultra subscription — down from $250 to $200 per month — as Google pushes to win enterprise customers away from OpenAI and Anthropic. Gemini's monthly active user base has doubled to 900 million over the past year, giving Google a formidable distribution moat even as rivals race ahead on capability benchmarks.

Claude Opus 4.8 Adds Effort Control, Faster Mode, and Top Benchmark Results

Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 4.8 this week, the latest iteration of its flagship model. New features include effort control — letting developers dial reasoning depth up or down to trade off cost and latency — along with dynamic workflows, a cheaper "fast mode" powered by Claude Opus under the hood, and improved agentic coding capabilities. Early benchmark results from Artificial Analysis place Opus 4.8 at the top of its class across reasoning and coding tasks, reinforcing Anthropic's position as the preferred model provider for engineering teams.

OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Targets Complex Reasoning and Demanding Coding Tasks

OpenAI released GPT-5.5 as its new flagship for users who need the most capable model available, positioning it explicitly against Claude Opus 4.8 for engineering and multi-step reasoning use cases. Meanwhile, benchmark leaks attributed to internal OpenAI testers are circulating online for GPT-5.6, with expected improvements in token efficiency and accuracy on multi-step agentic workflows — consistent with a late June or early July release window. The rapid iteration pace suggests OpenAI is under pressure to recapture ground it has ceded to Anthropic in developer mindshare.

Business

Qwen 3.7 Max Matches Frontier Performance at Half the Cost

Alibaba's Qwen 3.7 Max is drawing significant attention from developers this week after independent testing showed it matching or exceeding Claude Opus 4.7 on agentic benchmarks — at roughly half the input cost and a quarter of the output cost. The model is quickly becoming the go-to choice for cost-conscious teams running high-volume workloads, and its rise is the latest indicator that the performance gap between Chinese and Western frontier models has effectively closed. For enterprise buyers, Qwen 3.7 Max introduces real price competition into a market that has been dominated by a small number of US labs.

Hardware

MiniMax M3 Cuts Multimodal Compute to 1/20th of Previous Models

Chinese AI lab MiniMax unveiled M3, a multimodal model that slashes per-token compute requirements to just one-twentieth of what prior models required — while delivering 9× faster prefilling and 15× faster decoding for 1-million-token contexts. The efficiency gains are achieved through a novel mixture-of-transformers architecture that activates only the most relevant model components for each input. If the numbers hold up under independent scrutiny, M3 represents the most significant cost-efficiency jump in a multimodal model since the original Gemini Flash launch.

Policy

White House Issues New Executive Order on AI Innovation and Security

The White House released a new executive order titled "Promoting Advanced Artificial Intelligence Innovation and Security," setting out a framework that pairs deregulatory measures for domestic AI development with new national security guardrails on frontier model exports and critical infrastructure. The order directs federal agencies to accelerate AI procurement and establishes interagency coordination mechanisms for evaluating risks from foreign AI systems. Separately, DARPA announced its AI Forge program — a dedicated initiative to accelerate AI breakthroughs specifically for national security applications, with an emphasis on autonomous systems and battlefield decision support.

5 Patterns Worth Watching

  1. Coding AI is the central battlefield. Claude Code drove Anthropic to a near-trillion-dollar valuation. Microsoft launched its own coding model at Build. Google and OpenAI are both racing to match. The developer tool market has become the proxy war for AI supremacy.
  2. AI lab IPOs are now underway. Anthropic's confidential filing sets the clock ticking. OpenAI, with its $25B+ annualized revenue, faces new pressure to follow. The next 12 months may see the largest wave of AI company public listings in history.
  3. Model costs are collapsing — fast. Qwen 3.7 Max at half the price. MiniMax M3 at 1/20th the compute. The economics of running frontier AI are changing faster than enterprise procurement cycles can track.
  4. Speed is the new benchmark metric. Gemini 3.5 Flash leads with "4× faster." MiniMax M3 boasts 15× faster decoding. As models converge on capability, latency and throughput are becoming the primary differentiators for production workloads.
  5. AI policy is hardening on both sides. The White House executive order pairs domestic deregulation with tighter export controls — a split that will shape where frontier models can legally be deployed. Expect allies and adversaries alike to respond with their own frameworks before year-end.
